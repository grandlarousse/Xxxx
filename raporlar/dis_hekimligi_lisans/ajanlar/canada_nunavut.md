# Nunavut (Canada) — ajan bulgusu

Düzenleyici: Government of Nunavut, Department of Health - Registrar of Health Professions / Nunavut Dental Registration Committee (statutory committee under the Dental Profession Act, R.S.N.W.T. 1988, c.33 (Supp.), as duplicated for Nunavut). There is no self-regulating dental college in Nunavut; the NWT/NU Dental Association is a voluntary association, not the regulator. — https://www.gov.nu.ca/en/health (Department of Health; Act consolidation at https://nunavutlegislation.ca/en/file-download/download/public/811)

## Sınav yolları

- **Part One registration (general dentist) on the strength of an NDEB certificate** (el becerisi: hayır): Dental Profession Act (Nunavut) s.5: a person may apply to the Registration Committee to be registered in Part One of the Dental Register (general practice). Search-engine extracts of the Act and the regulations indicate the Committee registers applicants who 'meet the requirements set out in this Act' on provision of 'the appropriate fee and evidence showing that the applicant meets the requirements'. The examination standard across Canada, including the territories, is the NDEB certificate (since March 2023 = the single computer-based Virtual OSCE for graduates of CDAC/CODA-accredited programmes; no hands-on component). Nunavut has no territorial clinical/manikin exam and no published jurisprudence exam. CAVEAT: the official Act text (nunavutlegislation.ca, CanLII, opennwt) was Cloudflare-blocked in this session, so the exact s.5 wording (approved school + NDEB certificate; any citizenship clause) could not be read verbatim.
- **Part Two registration (specialist)** (el becerisi: hayır): Act s.6: registration of dental specialists in Part Two of the Register - requires recognised specialty training; not relevant to a new general-dentistry graduate.
- **Part Three registration (sponsored dentist, non-Canadian school graduate)** (el becerisi: hayır): Dental Profession Regulations (Nunavut): a graduate of a non-Canadian school of dentistry may apply in writing to the Minister for Part Three registration if he/she holds a dentistry degree or has 'practised actively for 12 consecutive months in the 18 months before applying' and is 'sponsored by a resident registered dentist' (a 'sponsoring licensee' = a licensed dentist who sponsors the applicant). This is a temporary/sponsored route tied to a Nunavut employer (typically GN dental contractors); it presupposes actually working in Nunavut and therefore a work permit - not suitable for a licence-holding-only candidate. Whether a CODA-accredited foreign school (Yeditepe) is treated as 'Canadian-equivalent' for Part One, or as 'non-Canadian' for Part Three, is a decision of the Registration Committee and is unverified.
- **PGY-1 residency in lieu of exam** (el becerisi: evet): Not a pathway in Nunavut; there is no provision substituting a residency for the NDEB certificate (no residency programmes exist in the territory).
- **Regional hands-on exam (ADEX/CRDTS/WREB etc.) or DLOSCE** (el becerisi: evet): Not applicable. Nunavut (like all Canadian jurisdictions) relies on the NDEB certificate; US regional exams and the DLOSCE play no role.

## Teori-only açıklaması
For a graduate of a CDAC/CODA-accredited programme the only examination needed anywhere in Canada is the NDEB certificate, which since March 2023 is a single computer-based Virtual OSCE at Prometric (CDAC confirms CODA-accredited programmes outside the US count as accredited for the NDEB Certification Process). Nunavut's Dental Profession Act (R.S.N.W.T. 1988, c.33 (Supp.), in force 22 Dec 1990, consolidated for Nunavut) provides that a person may apply to the Registration Committee for registration in Part One of the Dental Register and that 'where a person applying for registration meets the requirements set out in this Act for the registration, the Registration Committee shall register them'; the Regulations require only 'the appropriate fee and evidence showing that the applicant meets the requirements of the Act'. No territorial clinical, manikin or jurisprudence examination exists. CAVEAT: these quotations come from search-engine extracts of the official Act/Regulations because nunavutlegislation.ca, CanLII and opennwt returned HTTP 403 (bot block); the verbatim s.5 eligibility clause (approved school + NDEB certificate, and whether it contains any citizenship/residency condition) could not be read directly, hence overall confidence is low.

## DLOSCE notları
N/A for Nunavut. The DLOSCE/ADEX framework is American; Nunavut requires the NDEB certificate (Virtual OSCE) and has no provision accepting the DLOSCE, ADEX or any US regional exam. The ADEX/DLOSCE integration after Aug/Oct 2026 is irrelevant here.

## Yüz yüze adımlar
No in-person step is identified in the statute: application is a paper/e-mail submission to the Registrar of Health Professions (Department of Health, Iqaluit) decided by the Dental Registration Committee, which meets periodically; the Act contains no jurisprudence exam, interview or fingerprint requirement. A criminal record check (usually obtainable from abroad via RCMP-accredited fingerprinting companies) and notarised/certified copies of degree and NDEB certificate are customary for GN licensing offices but could not be confirmed from the form. The NDEB Virtual OSCE itself is sat at a Prometric centre (available in Canada and internationally). Part Three applicants additionally need a Nunavut-resident sponsoring dentist, which effectively requires being on site. Everything else appears doable from abroad or during a short visit, subject to confirmation of the document list.

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
NDEB certificate (Virtual OSCE, computer-based). Good character / professional standing evidence and application fee (Regulations: 'the appropriate fee and evidence showing that the applicant meets the requirements of the Act'). No territorial jurisprudence exam, no language test (Nunavut's official languages are Inuktut, English and French; English suffices in practice), no CPR requirement found in the statute. Liability insurance: not mentioned in the Act extracts (NWT/NU dentists are typically insured through CDSPI; unverified for licensure). Timelines: licences are issued by the Registrar after Committee approval; the Committee is a part-time body so processing can take weeks to a few months (no official SLA found). Licence is annual: 'the Registrar shall issue a licence each year to each person registered in the Dental Register'.

## İlk ücretler
Nunavut fee schedule not retrievable (Dental Profession Regulations, Schedule A - site blocked). Comparator from the sister NWT regime, which uses the same 1988 Act lineage: registration fee $128 plus annual licence fee $255 (NWT Professional Licensing Office, 2026). Expect Nunavut fees of a similar low order (likely a few hundred CAD). NDEB Virtual OSCE fees are separate and paid to NDEB.

- **insurance_required:** Not stated in the Act extracts; unverified. (Professional liability insurance is standard in Canadian practice but no Nunavut licensure condition was found.)
- **inactive_status:** No inactive/non-practising/retired category exists in the Dental Profession Act (a 1990 statute with only a Part One/Two/Three register and an annual licence). Keeping the registration means paying the annual licence fee every year; there is no cheaper non-practising register. Unverified whether the Registrar permits lapsing and later re-licensing without re-application.
- **reactivation:** Not specified in the extracts. Because the Act bases registration on the NDEB certificate (which does not expire) and sets no CE/currency hours, re-licensing after a lapse should in principle be a matter of re-applying and paying fees, but the Registration Committee has discretion and could ask for evidence of recent practice. Unverified.
- **remote_maintainability:** unclear
- **details:** On the face of the statute Nunavut is one of the lightest-touch regimes in Canada: annual licence fee, no CE, no practice hours, no jurisprudence exam, no inactive class. The uncertainty is entirely about (a) what the GN application and renewal forms actually demand (proof of eligibility to work in Canada, insurance, criminal record check) and (b) any unpublished Registration Committee policies. If the immigration-document question is resolved favourably, remote maintenance would be 'easy'; if the Registrar insists on a work permit at each renewal it becomes 'hard'.
