# Puerto Rico (USA) — ajan bulgusu

Düzenleyici: Junta Dental Examinadora de Puerto Rico (Puerto Rico Board of Dental Examiners), Departamento de Salud de Puerto Rico / Oficina de Reglamentación y Certificación de Profesionales de la Salud (ORCPS) — https://www.salud.pr.gov/

**Teori-only rota:** no · **Göçmenlik engeli:** high · **Uzaktan sürdürme:** unclear · **Güven:** medium

## Sınav yolları

- **Board 'reválida' (Puerto Rico's own licensure examination): theory + live-patient clinical + ethics/jurisprudence** (el becerisi: evet): Statutory route under 20 L.P.R.A. § 89(5): 'Aprobar los exámenes teóricos y prácticos de las ciencias básicas y las disciplinas clínicas de la cirugía dental que determine la Junta'. Per the Board's Manual de Información: (a) Theory part = JCNDE 'National Boards' theoretical exams (Parts I & II); the Board 'podrá eximir' applicants holding National Board (now INBDE) certificates passed within 5 years before the application, with the JCNDE score report sent directly to the Board; (b) Clinical part (must have passed all theory first) is a live-patient, hands-on exam at the UPR School of Dental Medicine in San Juan: complete denture impression, Class II amalgam, composite resin restoration, oral-surgery extraction, fixed prosthodontics; candidate must bring own patients, instruments, materials, and hold a professional liability policy in force on exam dates; (c) a separate multiple-choice Ethics and Jurisprudence exam (Ley 75 de 1925 and Board regulations). Exams held twice a year in the capital (§ 82), in Spanish or English on request. Four attempts; after 4 failures, remedial courses at UPR required.
- **ADEX / CDCA-WREB-CITA clinical exam in lieu of the Board's clinical part** (el becerisi: evet): ADA Licensure-by-State page (updated 16 Oct 2026) lists Puerto Rico's initial-licensure clinical exam as 'American Board of Dental Examiners (ADEX, CDCA-WREB-CITA)'. The Board manual states: 'Exención del examen clínico: La Junta podrá eximir de los exámenes clínicos correspondientes a los aspirantes que posean certificado acreditativo de haber aprobado los exámenes del Council of Interstate Testing Agencies (CITA)... No se admitirá otra evidencia que no sea el reporte de resultados recibido directamente de la CITA.' This exempts only the clinical part; theory (or INBDE exemption), the ethics/jurisprudence exam, 6-month residence and all other § 89 requirements still apply. ADEX includes manikin hand-skill sections, so this is hands-on.
- **DLOSCE-only / computer-based OSCE only** (el becerisi: hayır): NOT available. Neither the statute, the ADA summary nor the Board manual mentions the DLOSCE or any OSCE; Puerto Rico is not on the JCNDE DLOSCE acceptance list; § 89(5) expressly requires 'exámenes ... prácticos'. Listed only to record that it does not exist.
- **PGY-1 residency in lieu of clinical exam** (el becerisi: hayır): NOT available. No residency-in-lieu-of-exam provision in Ley 75 de 1925 (20 L.P.R.A. §§ 81-94u), the ADA summary or the Board manual.
- **Licensure by credentials / reciprocity (§ 84 and § 14H)** (el becerisi: evet): Statute merely empowers the Board to 'establecer convenios de reciprocidad con las juntas examinadoras de los estados y territorios de los Estados Unidos y países extranjeros' where the other state's requirements are similar or superior. No published reciprocity agreements were found and it presupposes an existing licence, so it is not a route for a new graduate; the underlying licence would itself have required a hands-on exam.
- **Provisional licence for charitable / non-profit institutions (§ 89a) and 60-day special authorisation** (el becerisi: hayır): Restricted permits only: provisional licence to practise gratuitously in charitable non-profit entities recommended by the Secretary of Health (applicant must meet reválida admission requirements AND show prior lawful practice in a US state or another country plus a contract with the charity); 60-day special authorisation for dentists licensed elsewhere coming to PR for a specific activity. Neither is a regular licence and both require an existing licence elsewhere; not usable by a new graduate.

## Teori-only açıklaması
Hands-on is written into the statute. 20 L.P.R.A. § 89(5) (Ley Núm. 75 de 8 de agosto de 1925, OGP consolidated text rev. 15 Apr 2024): 'Aprobar los exámenes teóricos y prácticos de las ciencias básicas y las disciplinas clínicas de la cirugía dental que determine la Junta para comprobar la capacidad del aspirante.' ADA English rendering: '(5) To pass practical and theoretical examinations on basic sciences and clinical disciplines of dental surgery as determined by the Board'. The National Board exemption covers only the theoretical part: 'eximir de los exámenes teóricos ... Ello no eximirá de cumplir con los demás requisitos dispuestos en esta sección.' The Board manual's only clinical exemption is for CITA (now ADEX/CDCA-WREB-CITA) results, which are themselves manikin hand-skill exams; otherwise the Board's own clinical exam uses live patients ('El candidato deberá proveer un paciente'). No DLOSCE, OSCE, residency or portfolio alternative exists. In addition, § 89(3) requires six months' uninterrupted residence in Puerto Rico, which no computer exam can replace.

## DLOSCE notları
The DLOSCE is not mentioned in the statute, the Board's Manual de Información, or the ADA Puerto Rico summary (updated 16 Oct 2026), and Puerto Rico is not on the JCNDE DLOSCE acceptance list. The Board's clinical-exam exemption is tied to a results report 'recibido directamente de la CITA' (now ADEX/CDCA-WREB-CITA); the ADA page lists ADEX as the accepted clinical exam. An ADEX-integrated DLOSCE pass would therefore only matter as one component of a full ADEX pass; there is no rule accepting a DLOSCE component alone, and earlier standalone DLOSCE scores have no recognised use in Puerto Rico. Standalone DLOSCE ended Oct 2026 anyway.

PGY-1 kabul: False

- **barrier_level:** high
- **details:** Citizenship: for DENTISTS the statute does not require US citizenship or permanent residence (the 'ser ciudadano de los Estados Unidos o residente permanente de Puerto Rico' clause in Ley 75 applies only to dental hygienists, § 94b, and dental assistants, § 94k). BUT: (1) 20 L.P.R.A. § 89(3) requires the applicant 'Haber residido, por lo menos, ininterrumpidamente durante un período de seis (6) meses en Puerto Rico, excluyendo salidas esporádicas del país con fines médicos, de negocios no relacionados con la profesión dental o de placer' - a mandatory six-month uninterrupted residence in Puerto Rico before licensure; a B-visa visitor is normally admitted for at most six months and 'residence' on a visitor visa is doubtful. (2) The Board manual's identity-document rule verifies immigration status: 'Extranjeros: Certificación de la Oficina de Imigración y Naturalización Federal, indicativa de su estatus ante dicha Oficina... la certificación deberá enviarse por la Oficina de Imigración y Naturalización directamente a la Junta Dental Examinadora' (naturalised citizens: certification of naturalisation certificate number/date). (3) The ADA's English text of § 89 still lists requirement '(6) To practice as an odontologist for a minimum period of one (1) year, wherever the Secretary of Health determines... in the public sector, through a special authorization issued... by the Board' after passing the exams - which would require work authorisation; the OGP Spanish consolidated text (rev. Apr 2024) that I read showed items (1)-(5) only, so whether (6) is still in force is unverified. (4) Practising requires compulsory membership of the Colegio de Cirujanos Dentistas de Puerto Rico (Ley 162 de 1941) and a professional liability policy in force for the clinical exam. (5) Foreign applicants must also supply a criminal-record certificate from every state/country of residence in the last 5 years plus a Puerto Rico Police certificate and an ASUME (child-support) certification. No SAVE-type statutory lawful-presence law for professional licences was identified, but the Board's own USCIS-status certification requirement functions as immigration-status verification.

## Yüz yüze adımlar
Essentially everything is in Puerto Rico and in person: (1) six months' uninterrupted residence in Puerto Rico before licensure (§ 89(3)) - cannot be done from abroad and does not fit a short visit; (2) the Board's examinations are held twice a year in San Juan (§ 82): the theory part (unless exempt via National Board/INBDE pass within 5 years, score report sent directly by JCNDE), the live-patient clinical-practical exam at the UPR Medical Sciences Campus School of Dental Medicine (candidate supplies own patients, instruments, materials, pre-op radiographs; liability policy in force) unless exempted by an ADEX/CITA report sent directly from the testing agency (ADEX itself is a hands-on exam taken in person at a US test site), and the multiple-choice Ethics and Jurisprudence exam (paper, administered by the Board; no online option mentioned); (3) application filed at least one month before the exam date with photo, a Certificación Jurada de Buena Conducta (sworn good-conduct certification) signed by a dentist holding a permanent Puerto Rico licence who knows the applicant, a Certificado de Antecedentes Penales from the Puerto Rico Police (plus criminal-record certificates from every state/country lived in during the last 5 years), USCIS certification of immigration status sent directly to the Board, official pre-dental and dental transcripts sent directly by the institutions, Dean's certification; (4) ASUME child-support certificate at licensure and at every recertification; (5) registration of the licence in the ORCPS Registry of Professionals ($30) and compulsory enrolment in the Colegio de Cirujanos Dentistas before practising. Fingerprinting is not mentioned in the documents read (unclear). Notarisation: the sworn certification implies a sworn/notarised statement; form not seen.

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
Ethics & Jurisprudence multiple-choice exam (Ley 75 de 1925 and Board regulations) - in person, no online version documented. Bachelor's/pre-dental diploma from a recognised university plus dental diploma; Board must find a non-UPR programme analogous to UPR's and the last 2 years completed at that school (a CODA-accredited programme meets the 'accrediting agencies of US dental schools' standard). INBDE/National Board pass within 5 years exempts the theory part only. Criminal background: PR Police certificate + certificates from each state/country of residence in last 5 years + sworn good-conduct certification by a PR-licensed dentist. Immigration-status certification from USCIS for foreigners. Professional liability policy in force on clinical-exam dates. ASUME (child-support) certification. Compulsory Colegio de Cirujanos Dentistas membership to practise (Ley 162-1941). Language: exams offered in Spanish, or English on request; all official documents and forms are in Spanish. No CPR requirement at initial licensure found (CPR course is required at recertification). Timeline: exams twice a year; application at least 1 month before the exam date; theory must be fully passed before the clinical part; 6-month prior residence.

## İlk ücretler
Per the Board's Manual de Información (undated, figures appear old): application $50 (non-refundable), theory exam $100 (Parts I and II) or $50 (Part II only), re-examination form $25, plus clinical facility-use and materials fees payable to the UPR School of Dental Medicine (amount set by the school), registration in the Registry of Professionals $30. Statute fee schedule lists recertification/renewal $75. ADEX Dental Examination (if used for the clinical exemption) about $2,995 (ADEX 2026). Colegio de Cirujanos Dentistas dues and liability-insurance premium not quantified. Current ORCPS fee schedule was not obtained - amounts may have changed.

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Every three (3) years (registration and recertification under Ley 11 de 23 de junio de 1976 and the Board's Reglamento de Educación Continua y Registro para la Recertificación).
- **renewal_fee:** $75 recertification/renewal (statute fee list '(e) Recertificación o renovación de licencia, setenta y cinco dólares ($75)' and Board manual); the manual also cites a one-time $30 registry fee. Colegio dues are separate and not quantified.
- **ce_hours:** 45 hours per 3-year cycle, including a minimum of 3 hours in infection control and a CPR (Resucitación Cardiopulmonar) course.
- **ce_online_ok:** Unclear. The Reglamento de Educación Continua was not obtained; the manual does not state whether online/self-study hours are capped. The mandatory CPR course is normally hands-on.
- **cpr_required:** Yes - 'un curso de Resucitación Cardiopulmonar' is part of the 45-hour recertification requirement; whether a hands-on skills session is mandatory is not specified in the manual (likely yes, as with standard BLS courses).
- **practice_hours_required:** None found in the statute or manual.
- **address_or_residency_required:** No residency requirement at renewal was found; the licensee must notify the Board of any change of 'dirección postal, domiciliaria o de oficina'. The 6-month Puerto Rico residence applies to initial licensure only. An ASUME (child-support) certificate is required at each recertification.
- **insurance_required:** A professional liability policy is required to sit the clinical exam; no statutory requirement to carry insurance to keep the licence was found. Practising in PR requires Colegio membership (Ley 162-1941).
- **inactive_status:** None found. Neither Ley 75 de 1925 (OGP text rev. Apr 2024) nor the Board manual describes an inactive, retired or non-practising licence category; the only non-regular categories are provisional (charitable), provisional for foreign faculty, provisional hygienist (for failed candidates) and 60-day special authorisation.
- **reactivation:** Unclear - no lapse/reinstatement rule was located in the documents read; ORCPS general rules under Ley 11-1976 would govern (not read).
- **remote_maintainability:** unclear
- **details:** Once licensed, upkeep appears to be: 45 CE hours (3 h infection control + CPR course) every 3 years, $75 recertification fee, ASUME certificate, and address-change notifications. No practice-hour or in-territory residency requirement for renewal was found, and Colegio membership is tied to practising rather than to holding the licence. However, I could not verify whether all CE may be online, whether the CPR course needs an in-person skills check, whether an inactive status exists, or how a lapsed licence is reinstated, so remote maintainability is rated 'unclear' (probably moderate at best). The much bigger obstacle is initial licensure (6-month PR residence, live-patient or ADEX hands-on exam, immigration-status certification).
