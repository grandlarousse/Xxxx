**Teori-only rota:** no · **Göçmenlik engeli:** low · **Uzaktan sürdürme:** moderate · **Güven:** medium

## Sınav yolları

- **Licensure by examination (board-approved regional clinical exam)** (el becerisi: evet): Minn. R. 3100.1100 subp. 1: applicant must provide a completed application, government-issued ID, the fee (Minn. Stat. 150A.091 subd. 2, $308), evidence of CODA graduation, 'evidence of passing all parts of a national board examination for the practice of dentistry within the past five years' (INBDE), 'evidence of passing a board-approved clinical examination within the past five years', the board's jurisprudence exam within 5 years, current CPR certification and a criminal background check under Minn. Stat. 214.075. Board-approved clinical exams per the ADA Licensure-by-State page (updated 9 Oct 2026, sourced from the Board website): ADEX (CDCA-WREB-CITA), CRDTS and SRTA; 'Minnesota requires applicants to pass ALL components of the exams, including posterior and anterior operative, except for periodontal component, within 5 years prior to application'. All are manikin/patient hands-on exams. After two failures further CODA education is required before a retake (3100.1100 subp. 1a).
- **PGY-1 residency waiver of the clinical exam (AEGD/GPR)** (el becerisi: hayır): Minn. Stat. 150A.06 subd. 3(b) (the ADA page calls it 'subdivision 3b'): 'The board shall waive the clinical examination required for licensure for any dentist applicant who is a graduate of a dental education program accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation, who has passed all components of the National Board Dental Examinations, and who has satisfactorily completed a postdoctoral general dentistry residency program (GPR) or an advanced education in general dentistry (AEGD) program after January 1, 2004. The postdoctoral program must be accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation, be of at least one year's duration, and include an outcome assessment evaluation'. Applicant submits a written request for the clinical waiver. No hands-on exam, but requires a one-year US residency (and therefore a visa), which the candidate excludes.
- **Discretionary waiver of all or part of the examination (statutory, not a published route)** (el becerisi: hayır): Minn. Stat. 150A.06 subd. 3(a): 'All or any part of the examination for dentists ... except that pertaining to the law of Minnesota relating to dentistry and the rules of the board, may, at the discretion of the board, be waived for an applicant who presents a certificate of having passed all components of the National Board Dental Examinations or evidence of having maintained an adequate scholastic standing as determined by the board.' On its face this allows the Board to waive the clinical exam for an INBDE passer, but Board rule 3100.1100 still lists a board-approved clinical exam as a requirement and the Board publishes no such waiver practice (the ADA summary lists only the regional exams and the PGY-1 waiver). Treat as theoretical/discretionary only.
- **Licensure by credentials (dentists already licensed in a US state or Canadian province)** (el becerisi: hayır): Minn. Stat. 150A.06 subd. 4 and Minn. R. 3100.1400: applicant must have passed all components of the National Board exams; 'has been in active practice at least 2,000 hours within 36 months of the application date, or passed a board-approved reentry program within 36 months'; 'currently has a license in another state or Canadian province' in good standing; is 'interviewed by the board'; at board discretion passes an English proficiency test; rule 3100.1400 additionally requires 'evidence of passing a clinical examination for licensure in another United States jurisdiction or Canadian province', jurisprudence exam, CPR, background check and the $893 fee (150A.091 subd. 9). This is the only place the rules connect to Canada/NDEB: a dentist registered in a Canadian province on the basis of the NDEB Virtual OSCE and with 2,000 practice hours could apply, and the Canadian 'clinical examination for licensure' would presumably be the NDEB certification, but whether the Board treats the Virtual OSCE as a 'clinical examination' is not stated anywhere in statute, rule or the ADA summary. Not available to a new graduate without practice hours.
- **DLOSCE / NDEB OSCE as the clinical examination for initial licensure** (el becerisi: hayır): NOT accepted for initial licensure: Minnesota is not on the JCNDE DLOSCE acceptance list, the ADA page lists only ADEX/CRDTS/SRTA, and neither Minn. Stat. 150A.06 nor Minn. R. 3100.1100 mentions the DLOSCE, the NDEB or any OSCE for initial licensure. The only OSCE reference in the rules is Minn. R. 3100.1850 subp. 3 (reinstatement after a lapse of 24+ months), which requires 'a nationally recognized objective structured clinical examination for general dentists' instead of a hands-on exam.

## Teori-only açıklaması
For initial licensure a new CODA graduate must satisfy Minn. R. 3100.1100 subp. 1 item F: 'evidence of passing a board-approved clinical examination within the past five years'. The Board-approved clinical examinations (ADA Licensure-by-State summary, updated 9 Oct 2026, sourced from the Board) are ADEX (CDCA-WREB-CITA), CRDTS and SRTA, with the requirement to 'pass ALL components of the exams, including posterior and anterior operative' - i.e. manikin hand-skill components. The only non-hands-on alternative written into law is the residency waiver in Minn. Stat. 150A.06 subd. 3(b) ('The board shall waive the clinical examination required for licensure for any dentist applicant who ... has satisfactorily completed a postdoctoral general dentistry residency program (GPR) or an advanced education in general dentistry (AEGD) program'), which the candidate rules out. Subd. 3(a) gives the Board a discretionary power to waive 'all or any part of the examination' for an applicant who has passed all components of the National Board exams, but the Board's own rule still demands a board-approved clinical exam and no published policy applies 3(a) to the clinical exam, so it cannot be relied on. No DLOSCE-only, NDEB/OSCE, portfolio or state-specific written clinical route exists for initial licensure; the OSCE language in Minn. R. 3100.1850 applies only to reinstating a lapsed licence. The 2023 review article's statement that Minnesota accepts the NDEB OSCE could not be matched to any current statute, rule or Board summary for initial licensure; the nearest provisions are licensure by credentials for Canadian-licensed dentists (requires 2,000 practice hours in 36 months plus 'a clinical examination for licensure in ... [a] Canadian province') and the 'nationally recognized OSCE' required for reinstatement after a 24-month lapse.

## DLOSCE notları
Minnesota does not accept the DLOSCE (standalone or ADEX-integrated) in fulfilment of the clinical examination for initial licensure: it is absent from the JCNDE acceptance list, the ADA summary lists only ADEX/CRDTS/SRTA with 'ALL components' required, and ch. 3100 never names the DLOSCE. ADEX reports component pass/fail, so an ADEX-reported DLOSCE pass without the hand-skill components would not meet the 'all components' requirement. The only OSCE acceptance in the rules is Minn. R. 3100.1850 subp. 3 (licence terminated for 24 months or more): the applicant must provide 'evidence of passing the following examinations within 24 months prior to the board's receipt of the application: a nationally recognized objective structured clinical examination for general dentists' (plus CE, CPR, jurisprudence within 12 months, background check) - historically satisfied by the DLOSCE. With standalone DLOSCE administrations ended after 9 Oct 2026 it is unclear which exam the Board will accept for that reinstatement requirement (possibly the ADEX-integrated DLOSCE component or the NDEB Virtual OSCE); the Board has published no guidance that could be read (Board website blocked by CAPTCHA for this session). No Canadian/NDEB provision exists for initial licensure by examination.

## Yüz yüze adımlar
(1) Hands-on clinical exam (ADEX/CRDTS/SRTA) must be taken in person at a US testing site; a visitor can sit it during a trip. (2) Fingerprint-based criminal background check: Minn. Stat. 214.075 subd. 3 - 'the applicant or licensee must submit a completed criminal history records check consent form and a full set of fingerprints to the respective health-related licensing board or a designee in the manner and form specified by the board'; the statute does not mandate Live Scan, and the Board's practice (fingerprint card from the Board, rolled by any law-enforcement agency, mailed back) could not be verified because the Board site was unreadable - probably doable abroad via card but unconfirmed. (3) Jurisprudence examination: required (Minn. R. 3100.1100 item G); format (the Board is believed to offer it online/open book) not verified. (4) No interview for licensure by examination; an interview 'by the board' is part of licensure by credentials (150A.06 subd. 4(a)). (5) No notarisation requirement found in statute or rule. (6) CPR certification requires a course with 'hands-on practice' (Minn. R. 3100.0100 definition of CPR) - can be done anywhere, but must be a hands-on BLS course.

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Biennial: initial licence valid until the last day of the licensee's birth month in the following even- or odd-numbered year matching the birth year; thereafter 24-month terms (Minn. R. 3100.1700 subp. 1a-1b).
- **renewal_fee:** $475 per biennium for a dentist (Minn. Stat. 150A.091 subd. 5); late fee under subd. 7; renewal application must certify 'maintaining a consecutive and current CPR certification'.
- **ce_hours:** 50 hours of professional development per biennial cycle for dentists, at least 30 'fundamental' and no more than 20 'elective' (Minn. R. 3100.5100); a mandatory infection-control course each cycle; licensee keeps a portfolio subject to random audit (3100.5300). Additional 15 hours if holding an anesthesia/sedation certificate.
- **ce_online_ok:** Yes in substance: fundamental activities include 'clinical subjects ... covered through seminars, webinars, symposiums, lectures, college courses'; elective activities include 'professional reading of published articles or other forms of self-study'. No rule requires any live/in-person CE hours. The hands-on element comes only via the CPR definition.
- **cpr_required:** Yes. Renewal requires certifying continuous current CPR certification (3100.1700 subp. 2), and 'CPR' is defined in Minn. R. 3100.0100 as a health-care-provider BLS course that 'must include both hands-on practice and written examination' - so a fully online CPR card is not sufficient; an in-person skills session (anywhere) is required.
- **practice_hours_required:** None for renewal of an active licence. (2,000 hours in 36 months applies only to licensure by credentials.)
- **address_or_residency_required:** No Minnesota address or residency requirement found in 150A.06 or ch. 3100; licensee must keep the Board informed of current address (standard), but may live abroad.
- **insurance_required:** No mandatory professional liability insurance requirement found in Minn. Stat. ch. 150A or Minn. R. ch. 3100.
- **inactive_status:** No ordinary 'inactive' status. Options under 3100.1700 subp. 3 if one elects not to renew: voluntary termination, or 'emeritus inactive' / 'emeritus active'. Emeritus inactive (3100.1340): one-time fee (150A.091 subd. 19), 'is not a license to practice dentistry', 'is not renewable', and 'once an emeritus inactive license is issued by the board, the licensee cannot seek reinstatement of the licensee's prior license' - it is a permanent retirement status, useless for later reactivation. Emeritus active (3100.1350; 150A.06 volunteer/retired waiver): for retired/volunteer practice, 5 hours CE (infection control, medical emergencies, medical management) plus CPR - not a parking status for a full licence.
- **reactivation:** Licence not renewed within 30 days after expiry is administratively terminated (3100.1700 subp. 3). Reinstatement (3100.1850; fee $140 + $475 renewal): terminated 6 months or less - 50 hours CE within prior 24 months + CPR; more than 6 but less than 24 months - CE + CPR + jurisprudence exam within 12 months + background check if terminated more than a year; 24 months or more - CE + CPR + jurisprudence + background check + 'evidence of passing ... within 24 months prior to the board's receipt of the application: a nationally recognized objective structured clinical examination for general dentists' (an OSCE, not a hands-on exam; historically the DLOSCE - which exam will qualify after standalone DLOSCE ended in Oct 2026 is unclear). No refresher course or practice hours required.
- **remote_maintainability:** moderate
- **details:** Keeping the licence active from abroad is feasible: $475 every two years, 50 CE hours obtainable through webinars/self-study (with the mandatory infection-control course, available online), no practice-hour, address or insurance conditions. The awkward element is CPR, which by rule must include hands-on practice (an in-person BLS skills session abroad, e.g. AHA international training centre, is possible). There is no reversible inactive status: emeritus inactive is permanent and non-reinstatable. If the licence lapses for 24+ months, reinstatement requires a 'nationally recognized OSCE' rather than a hands-on exam, which is comparatively favourable.
