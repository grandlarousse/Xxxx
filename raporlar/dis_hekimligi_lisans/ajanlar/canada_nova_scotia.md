# Nova Scotia (Canada) — ajan bulgusu

Düzenleyici: Nova Scotia Regulator of Dentistry and Dental Assisting (NSRDDA) – formerly the Provincial Dental Board of Nova Scotia (PDBNS); migrated under the Regulated Health Professions Act (RHPA) in May 2025 — https://nsrdda.ca/ (pdbns.ca redirects here)

**Teori-only rota:** yes · **Göçmenlik engeli:** high · **Uzaktan sürdürme:** hard · **Güven:** medium

## Sınav yolları

- **NDEB certificate route (standard and only route for graduates of CDAC/CODA-accredited programmes)** (el becerisi: hayır): 'First Application for a Dental License in Canada – General Dentist': upload (1) passport photo, (2) copy of NDEB general dentistry certificate (for accredited-programme graduates the NDEB certificate is obtained via the computer-based Virtual OSCE only, no hands-on component), (3) certified copy of graduation certificate from a programme accredited by CDAC or 'American Dental Association Commission on Dental Accreditation (CODA)' (also ADC/DCNZ/Irish Dental Council under MRAs), (4) Dean's letter, (5) proof of Canadian citizenship/PR OR immigration authorisation to practise dentistry in Canada, (6) CDSPI malpractice insurance $3M, (7) currency of practice 450 h/3 yrs (only if not a new graduate / NDEB cert >36 months old), (8) Vulnerable Sector Search + Criminal Record Check within 12 months, (9) two character references, (10) BLS within 12 months ('must be an in-person hands-on course'), (11) translations. No regional/ADEX exam, no provincial clinical exam, no jurisprudence exam listed; licensing meeting with Registrar then licence issued within 5 business days of a complete application. Application is online (Alinity portal).
- **Already licensed in another Canadian province (labour mobility / Patient Access to Care Act)** (el becerisi: hayır): For dentists currently licensed in another Canadian province/territory: processed within 5 business days of a complete application; initial application fee and initial registration fee waived under the Patient Access to Care Act. Not relevant to a new graduate with no Canadian licence.
- **Internationally educated dentist (non-accredited programme) – NDEB Equivalency Process** (el becerisi: evet): Not applicable to this candidate: Yeditepe (CODA-accredited since 1 Feb 2024) is treated as an accredited programme; NSRDDA page explicitly lists CODA-accredited programmes. The NDEB Equivalency Process (AFK + ACJ/ACS, the ACS being hands-on) is only for non-accredited graduates.
- **PGY-1 / residency in lieu of exam** (el becerisi: hayır): No such pathway exists: the NDEB certificate is mandatory in every case; residency is not a substitute for anything in Nova Scotia.
- **Temporary authorization for dentists** (el becerisi: hayır): NSRDDA has a 'Temporary Authorization for Dentists' page (not read in this research); likely for short-term/locum/educational purposes; immigration authorisation would still be required under the regulation.

## Teori-only açıklaması
Exam-wise, Nova Scotia requires only the NDEB certificate: the application page states applicants must upload 'A copy of your National Dental Examining Board of Canada (NDEB) general dentistry certificate' and 'A certified copy of your graduation certificate from a dental program accredited by the: Commission on Dental Accreditation of Canada (CDAC), American Dental Association Commission on Dental Accreditation (CODA) ...' (https://nsrdda.ca/applicants/dentists/first-application-for-a-dental-license-in-canada-dentist/; Policies on Requirements for Registration and Licensing of Dentists 2025, s.3.2–3.3). For accredited graduates the NDEB certificate is earned through the computer-based Virtual OSCE alone. No regional clinical exam, no provincial practical exam, and no jurisprudence examination is listed; the only other 'exam' is BLS (hands-on CPR course, not a dental clinical exam). HOWEVER the route is blocked for this candidate by the immigration requirement: Dentistry and Dental Assisting Regulations under the RHPA, s.12(1)(a)(vi) – applicants must prove 'they are a Canadian citizen or legally entitled to live and work in Canada' – and the application checklist item 5: 'If an applicant is not a Canadian citizen or permanent resident of Canada, the applicant must provide details about their current citizenship status and a certified copy of the authorization issued by Citizenship and Immigration Canada which permits them to engage in the practice of dentistry in Canada.'

## DLOSCE notları
N/A – the DLOSCE (ADA/JCNDE) plays no role in Canada. Nova Scotia requires the NDEB general dentistry certificate; for graduates of CDAC/CODA-accredited programmes (CDAC treats CODA-accredited programmes outside the US as accredited; NSRDDA's own checklist names CODA explicitly) the NDEB certificate is obtained via the computer-based Virtual OSCE at Prometric, with no hands-on component. ADEX/DLOSCE results are not referenced anywhere in NSRDDA requirements.

PGY-1 kabul: False

- **itin_or_no_ssn_alternative:** SIN is not listed among the required documents on the NSRDDA application checklist or in the 2025 Policies, so no SIN alternative is needed (field apparently not required). The binding requirement is instead a copy of the IRCC authorisation permitting practice (i.e., a work permit) for non-citizens/non-PRs.
- **in_jurisdiction_address_required:** False
- **barrier_level:** high
- **details:** Regulation: Dentistry and Dental Assisting Regulations made under the Regulated Health Professions Act, s.12(1)(a)(vi) – criteria for registration include proof that 'they are a Canadian citizen or legally entitled to live and work in Canada' (also (v) 'they have demonstrated proficiency in the English language, in the manner prescribed by the registrar'). Application checklist item 5: 'An applicant must provide a certified copy of their Canadian birth certificate, Canadian citizenship card, or proof of permanent Canadian residency status. If an applicant is not a Canadian citizen or permanent resident of Canada, the applicant must provide details about their current citizenship status and a certified copy of the authorization issued by Citizenship and Immigration Canada which permits them to engage in the practice of dentistry in Canada.' Policies on Requirements for Registration and Licensing of Dentists (2025) s.3.5 repeats this wording for every dentist category. A visitor-status applicant therefore cannot be registered/licensed; there is no evidence of a non-practising or registration-only category that can be held without status (the regulation lists only 'dentist practising licence', 'dental assistant practising licence', 'any other category of practising licence established in the bylaws' and conditional licence categories). No SIN requirement and no in-province address requirement were found in the checklist; a Canadian mailing address was not listed as a requirement (unverified in the online portal).

## Yüz yüze adımlar
(1) BLS: 'Verification of your certification in BLS obtained within the past 12 months (must be an in-person hands-on course from a recognized provider)' – can be done abroad if the provider is 'recognized' (unclear whether a non-Canadian provider counts). (2) Criminal Record Check and Vulnerable Sector Search within 12 months – Vulnerable Sector checks are issued by Canadian police and for non-residents usually need fingerprints (RCMP certified criminal record check) and a Canadian address/affiliation; feasibility from abroad unclear. (3) 'Our office will contact the applicant to schedule an appointment to meet with the Registrar to discuss their application' – a licensing meeting; format (in person vs. video) not stated. (4) Certified copies of documents (see NSRDDA 'Certifying Documents' page) – notarised/certified copies can be made abroad. (5) No jurisprudence exam, no fingerprinting by the regulator, and no in-person clinical exam; the application itself is fully online via the Alinity portal (sign up, upload documents, pay online). NDEB Virtual OSCE is taken at a Prometric centre (available internationally). Applications not completed within 3 months are deleted.

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
No jurisprudence exam listed. English proficiency 'in the manner prescribed by the registrar' (reg. s.12(1)(a)(v); likely satisfied by graduation from an accredited programme – unverified for a Turkish programme). BLS for healthcare providers within 12 months, in-person hands-on. Criminal Record Check + Vulnerable Sector Search within 12 months. Two signed, dated character references from non-family members who have known the applicant 4 years, written within 12 months. Passport photo within 12 months. Dean's letter certifying graduation. Mandatory malpractice insurance through CDSPI, minimum $3 million (proof that an application is in progress accepted). Currency of practice (450 h in preceding 36 months) only if NDEB certificate obtained more than 36 months before applying – not applicable to a new graduate. Timeline: licence issued within 5 business days of a complete application (longer in December and May–June); PACA-compliant.

## İlk ücretler
Fee schedule effective 1 Oct 2026 (approved 25 Sept 2026): non-refundable application fee CAD $100 (paid on submission); initial registration $101; annual licence fee $1,940 (licensing year 1 Dec – 30 Nov; reduced to $970 if licensing on/after 1 June). Plus CDSPI malpractice insurance (cost not stated), NDEB fees, police checks, BLS course. Reinstatement after expiry for non-payment $971; licence reactivation fee $99 for dentists who relinquished more than 18 months ago. Application and initial registration fees are waived only for applicants already licensed in another Canadian province.

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Annual; licensing year 1 December – 30 November (renewal opens in the fall).
- **renewal_fee:** $1,940 per year (2026-27 fee schedule); $970 half-year if licensing on/after 1 June; reinstatement for non-payment $971.
- **ce_hours:** Continuing Competence Program on a 36-month cycle (prorated if cycle <36 months, e.g. reinstatement after medical leave), plus a Mandatory Medical Emergencies Course and optional study clubs. The exact credit-hour total was not verified in this research (the CE page links to a separate 'Continuing Competence Program' page not read); historically PDBNS required 90 credits per 3-year cycle – unverified.
- **ce_online_ok:** Unclear. The programme includes a 'Mandatory Medical Emergencies Course' (format not verified, likely involving hands-on emergency/CPR content) and BLS is required to be in-person hands-on, so at least some components are not online self-study.
- **cpr_required:** Yes – BLS for healthcare providers, 'must be an in-person hands-on course from a recognized provider', within 12 months at initial licensure; recurrence at renewal not verified but a medical emergencies course is mandatory in the continuing competence cycle.
- **practice_hours_required:** Yes in principle: reg. s.13(1)(a)(v) requires for a practising licence that 'they meet the currency of practice requirements for the licensing category for which they are seeking a practising licence'. The board's defined currency standard is 'a minimum of 450 hours of clinical practice within the 36 months preceding the date of application' (Policies 2025 s.3.8, applied to initial applicants whose NDEB certificate is >36 months old; the same standard applies on reactivation/reinstatement – exact renewal application not verified).
- **address_or_residency_required:** No in-province address or residency requirement found; but the regulation requires the registrant to be a 'Canadian citizen or legally entitled to live and work in Canada', so a work permit must remain valid – a visitor abroad cannot satisfy it.
- **insurance_required:** Yes – mandatory professional liability (malpractice) insurance through CDSPI, minimum $3 million (reg. s.13(1)(a)(iii); application checklist item 6). Only Canadian Armed Forces dentists are exempt.
- **inactive_status:** No non-practising/inactive/retired register was found. The regulation lists only practising licence categories (dentist, dental assistant, any other bylaw category) and conditional licence categories. Retirement = relinquishing the licence ('When you relinquish your license, you will cease to be licensed as a dentist in Nova Scotia'); CDSPI provides run-off coverage at no cost for retirees. Re-entry: 'Licence Reactivation Fee $99 (for dentists who have relinquished their licence more than 18 months ago and who wish to be reinstated)'.
- **reactivation:** Reactivation after relinquishment >18 months: $99 reactivation fee plus the standard practising-licence criteria – currency of practice (450 h in prior 36 months), continuing competence requirements, CDSPI insurance, police checks, BLS and valid immigration authorisation; reg. s.13(1)(a)(vii) allows the registrar to require 'any assessments or education' – so after years without practice an assessment/refresher is likely. No new clinical exam is specified. Details unverified.
- **remote_maintainability:** hard
- **details:** Keeping an active Nova Scotia licence from abroad is impractical: (a) must remain 'legally entitled to live and work in Canada' (work permit) – impossible on visitor status; (b) $1,940/year plus CDSPI $3M insurance; (c) currency-of-practice standard of 450 clinical hours per 36 months; (d) in-person hands-on BLS and a mandatory medical emergencies course; (e) no cheap inactive/non-practising category – only relinquishment with a $99 reactivation fee, after which full practising criteria must be met again.
