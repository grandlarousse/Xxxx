# Delaware (USA) — ajan bulgusu

Düzenleyici: Delaware Board of Dentistry and Dental Hygiene (Division of Professional Regulation, DPR; online portal DELPROS) — https://dpr.delaware.gov/boards/dental/

**Teori-only rota:** no · **Göçmenlik engeli:** low · **Uzaktan sürdürme:** moderate · **Güven:** medium

## Sınav yolları

- **Licensure by examination (standard route for a new CODA graduate)** (el becerisi: evet): 24 Del. C. §1122(a): CODA dental degree + (a)(3) ONE YEAR as a dental intern in a CODA-accredited general practice residency (PGY-1) OR a CODA-approved specialty residency (4+ years deemed equivalent; shorter specialty residencies must show GPR-equivalent rotations, Reg. 4.3-4.4) + (a)(4) passing score on the Delaware jurisprudence exam and the National Board (NBDE/INBDE) + (a)(5) the Delaware Practical Board Examination under §1123, 'a validated practical examination prepared and administered by the Board'. The practical exam is Delaware's own hands-on clinical exam, given in Delaware twice a year (early January and June; next sitting 7-8 Jan 2027), capped at 18 candidates, $250 exam fee on top of the $362 application fee; three failures bar further attempts absent mitigating circumstances; it must have been passed within 5 years of application (Reg. 10.4). No regional exam (ADEX, CRDTS, SRTA, WREB, CITA) and no DLOSCE/OSCE is accepted in lieu of it. Also required: CPR certification, SBI + FBI fingerprint criminal-history checks.
- **Licensure by reciprocity / credentials (24 Del. C. §1124) - not open to a new graduate** (el becerisi: evet): Requires a current licence in good standing in another US state/DC/territory PLUS proof of 3 years of active dental practice in another US jurisdiction (Reg. 4.5.1: average of at least 1000 hours of direct patient care per year), CODA degree, National Board, Delaware jurisprudence exam, CPR, and STILL the Delaware practical examination (§1124(a)(3)c incorporates §1122(a)(5); DPR exams page: 'All applicants, regardless of years of practice, are required to pass the Delaware Practical Board Examination in dentistry'). The 3 years of practice substitutes only for the residency requirement (Reg. 4.5), not for the practical exam.
- **DLOSCE / OSCE-only route** (el becerisi: evet): Not available. Delaware is not among the states listed in the 2026 DLOSCE Candidate Guide and nothing in Title 24 Ch. 11 or Regulation 1100 recognises the DLOSCE, an ADEX-integrated DLOSCE component, or any other third-party clinical/OSCE exam; the statute mandates the Board's own practical examination (§1123(a)(1)). hands_on_required is marked true because the only way to be licensed still goes through the Delaware practical exam.
- **PGY-1 residency in lieu of clinical exam** (el becerisi: evet): Not available as a substitute. In Delaware the one-year CODA general practice residency (or specialty residency) is an ADDITIONAL mandatory prerequisite under §1122(a)(3), stacked on top of the Delaware practical exam; it does not replace the exam. (For the candidate, completing a CODA GPR would itself require a US training visa, which they do not intend to obtain.)
- **Dentist-Community Health limited licence with later upgrade** (el becerisi: hayır): DPR lists a 'Dentist-Community Health' licence ($352) and a 'Dentist-Community Health Upgrade to Dentist' ($40). The DPR dentist page states: 'You should only apply to upgrade for full Dentist licensure when you have completed 3600 hours over 2 years at an FQHC or a government-operated dental clinic' (max 40 h/week, at least 32 h/week direct patient care at the approved site); the upgrade requirements list only proof of those hours. The entry requirements for the initial Community Health licence (and whether the practical exam is waived at the upgrade stage) were NOT verified in this research. Either way it requires two years of employment inside Delaware, i.e. US work authorisation, so it is not usable by the candidate.
- **Dentist Limited-Resident (training) licence** (el becerisi: hayır): $77 annual licence (expires 30 June each year) for dentists in a Delaware residency programme; not eligible for inactive status; not a practice licence and requires enrolment in a US residency (visa).

## Teori-only açıklaması
Delaware has no computer/written-only route. 24 Del. C. §1122(a) requires that an applicant 'Has achieved a passing score on the Delaware jurisprudence and National Board of Dental Examiners' examinations' AND '(5) Has successfully completed the Delaware practical examination under § 1123 of this title'; §1123(a)(1): 'An applicant applying for licensure by examination shall successfully pass, with a score established by the Board, a validated practical examination prepared and administered by the Board.' Regulation 1100 §10.1: 'No person shall be eligible to take the practical (clinical) examination in dentistry administered by the Delaware Board of Dentistry and Dental Hygiene unless the applicant has received a degree in dentistry from an accredited dental college ... accredited by [CODA].' DPR Examinations page: 'All applicants, regardless of years of practice, are required to pass the Delaware Practical Board Examination in dentistry or dental hygiene and Delaware Jurisprudence Examination except for dental hygienists applying by reciprocity.' In addition §1122(a)(3) requires '1 year's experience as a dental intern within a general practice residency accredited by [CODA]' or a CODA specialty residency (only applicants by reciprocity with 3 years of US practice are excused from the residency, Reg. 4.5). So a new graduate faces BOTH a hands-on state clinical exam AND a mandatory residency; neither the DLOSCE, a regional exam, nor the INBDE alone can substitute. (ADA Delaware Laws & Rules summary, updated 7 Oct 2026.)

## DLOSCE notları
Delaware does not accept the DLOSCE in any form. It is not on the DLOSCE Candidate Guide list of accepting states, and neither Title 24 Chapter 11 nor Regulation 1100 (as reproduced on the ADA Delaware page updated 7 Oct 2026) mentions the DLOSCE, an OSCE, ADEX or any regional/third-party clinical exam; the only clinical exam recognised is the Board's own Delaware Practical Board Examination (§1123). Consequently the question of whether an ADEX-integrated DLOSCE score or an earlier standalone DLOSCE score would suffice is moot: neither has any status in Delaware, and ADEX itself is not accepted either.

Laws c. 193, Senate Substitute 1 for SB 80, approved 31 Aug 2023): 'Notwithstanding any other law or regulation, if an applicant for a license does not have a social security number, the applicant must do 1 or more of the following when completing an application for a license: a. Provide an alternative personally identifying number, such as the applicant's individual taxpayer identification number, b. The same language was inserted into 29 Del. C. §8735(m) (the DPR statute that otherwise requires an SSN on every licence application/renewal). Source: https://legis.delaware.gov/SessionLaws/Chapter?id=41812. The exact DELPROS screen mechanics (how the attestation is captured online) were not verified.
- **in_jurisdiction_address_required:** False
- **barrier_level:** low
- **details:** Delaware is one of the most permissive US states on immigration status for professional licences. 29 Del. C. §610 (enacted 2023): '(c) Notwithstanding any other law or regulation, the State may not deny an application because of the applicant's immigration or citizenship status.' The Act's title is 'AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 29 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO IMMIGRATION STATUS REQUIREMENTS FOR PROFESSIONAL LICENSES' and its policy statement invokes 8 U.S.C. §1621(d) ('each resident of this State, regardless of immigration or citizenship status, is eligible to receive the benefit of applying for a license'). No SAVE check, visa/I-94 copy, or lawful-presence affidavit appears in Title 24 Ch. 11, Regulation 1100 or the DPR application pages reviewed. Title 24 Ch. 11 contains no citizenship, residency, Delaware-address or work-authorisation requirement for dentists; DPR sends all notices by email and asks only that contact information be kept current. CAVEAT: although the licence itself has no immigration barrier, the statutory prerequisite of a one-year CODA general practice residency (§1122(a)(3)) can in practice only be met inside a US (or Canadian) programme, which requires a training visa the candidate does not intend to obtain; and the in-person Delaware practical exam and fingerprinting require travel to Delaware (possible on a visitor visa).

## Yüz yüze adımlar
(1) Delaware Practical Board Examination: in person in Delaware, offered only twice a year (early January and early June; next dental sitting 7-8 January 2027), capped at 18 candidates; Board must pre-approve the candidate via a DELPROS application; candidates have 2 years from the application to pass; results take up to 2 weeks to reach the Board. This alone forces at least one trip (and a second if a retake is needed). (2) Fingerprinting: §1122(c)(6) requires the applicant to 'submit, at the applicant's expense, fingerprints and other necessary information' for a Delaware State Bureau of Identification (SBI) report and an FBI federal criminal-history report; the DPR 'Criminal Background Check Process' normally uses in-person fingerprinting at SBI sites in Delaware. Whether DPR accepts mailed fingerprint cards from abroad was not verified (unclear); it could be done during the exam trip. (3) Delaware Jurisprudence Examination: open-book, 30 multiple-choice questions on the licence law and regulations; must be passed before the licence issues. The ADA summary and DPR page do not state whether it is taken online or at the Board office (unclear). (4) Application: entirely online in DELPROS (account, uploads, acknowledgements; application must be submitted within 6 months of starting); third-party documents (transcripts, residency letters, licence verifications) are sent directly by the institution; forms needing notary/seal may have to be mailed. (5) Evidence 'verified by oath' (§1122(a)) implies notarised attestations; a foreign notary/consular notarisation should be possible but was not verified. (6) CPR course with hands-on participation is required at initial licensure and every renewal (AHA/Red Cross or equivalent; online-only courses not accepted) - can be taken outside Delaware but not purely online. No interview requirement was found.

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
Delaware jurisprudence exam (open-book, 30 MCQ); National Board (NBDE/INBDE) passing score; proof of current CPR certification with hands-on component (online-only courses not accepted; e.g. American Heart Association, American Red Cross); SBI and FBI fingerprint criminal-history checks at applicant's expense; sworn/oath-verified application; no administrative penalties, no drug/alcohol impairment, no substantially related criminal conviction (waiver possible); one-year CODA GPR or CODA specialty residency (letter sent directly from the sponsoring institution) unless 3 years of US practice (reciprocity). No English-language test is mentioned. No liability-insurance requirement found for licensure. Timelines: practical exam only twice yearly (Jan/June), 18-candidate cap; Board approval needed before sitting; up to 2 weeks after passing for scores to be received and licence issued; DELPROS application must be submitted within 6 months of being started.

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Biennial; dentist licences expire 31 May of even-numbered years (24 Del. C. §1126(b); Reg. 6.1). Renewal is online in DELPROS with electronic CE attestation.
- **renewal_fee:** Not published; DPR fee page says 'You are notified of the amount of the renewal fee at the time of renewal'. Late renewal (up to 1 year after expiry) requires a late fee in addition to the renewal fee.
- **ce_hours:** 50 hours of continuing professional education (CPE) every 2 years, of which 2 hours must be in infection control (Reg. 6.3). Pro-rated for the first renewal: 0 hours if licensed less than 1 year as of 31 May of the renewal year (Reg. 6.7.2). Random post-renewal audits; keep certificates.
- **ce_online_ok:** Partly verified. The regulations recognise programmes sponsored/approved by ADA CERP, AGD PACE, dental schools, specialty societies and study clubs and expressly include 'live webinars'; any cap on self-study/non-live hours in Reg. 6.4-6.6 for dentists was not read (unclear). The CPR component (see below) cannot be done online. Reg. 6.7.1 allows the Board to grant CPE exemptions in 'unusual or extraordinary circumstances' such as 'extended absence from the country'.
- **cpr_required:** Yes. Reg. 6.3: 'licensees must provide evidence that they have successfully completed a current course in cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) every two (2) years. The CPR course must encompass hands on clinical participation. On-line courses will not be accepted to satisfy the CPR requirement.' Hands-on skills session required (AHA/Red Cross or equivalent), but it can be taken anywhere, not necessarily in Delaware.
- **practice_hours_required:** None found for renewal of an active licence. (Practice hours matter only for reciprocity applicants - 1000 h/yr for 3 years - and for §1126(d) reinstatement after a lapse of more than 5 years.)
- **address_or_residency_required:** No in-state address or residency requirement found in Title 24 Ch. 11 or Reg. 1100. DPR sends all renewal and audit notices by email only and requires licensees to keep contact information current in DELPROS.
- **insurance_required:** No mandatory professional liability insurance requirement found in the statute, regulations or DPR pages reviewed.
- **inactive_status:** Available. 24 Del. C. §1126(e): 'All individuals licensed under this chapter, upon written request, may be placed in an inactive status in accordance with the Board's rules and regulations. Such individual may reenter practice upon written notification to the Board of the intent to do so and completion of continuing education as required in the Board's rules and regulations.' DPR renewal page: an active Dentist, Hygienist or Academic licensee 'not practicing in Delaware' may request inactive status by Service Request in DELPROS; the request must be made before the late-renewal period ends (31 May of odd years). Fee, CPE obligations while inactive and any maximum duration are in Reg. 8.2, which could not be retrieved (regulations.delaware.gov page returned no content) - unclear.
- **reactivation:** From inactive: Service Request in DELPROS, written notice of intent to resume practice, and completion of CE as required by Reg. 8.2 (details not retrieved; no re-examination is mentioned in the statute for reactivation from inactive status). From lapse: late renewal possible up to 1 year after expiry with late fee and proof of CE (§1126(c), Reg. 6.2); after that the person 'will be considered a new applicant'. §1126(d): a licence expired for failure to renew 'over a period of more than 5 years' where the person has not been in active full-time practice in another US state/territory during the previous 5 years requires, in addition to applying as a new applicant, 'reexamination or other formal assessment of competency as approved by the Board'.
- **remote_maintainability:** moderate
- **details:** An active Delaware licence can be renewed from abroad every two years online with a fee and an electronic attestation of 50 CPE hours (live webinars acceptable) and no practice-hour, address, residency or insurance requirement, but the biennial hands-on CPR course is unavoidable (online CPR expressly rejected) and audit documentation may be requested. Inactive status exists and keeps the licence from lapsing; the regulation governing its cost, CPE and reactivation (Reg. 8.2) was not retrievable, so its long-term use is unclear. Letting the licence lapse for more than 5 years without US practice triggers re-examination. All of this is academic for the candidate, because initial licensure is not attainable without the hands-on Delaware practical exam and a CODA residency.

## Açık sorular
1) Content of the Delaware Practical Board Examination (live-patient vs manikin/typodont sections) was not readable (JS-only page); it is hands-on either way. 2) Regulation 8.2 (inactive status: fee, CPE while inactive, maximum duration, reactivation steps) and Reg. 6.4-6.6 (any cap on self-study/online CPE for dentists) could not be retrieved from regulations.delaware.gov and should be read directly. 3) Whether DPR accepts mailed fingerprint cards for SBI/FBI checks from applicants abroad, and whether the jurisprudence exam is delivered online or at the Board office. 4) How DELPROS implements the 29 Del. C. §610 'social security number exemption attestation' in practice (screen/form), and whether the DELPROS account registration itself demands an SSN. 5) Entry requirements of the Dentist-Community Health licence and whether the practical exam is waived on upgrade (irrelevant for the candidate since it requires 2 years of Delaware employment). 6) Renewal fee amount (not published). 7) Whether a CODA-accredited general practice residency located outside the US would satisfy §1122(a)(3) (none is known to exist in Turkey). Bottom line: Delaware is immigration-friendly for licensure (no citizenship/lawful-presence/SSN barrier) but fails the candidate's core requirement because it mandates both a Board-administered hands-on practical exam and a one-year CODA residency, with no DLOSCE, regional-exam or theory-only alternative.
