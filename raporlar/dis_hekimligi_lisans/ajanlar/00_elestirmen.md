NDEB logistics missing from the summary: Virtual OSCE sittings are at Prometric centres (Canada and some international sites) - whether a Turkish citizen can sit outside Canada without a visitor visa, NDEB application ID requirements, and the three-year NDEB-currency rule in BC (and any similar rule elsewhere) should be captured. 4. US INBDE-only licensure: none exists; the sweep correctly finds no state licensing on INBDE alone, but it should state explicitly that Minnesota's former in-house exam, New York's residency mandate, California's portfolio and Delaware's practical exam are all hands-on/residency, and that the DDH Compact (13 states, no privileges issued yet, requires a 'clinical assessment' and an unencumbered home-state licence) provides no route. 5. Two-step routes were not analysed: (a) a Canadian licence (BC/SK) followed by US licensure by credentials/endorsement (Pennsylvania's Dental Law Canada reciprocity clause; Washington's licensure-by-credentials; Minnesota/other states accepting Canadian licensure) - most credential routes require years of active practice, which the candidate (practising abroad) will not accumulate, but this should be stated; (b) Colorado's OSCE clause combined with an NDEB certificate. 6. 2025-2026 law changes not surveyed systematically: state laws granting provisional/limited licences to internationally trained dentists (Iowa, Florida, Tennessee, Virginia, Wisconsin, Illinois 2024-2026 bills) are irrelevant to a CODA graduate but should be listed as 'not applicable'; new lawful-presence laws (Texas AG opinion KP-0516 2026 on SSN affidavits, Florida 2025 licence changes) should be cross-checked for the 'low' barrier states that have no findings file (California, Maine, Michigan, Connecticut, Nevada, New Hampshire, Utah). 7. The ADEX-reported DLOSCE question (will any of CO/WA/KY/OR/AZ license on the DLOSCE component alone) is the single most consequential unknown and has not been put to any board in writing; the sweep should record board e-mail answers rather than inferences. 8. Puerto Rico row says lawful presence true and remote 'unclear' with no findings file - needs a source (Puerto Rico Board requires a Spanish-language revalida exam, which the summary may not mention). 9. Alaska is rated barrier 'low' with an SSN-exemption form but the DLOSCE 'partial' asterisk (hands-on assessment required) should be reflected consistently with Iowa. 10. The summary lacks a uniform record of which jurisdictions have an explicit no-SSN/ITIN mechanism (confirmed: Colorado C.R.S. 24-34-107, Washington RCW 18.415.010 and DOH form, California B&P 30, Alaska exemption form) versus those where an SSN field exists with no stated alternative (Kentucky, Oregon, Arizona, Iowa, Pennsylvania); 'ssn_or_sin_required' should be 'unclear' rather than true/false in the latter group.
