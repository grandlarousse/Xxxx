# Kesişen konu: INBDE and US exam logistics for a graduate of a CODA-accredited programme located outside the US (Yeditepe University, Turkey): eligibility, DENTPIN, fees, test locations, score reporting, ID/visa issues, and the ADA / CMDL licensure dashboards as data sources

Güven: high

## Bulgular

All INBDE facts below are quoted from the official JCNDE "INBDE 2026 Candidate Guide" (PDF, footer "Updated: 12/15/2025"), https://jcnde.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/jcnde/files/2026_inbde_candidate_guide.pdf, unless another URL is given.

(a) ELIGIBILITY, DEAN CERTIFICATION, DENTPIN, FEES
- The guide has no separate category for CODA-accredited programmes located outside the US; eligibility turns only on whether the programme is CODA-accredited: "Eligibility requirements differ based on factors such as the candidate's educational background (e.g., educated by a program accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation (CODA) vs. not) and current stage of education". It adds that CDAC (Canada)-accredited candidates are treated like CODA candidates "through a reciprocal agreement" (guide p.20).
- CODA-accredited pathways (guide p.20): "Current Student – CODA Accredited Program: A current student in a CODA accredited program is eligible for examination when the dean (or their designee) provides confirmation that the student is prepared in all applicable disciplines." "Dentist – Graduate of CODA Accredited Program: A dentist who is an active, life, or retired member of the American Dental Association at the time of application is eligible for examination without further documentation. An affiliate member or nonmember must submit a copy of their diploma or provide other appropriate verification of their degree." So a Yeditepe graduate (post-1 Feb 2024) applies as a graduate of a CODA programme with a copy of the diploma; no dean certification form is needed once graduated (the dean's confirmation applies to current students).
- By contrast, the non-CODA route requires an Educational Credential Evaluators (ECE) degree confirmation and, for current students, a "Certification of Eligibility Form ... with the seal of the university and the signature of the dean or registrar" (guide p.20). This should NOT apply to a CODA-accredited Yeditepe graduate.
- DENTPIN: "Before applying to take this examination, candidates must first obtain a Dental Personal Identification Number (DENTPIN). Register ... at ADA.org/DENTPIN" (guide, Application Procedures). The ADA DENTPIN page states: "DENTPIN ... is a unique, eight-digit number that helps protect your personal identifying information. It replaces the use of Social Security Numbers, Canadian Social Insurance Numbers or Reference Numbers" (https://www.ada.org/dentpin). A grep of the entire 2026 guide for "social security", "SSN", "citizen" and "visa" returned zero hits: the INBDE has no SSN, citizenship or immigration-status requirement. The name on the DENTPIN record and application must match the test-day IDs exactly (guide; and https://jcnde.ada.org/inbde/inbde-apply: "The name on your IDs must match exactly the name on your application or you will be denied admission").
- Fees (guide p.21, all USD, non-refundable): Examination Fee $890 ("includes official results reporting to the candidate, three licensing jurisdictions (provided result report requests are made at the time of application), and the candidate's dental program (CODA accredited program)"); "RECOMMENDED: Pricing Bundle" INBDE + DLOSCE $1,080 ("save $470"); "Processing Fee for International Candidates $435 — This processing fee applies to candidates who are students or graduates of a dental program that is not accredited by CODA" (i.e. defined by accreditation, not geography, so it should not apply to Yeditepe graduates; confirm with JCNDE); Results Report Fee $55 per report for recipients not selected at application; Results Audit $105; Eligibility Extension $150 (45-day extension of the eligibility window); Prometric rescheduling fees $50 (30+ business days), $70 (5-29), $150 (1-4). "A new application and fee must be submitted before each testing attempt." If the exam is not taken within the eligibility window "the application fee is forfeited". Retest rule: "5 Years/5 Attempts" from the first attempt; a fifth attempt only after 12 months from the first attempt.
- Note: the 12/15/2025 guide still sells the INBDE+DLOSCE bundle; per the known facts, standalone DLOSCE administrations ceased after 9 Oct 2026, so the bundle wording is out of date for new candidates.

(b) WHERE THE INBDE CAN BE TAKEN
- Guide p.2: "This examination is administered by Prometric ... in the United States and Canada." JCNDE apply page: "All candidates will take the INBDE at a testing center operated by Prometric ... Contact Prometric to choose your exam date. Your appointment should be scheduled 60 to 90 days before your desired test date ... supply your preferred state to find a test center" (https://jcnde.ada.org/inbde/inbde-apply). The only occurrences of "international" in the whole guide are the $435 non-CODA processing fee and the ADEA CAAPID reference; there is NO mention of international (non-US/Canada) Prometric centres and NO international testing fee. Conclusion: the INBDE must be sat in the US (or its territories) or Canada; a Turkish citizen therefore needs a US B-1/B-2 visa or a Canadian visitor visa (TRV) for the test trip. Canada is attractive because the NDEB Virtual OSCE is also delivered at Prometric centres, so one Canadian trip could cover both exams (whether Prometric Canada sites are open to INBDE candidates who are not Canadian residents is not restricted in the guide, but was not separately confirmed).
- Application flow (apply page): obtain DENTPIN -> submit online application and pay by card -> receive "eligibility email" after review -> schedule with Prometric. Total administration time is twelve hours over two days (guide, administration schedule).

(c) SCORE VALIDITY AND REPORTING
- "National Board Examination results are reported as either 'pass' or 'fail'" (guide p.17). Reporting: the $890 fee covers reports to the candidate, up to three licensing jurisdictions named at application, and the CODA programme; later reports cost $55 each and are requested in the DENTPIN account under 'Requests': "DTS will provide a history of results to dental and dental hygiene boards and any additional recipients indicated" (guide p.19). Results go to boards only "upon written candidate authorization or designation by electronic means through the electronic application or score report request form" (guide, Privacy). Candidates from accredited programmes who graduated within the last five years automatically consent to results being shared with their programme dean.
- Validity: the guide imposes no expiry on a passing result; "Examination results shall be retained indefinitely" (guide, Privacy). Any time limit on how old an INBDE pass may be is set by individual state boards (not verified per state here; check each board's rules). The 5-years/5-attempts rule limits attempts, not the life of a pass.

(d) VISITOR-VISA HOLDERS AND ID
- Test-day ID (guide, Identification Policy): "two original and current forms of identification (ID) — one primary and one secondary — will be required ... The primary ID must be a government issued ID with your photograph, name, and signature. Examples of acceptable primary IDs include—but are not limited to—a driver's license, passport, or passport card. All IDs, with the exception of passports, must be in English. The secondary ID must contain your name and signature ... debit cards, library cards, or a credit card." Expired IDs are not accepted. Prometric collects biometrics for identity verification. A Turkish passport plus a signed bank card therefore satisfies the INBDE ID rule; no US-issued ID, SSN or immigration document is required.
- Immigration status: neither the JCNDE guide nor the apply page mentions citizenship, visa class or lawful presence; eligibility is purely educational. Whether sitting an exam is a permitted B-1/B-2 activity was not verified from a US Department of State/USCIS page within this task (widely done in practice, e.g. by foreign medical graduates, but mark as "to confirm with the consulate"). ADEX (hands-on, not wanted) and state jurisprudence exam ID/visa rules were not checked here (budget) — unclear, but the same passport-based Prometric/CDCA ID policies are expected.

(e) ADA DENTAL LICENSURE DASHBOARD AND CMDL AS DATA SOURCES
- The ADA "Dental licensure maps" page (https://www.ada.org/en/resources/licensure/dental-licensure-by-state-map, a.k.a. ADA.org/licensuremaps) hosts the green "Dental Licensure Dashboard" with four interactive maps: "Initial Licensure Requirements; CE & Renewal Requirements; Licensure by Credentials; Specialty Licensure". Disclaimer on the page: "The ADA attempts to keep all maps current based on available information from state dental boards, clinical testing agencies and state dental associations ... only official documented changes are reflected on the maps. Individuals ... are strongly urged to consult with the respective state board of dentistry". The dashboard is an embedded interactive visualisation; its state-by-state content is NOT extractable as text with the fetch tool, so the per-state exam list could not be read in this task.
- The ADA "Licensure for dental students" page (https://www.ada.org/en/resources/licensure/student-licensure) defines the pathway taxonomy used on the map: PGY-1 residency; "an objective structured clinical examination (DLOSCE administered by JCNDE or OSCE administered by NDEB)"; "a portfolio exam (2014 California Portfolio Exam)"; or hands-on exams by CDCA-WREB-CITA, CRDTS or SRTA. It instructs: "Review the Dental Licensure Dashboard maps to see which exams (CDCA, CITA, CRDTS, SRTA, WREB, DLOSCE) and which type (manikin, patient, OSCE) are accepted ... Note that Delaware administers its own patient exam and New York requires completion of an approved post-graduate year residency program (PGY-1)." It also offers sub-maps "DLOSCE & OSCE — See which states accept objective structured clinical exams" and "Portfolio exam". No ADA page read lists any US state that licenses with NO clinical assessment at all; the only non-exam pathways named are PGY-1 and the California portfolio (which is itself a patient-based in-school clinical assessment).
- The CMDL "Licensure Map" page (https://www.adafoundation.org/sitecore/content/ADA-Organization/ADA/CMDL/home/licensure-map) does not hold its own dataset: "The Dental Licensure Dashboard is provided by the ADA" (it re-embeds the ADA dashboard). CMDL disbanded in September 2026 (ADA News, https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2026/september/coalition-for-modernizing-dental-licensure-sunsets-after-achieving-portability-goals/): "Currently, 49 states and Washington, D.C., recognize at least one initial dental or dental hygiene licensure pathway that does not require a patient exam"; alternatives are "manikin-based assessments and objective structured clinical examinations"; "14 states ... still maintain statutory or regulatory language recognizing procedure-based live-patient exams"; the Dentist and Dental Hygienist Compact has 13 states (Arkansas, Colorado, Iowa, Kansas, Maine, Minnesota, Nebraska, Ohio, Oklahoma, Tennessee, Virginia, Washington, Wisconsin). Important nuance: "no patient exam" is NOT "no hands-on exam" — manikin exams count as hands-on for this candidate, so the CMDL "49 states" figure must not be read as 49 theory-only states. The only OSCE-only (computer-based) US route remains the DLOSCE list already in the known facts (AZ, CO, IN, KY, OR, WA; AK and IA partial), subject to the ADEX-integration problem.

## Aday için sonuçlar

1. The INBDE itself is immigration-neutral: no SSN (DENTPIN explicitly replaces SSN/SIN), no citizenship/visa question in the application, and a Turkish passport is an acceptable primary ID (passports are the one ID allowed in a non-English language). As a graduate of a CODA-accredited programme the candidate applies with a copy of the Yeditepe diploma (no ECE, no Certification of Eligibility Form) and the $435 non-CODA processing fee should not apply; fee is $890 per attempt. Confirm with JCNDE that its online application's school list recognises Yeditepe as CODA-accredited (graduation after 1 Feb 2024) before paying.
2. There are no international Prometric sites for the INBDE: it is delivered only in the US (and territories) and Canada, so at least one visitor-visa trip is unavoidable. A Canadian visitor visa trip could combine the INBDE with the NDEB Virtual OSCE (both at Prometric), which is the only North American pathway with no hands-on component; the INBDE is not needed for NDEB certification but is required by every US state board.
3. At application, name the three most likely state boards as free report recipients (later reports cost $55 each); results are pass/fail, retained indefinitely by JCNDE, and have no JCNDE expiry, but each state board may impose its own age limit on the exam, so check the target board's rule before planning a multi-year gap.
4. Schedule 60-90 days ahead, test within the eligibility window (extension $150, fee forfeited otherwise) and make sure the passport name exactly matches the DENTPIN/application name.
5. The ADA dashboard and the (now defunct) CMDL map are the authoritative public datasets for which exams each state accepts, but they are interactive maps that must be read in a browser (Chrome recommended); their headline "49 states + DC without a patient exam" counts manikin exams, which are hands-on and therefore not useful for this candidate. No ADA source identifies any state that requires no clinical assessment at all; the non-hands-on US possibilities are only the DLOSCE-accepting states and PGY-1 (which the candidate excludes).
