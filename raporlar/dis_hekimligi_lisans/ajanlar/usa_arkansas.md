# Arkansas (USA) — ajan bulgusu

## Sınav yolları

- **Licensure by examination (ADEX via CDCA-WREB-CITA, CRDTS, SRTA)** (el becerisi: evet): A.C.A. 17-82-303/304 and Board Rules Article VIII. Requirements (ADA summary updated 7 Oct 2026): age 21, good moral character, graduation from an ADA(CODA)-accredited dental college (DDS/DMD), National Board (INBDE) results sent directly from JCNDE, satisfactory completion of a clinical examination 'administered by the Arkansas State Board of Dental Examiners or any regional testing agency whose examination is accepted by the Board' (minimum 75% on each segment), current Healthcare Provider BLS/CPR certificate, Arkansas Jurisprudence Examination, criminal background check, Board forms, fee ($150 by rule). Accepted agencies: ADEX (CDCA-WREB-CITA), CRDTS, SRTA. Scores valid 5 years. Board policy: 'A clinical patient based component of the examination must be administered by the testing agency and successfully completed by the candidate in order to be accepted. Effective January 1, 2020, the Board will only accept clinical dental examinations that consist of at least the following components: Restorative clinical examination section, manikin prosthetic section, manikin endodontic section with posterior and interior access, treatment planning, and periodontal section.' Therefore a live-patient restorative component plus manikin sections is unavoidable.
- **DLOSCE / OSCE-only** (el becerisi: evet): NOT available. Arkansas is not among the DLOSCE-accepting states in the 2026 DLOSCE Candidate Guide, the Board's rules do not mention DLOSCE/OSCE, and the Board expressly requires a patient-based clinical component plus manikin sections. Neither a standalone DLOSCE score nor the DLOSCE component reported from an ADEX series would satisfy the clinical-exam requirement on its own.
- **PGY-1 / residency in lieu of clinical exam** (el becerisi: evet): NOT available. No residency-in-lieu-of-exam provision appears in A.C.A. 17-82-303/304 or Board Rules Article VIII as summarised by the ADA (updated 7 Oct 2026). Residency only counts toward CE (Article XIV.G).
- **Licensure by credentials (A.C.A. 17-82-308; Board Rules Article IX)** (el becerisi: evet): Only for dentists already licensed in another US state/territory/DC and in practice (resume, two letters from dentists in the other state, peer-review letters, last two years of CE, CPR card, DEA/NPDB clearance, 'proof of successfully passing a clinical examination for licensure' sent directly by the testing agency, 'signed letter of intention to practice in this State within one year of receiving his or her license', jurisprudence exam at 75% and a 'personal interview' before the Board at a time and place it designates). Fee $1,000. Not a route for a new graduate and still rests on a prior hands-on clinical exam; the intent-to-practise letter conflicts with living abroad.
- **Foreign-trained dentist provisional-licence pilot (A.C.A. 17-82-304(b)(2))** (el becerisi: evet): Historical only: required graduation from a North American dental college, a Board-approved exam, being 'a resident of the State of Arkansas and the United States and ... in compliance with federal laws of immigration', and one year under a provisional licence. The statute states the pilot 'will expire at the close of the 2005 regular session'. Not applicable to a CODA-programme graduate, who falls under 17-82-304(b)(1).

## Teori-only açıklaması
Board policy quoted on the ADA Arkansas Licensure page (updated 7 October 2026, citing the Board's 'Dental Licensure by Examination' web site): 'For licensure by examination, clinical examination scores are good for 5 years from the date of successful passage. A clinical patient based component of the examination must be administered by the testing agency and successfully completed by the candidate in order to be accepted. Effective January 1, 2020, the Arkansas State Board of Dental Examiners will only accept clinical dental examinations that consist of at least the following components: Restorative clinical examination section, manikin prosthetic section, manikin endodontic section with posterior and interior access, treatment planning, and periodontal section.' Board Rules Article VIII item 5: 'Satisfactory completion of a clinical examination, which will be administered by the Arkansas State Board of Dental Examiners or any regional testing agency whose examination is accepted by the Arkansas State Board of Dental Examiners for the initial licensure of a dentist. Satisfactory completion shall mean a minimum score of 75% on each segment of the examination.' Accepted agencies listed: 'American Board of Dental Examiners (ADEX, CDCA-WREB-CITA), CRDTS+SRTA'. There is no DLOSCE, OSCE-only, residency or portfolio alternative; a live-patient plus manikin hands-on exam is mandatory.

## DLOSCE notları
Arkansas does not accept the DLOSCE in full or partial fulfilment of its clinical examination requirement: it is absent from the DLOSCE 2026 Candidate Guide list (Alaska*, Arizona, Colorado, Indiana, Iowa*, Kentucky, Oregon, Washington), and the Board's rules make no reference to DLOSCE/OSCE. Arkansas instead requires a 'clinical patient based component' administered by the testing agency plus manikin prosthetic and endodontic sections, treatment planning and periodontal sections. Consequently neither earlier standalone DLOSCE scores nor the DLOSCE component reported from the ADEX-integrated series (post-Aug 2026) would suffice; the DLOSCE only matters in Arkansas as one reported component of a full ADEX series that also includes the hands-on sections. Note also that Arkansas's stated 'patient based component' requirement implies that an all-manikin (CompeDont) ADEX restorative option may not be accepted; candidates should confirm with the Board which ADEX configuration it accepts (unverified).

## Yüz yüze adımlar
(1) Hands-on clinical examination (ADEX/CRDTS/SRTA with a patient-based restorative section plus manikin sections) must be taken in person at a US testing site; the live-patient component also means the candidate must bring/arrange a patient. (2) Arkansas Jurisprudence Examination: Board Rules say 'Applicant must pass the Arkansas Jurisprudence exam with a score of 75% as administered by the Board'; the delivery format (online vs. at the Board office in Little Rock) is UNVERIFIED because the Board web pages were unreadable. For licensure-by-credentials applicants the rules explicitly require the applicant to 'present himself or herself before the Board at a time and place designated by the Board for a jurisprudence examination and a personal interview' (in person). (3) Criminal background check (Arkansas State Police/FBI; fee $38.50 per the fee rule); the fingerprinting method (cards mailed from abroad vs. in-state) is UNVERIFIED. (4) Board forms: the Board states handwritten applications are not accepted; whether notarisation is required is unverified. (5) DEA clearance/NPDB inquiry are initiated by the Board office. (6) CPR Healthcare Provider card. Most paperwork could in principle be assembled during a single US visit around the exam dates, but the jurisprudence exam/interview format and fingerprinting logistics need confirmation with the Board.

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
Age 21 and good moral character; graduation from a CODA-accredited DDS/DMD programme (Yeditepe post-1 Feb 2024 qualifies under 17-82-304(b)(1)); INBDE results sent directly from JCNDE (rule text still says 'minimum of 75% on each segment' of the National Board, written for the old NBDE); clinical exam passed within 5 years (ADEX, CRDTS or SRTA, with patient-based restorative component); Arkansas Jurisprudence Examination at 75%; current Healthcare Provider level BLS/CPR certificate; criminal background check ($38.50) with Act 990 of 2019 pre-licensure petition/waiver process for convictions; completion of Board forms (no handwritten applications); fee $150 for dental licence by examination (rule 007.33.22-020, effective 13 Jan 2023). No English-language test, no liability-insurance requirement found for initial exam-route licensure. For new licensees CE obligations begin 1 January of the calendar year following licensure. Timelines: licence issued after Board review of complete file; exam seat and score reporting timelines depend on the regional agency (ADEX scores valid 5 years).

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Biennial: every dentist must renew on 1 January of even-numbered years (A.C.A. 17-82-310); failure to renew within 30 days after 1 January results in forfeiture (17-82-311). CE reporting periods are the same two calendar years.
- **renewal_fee:** $300 dentist renewal (Rule 007.33.22-020, effective 13 Jan 2023); reinstatement after forfeiture $200 plus renewal fee (rule) and, under 17-82-312(a), all accrued renewal fees plus a $200 penalty.
- **ce_hours:** 50 CEUs per two-year reporting period (Board Rules Article XIV.D.1), including at least 1 hour on infection control; 1 CEU = 25 minutes minimum (actually '(1) continuing education hour equals a minimum of twenty-five (25) minutes' per the rule text); no carry-over of excess hours; records kept 3 years; Board audits each cycle. New licensees start CE on 1 January of the year after licensure (pro-rated).
- **ce_online_ok:** Yes, apparently in full: Article XIV.E states 'Home study courses which include a post-test and the issuance of a certificate of completion will be accepted' and sets no cap on home-study/self-study hours. Only courses 'directly related to provision of services and patient care' count (practice-management courses acceptable; money management/investments/organisational meetings excluded). Teaching in CODA programmes, publishing and residencies also earn credit. No live/in-person component is specified, other than the CPR card below.
- **cpr_required:** Yes: Article XIV.I requires every licensed dentist to 'take a Healthcare Provider level of cardiopulmonary resuscitation course and submit a photocopy of the cardiopulmonary resuscitation card or certificate with the renewal form'. Healthcare-provider-level BLS normally involves a hands-on skills session; whether the Board accepts online-only certificates is unverified.
- **practice_hours_required:** No minimum hours, BUT A.C.A. 17-82-314: 'All licenses issued to dentists and dental hygienists are automatically forfeited if the licensee ceases to practice his or her profession either in the State of Arkansas or elsewhere for a period of two (2) years. (b) The licensee is not entitled to resume the practice of his or her profession in the State of Arkansas until he or she is reexamined and licensed by the Arkansas State Board of Dental Examiners. However, the examination shall consist only of clinical work.' Practising abroad ('elsewhere') appears to count as practising, so the licence can survive only if the holder keeps practising dentistry somewhere; two years of no practice anywhere forfeits it.
- **address_or_residency_required:** No. A.C.A. 17-82-313: a licensed dentist 'who becomes a nonresident of the State of Arkansas may continue in effect his or her license to practice in the State of Arkansas by paying the renewal fee required of resident dentists'. A mailing address for renewal notices is needed (Board mails renewal forms in December of odd-numbered years).
- **insurance_required:** No mandatory professional liability insurance for renewal found in the statute/rules summarised by the ADA. (A copy of a malpractice policy appears as a document in one Board application context in the rules excerpt, but not as a renewal condition - unverified context.)
- **inactive_status:** No dentist inactive, retired or non-practising licence category was found in A.C.A. 17-82-310 to 17-82-314, in Board Rules Article XIV, or in the Board fee schedule (which lists no inactive fee). Article XIV.C refers to 'regaining an active status license' (requiring one reporting period of CE earned in the immediately preceding two years), which suggests a non-active status may exist administratively, but its terms are UNVERIFIED. Even if one exists, 17-82-314's two-year non-practice forfeiture would still apply on its face.
- **reactivation:** After forfeiture for non-renewal: within the same year, reapply, pay all accrued renewal fees plus $200 penalty and furnish proof of ethical conduct (17-82-312(a)); after two years from the date renewal was due, the same plus 'undergoing reexamination and passing the examination required by the regulations of the board. The re-examination shall consist of clinical skills only' (17-82-312(b)). After ceasing practice anywhere for two years: automatic forfeiture and clinical re-examination (17-82-314). To regain active status the licensee must also show 50 CEUs earned within the immediately preceding two years (Article XIV.C). Reinstatement fee $200 plus renewal fee; reinstatement application requires licence verifications from every state of licensure.
- **remote_maintainability:** hard
- **details:** Keeping an Arkansas licence active from abroad is possible only if the dentist keeps practising dentistry somewhere, because A.C.A. 17-82-314 automatically forfeits the licence after two years of not practising 'either in the State of Arkansas or elsewhere', and reinstatement after that or after a two-year lapse requires a hands-on clinical re-examination. The routine parts are easy: biennial $300 renewal, 50 CEUs that can apparently be entirely home-study, no residency requirement (17-82-313), no insurance mandate. Awkward parts: a Healthcare-Provider CPR card each renewal, Board-mailed renewal forms, no identified inactive/retired category, and the absence of any way to park the licence while not practising. For a candidate living abroad and not practising, the licence would lapse within two years with no theory-only way back.
