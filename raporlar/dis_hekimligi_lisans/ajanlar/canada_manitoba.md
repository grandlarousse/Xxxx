# Manitoba (Canada) — ajan bulgusu

Düzenleyici: Manitoba Dental Association (MDA) – statutory regulator under The Dental Association Act, C.C.S.M. c. D30 (Registrar's office, 202-1735 Corydon Ave, Winnipeg) — https://www.manitobadentist.ca/dental-professionals/dentists/registration-and-licencing

**Teori-only rota:** yes · **Göçmenlik engeli:** high · **Uzaktan sürdürme:** hard · **Güven:** high

## Sınav yolları

- **General Practitioner class via NDEB Certification (Virtual OSCE) + MDA jurisprudence & ethics programme (standard route for graduates of CDAC/CODA-accredited programmes)** (el becerisi: hayır): MDA GP package (Graduate of Accredited Dentist Program): '1) An accredited Dental program 2) The National Dental Examining Board (NDEB) exam 3) A Certification in Resuscitation Program (CPR) 4) An approved Jurisprudence and Ethics program 5) An approved MDA application'. Bylaw V(1)(a)(ii): applicant 'has successfully met a minimum test equated passing score on both the NDEB written and objective structured clinical examinations'. NDEB now issues the certificate 'upon successful completion of the Virtual OSCE, and after proof of graduation' - the Virtual OSCE is a 200-question computer exam (50 MCQ + 150 case-based) at Prometric centres in Canada and the US (May/November); fee CAD 1,750 + 450 application. No manikin/patient exam. The only hands-on element is the CPR certificate (Manitoba Health-approved programme with a hands-on component), which is not a dental clinical exam. BLOCKER for this candidate: Bylaw IV(1)(b) requires 'evidence satisfactory to the Registrar of legal entitlement to work in Canada' (citizenship, PR or IRCC work permit).
- **Out-of-province licensed dentist (labour mobility, Canada only)** (el becerisi: hayır): Separate 'Registration Package for Out of Province Licensed Dentists (Canada only)'; the application form allows 'applying using labour mobility legislation' without an NDEB certificate. Not available to a new graduate who holds no Canadian provincial licence.
- **Academic class (full-time University of Manitoba Faculty of Dentistry appointment)** (el becerisi: hayır): Bylaw V(3)/III(4): full-time faculty appointment required; may practise only under supervision until an NDEB certificate is obtained and ceases automatically if no NDEB certificate within five years. Requires a job (and therefore a work permit); not a realistic route for the candidate.
- **Student class (hospital-based internship or accredited specialty programme at U of Manitoba)** (el becerisi: hayır): Bylaw V(4): requires enrolment/internship agreement; supervised, no fees collected; ends when programme ends. A residency is NOT a substitute for the NDEB certificate; it is a separate restricted class. Requires study/work authorization.
- **Non-Practising class (registration without licence to practise)** (el becerisi: hayır): Bylaw V(8): applicant must be 'is or is eligible to be registered in the General Practitioner or Dental Specialist class' and sign a declaration not to practise, delegate or supervise in Manitoba. Because eligibility for the GP class incorporates Section IV (including IV(1)(b) legal entitlement to work in Canada), this class is also closed to a visitor without status. Fee $100 initial / $100 annual.
- **ADEX / regional hands-on exams, DLOSCE, PGY-1 residency** (el becerisi: evet): Not recognised at all. The Act s.14(1)(b) lets the board require examinations and the bylaw recognises only the NDEB certificate (or an MDA GP registration before Feb 28, 2014). US regional/ADEX, DLOSCE and PGY-1 completion play no role in Manitoba licensure.

## Teori-only açıklaması
From an examination standpoint Manitoba is theory/computer-only: The Dental Association Act s.14(1) - 'A person is qualified to be registered as a dentist if the person (a) is a graduate of a dental education program approved by the board; (b) passes any examinations that the board may require; ... (e) meets any other requirements set out in the by-laws.' The MDA Bylaw for Registration and Licensure of Dentists (10 April 2025, effective 5 Sept 2025) Section V(1)(a) requires for the General Practitioner class that the applicant 'ii. has successfully met a minimum test equated passing score on both the NDEB written and objective structured clinical examinations since those examinations were approved by the MDA', and Section IV(1)(e) 'evidence satisfactory to the Registrar of successful completion of a course in Canadian jurisprudence and ethics (SCHEDULE B)'. The MDA's own GP page lists Step 2 as 'Virtual Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE)' and NDEB states 'An NDEB certificate is issued upon successful completion of the Virtual OSCE, and after proof of graduation is provided' - a 200-question computer-based examination at Prometric centres. There is no manikin/patient examination, no residency and no Manitoba-specific written exam (jurisprudence is a course, not an in-person exam). The only hands-on item is CPR: 'only those with a hands-on component are valid and acceptable' (Manitoba Health-approved providers). CAVEAT: although the exam route is theory-only, the candidate cannot actually be registered because Bylaw IV(1)(b) demands 'evidence satisfactory to the Registrar of legal entitlement to work in Canada' for ALL classes including Non-Practising.

## DLOSCE notları
Not applicable. Manitoba recognises only NDEB certification (Virtual OSCE); the DLOSCE, ADEX/CDCA-WREB-CITA, CRDTS, SRTA and other US exams are neither required nor accepted. Note that NDEB itself has replaced the former Written Examination + OSCE with a single computer-based Virtual OSCE (the MDA bylaw text still refers to 'NDEB written and objective structured clinical examinations' but the MDA GP page and NDEB confirm the Virtual OSCE now leads to certification).

PGY-1 kabul: False

- **details:** Bylaw Section IV(1) (applies to ALL registrant classes, including Non-Practising): 'An applicant is eligible to apply for registration and initial licensure if the applicant submits to the Registrar: ... (b) evidence satisfactory to the Registrar of legal entitlement to work in Canada; (c) evidence satisfactory to the Registrar of identity and current legal name'. Application form, Personal section: 'If you are a Canadian citizen, please provide a certified copy of your birth certificate, citizenship card or Canadian passport. If you are not a Canadian citizen, please check one of the boxes below: [ ] Permanent Resident - Please provide a certified copy of your Confirmation of Permanent Residence (CoPR), PR card, or Landing Paper. [ ] Work Permit - Please provide a certified copy of your work permit issued by the Immigration, Refugees & Citizenship Canada which permits you to work in Canada'. Instructional Guide s.7: 'The section on citizenship and identification is required to confirm your entitlement to work in Canada... Applicants who are neither Canadian citizens nor permanent residents will need to submit a certified copy of your work permit issued by the Immigration, Refugees & Citizenship Canada which permits the applicant to work in Canada.' GP checklist: 'Certified copy of citizenship, permanent residency or work permit'. The guide's document matrix lists 'Authorization to work or study' as a certified-copy requirement for every category. The Registrar's power to 'alter or waive' requirements is expressly limited to paragraphs IV(1)(e)-(g) (jurisprudence, CPR, certificates of standing) for the charitable/educator/course-participant classes - there is no waiver authority for IV(1)(b). Non-Practising class applicants must be 'eligible to be registered in the General Practitioner ... class' (V(8)(a)(i)), i.e. must also satisfy IV(1)(b). A visitor on a tourist/business visa therefore cannot be registered in any class. No residency in Manitoba is required: the form takes a 'Practice address' and 'Home address' (Province/Postal Code fields) and the guide says members without a practice address may give a home address; XVII requires notifying address changes within 15 days.

## Yüz yüze adımlar
(1) Notarised application: the Dentist Initial Registration Application Form must be signed and 'Taken and declared before me in the District, Province, or State of ...' by 'A Commissioner for Oaths, Notary Public, Lawyer' (Guide s.22: 'A registration and initial licence cannot be reviewed unless it is an original signed by the applicant with a notarized witnessed'). The wording ('Province, or State') allows a foreign notary, so this can be done abroad. (2) Certified copies: every supporting document must be a copy certified by 'a court, a government, lawyer or notary public'; 'The MDA will not accept copies sworn by you, the applicant'; alternatively 'bring original documentation (in English only) to the MDA' in Winnipeg. Certified translations required for non-English documents. Can be done abroad. (3) No fingerprinting or police certificate: the Act (s.16) and form rely on self-declared criminal/regulatory history; Certificates of Standing are sent directly by other regulators. (4) NDEB Virtual OSCE: sat at 'Prometric test centres in Canada and the US' in May and November - requires travel to Canada or the US (visitor visa acceptable for an exam). (5) Jurisprudence & ethics: a course from Schedule B (McGill, UBC, U Manitoba, U Toronto, Western; or 'Programmes provided as a requirement for licensure in Manitoba, Ontario') - the Ontario RCDSO course is an online course; the MDA says applicants who have not completed a listed programme should 'contact the Registrar'. No in-person MDA jurisprudence exam. (6) CPR: must be from a Manitoba Health-approved provider (Schedule C: St. John Ambulance, Red Cross, Heart & Stroke Manitoba, etc.) and 'only those with a hands-on component are valid and acceptable' - practically an in-person course in Manitoba, needed at registration and valid at every renewal. (7) No interview. (8) Two signed letters of reference. (9) Appeals: appellant 'may appear before the appeal committee' (optional).

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
Jurisprudence & ethics: MDA-approved Canadian programme (Bylaw IV(1)(e), Schedule B) - a course, letter from programme director required; no separate MDA exam. CPR: valid certificate from Manitoba Health-approved programme with hands-on component (IV(1)(f), Schedule C). English: 'demonstrates ability to speak and write in English with reasonable fluency' (IV(1)(i)); MDA policy: 'There are no additional registration requirements or documentation required on English language proficiency', benchmarks given only as a guide (CLB 7, TOEFL 100 iBT, IELTS 6.5). Liability insurance: GP applicants must carry 'professional liability insurance in an amount of not less than $3,000,000.00 per claim under the malpractice plan administered by Canadian Dental Services Plans Inc. (CDSPI)' (V(1)(c)); proof required with application. Continuity: no three-year period without regular practice since obtaining the NDEB certificate (V(1)(b), waived if certificate is recent, V(1)(e)). Background: self-declarations on health, regulatory conduct, criminal convictions, abuse registries; Certificate of Standing from every other health regulator; Consent to Release forms (regulatory, NDEB, health); Declaration of Commitment to Ethical Practice; passport photo; certified copy of degree (or Dean's letter) and NDEB certificate; two reference letters; Manitoba Health practitioner registration form. Registrar may defer a decision up to one year; refusals appealable to Board within 30 days, hearing within 90 days. Timelines: MDA publishes no processing time; NDEB says certification takes 6-18 months from eligibility, Virtual OSCE results within 8 weeks; certificate mailed and name added to National Register. Fees must accompany the application (licence fee refunded if refused; $850 application fee non-refundable).

## İlk ücretler
MDA (Schedule A 2025-2026, passed 18 Sept 2025, all CAD, non-refundable except licence fee on refusal): registration application fee $850; initial licence - $3,950 if registered before 1 September, $2,950 if registered in the year of graduation or on/after 1 September, $2,950 academic class, $650 student class, $100 non-practising class; dentist certificate of standing $175 + GST, letter of standing $100 + GST. NDEB: application fee $450 (one-time) + Virtual OSCE $1,750; proof of certification $30/letter. Plus: CDSPI malpractice premium ($3M/claim), jurisprudence & ethics course fee, CPR course fee, notary/certified-copy and translation costs. Realistic first-year total for an active GP licence: roughly CAD 6,000-7,500 before insurance.

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Annual. 'Every licence shall expire on February 28th of each year' (Bylaw XI(1)); renewal package must be submitted on or before Feb 28 (X(1)). Late fee $20/day after 29 Feb to max $600; licence suspended 1 March if unpaid and cancelled 30 March (Schedule A) / within 30 days (XI(6)).
- **renewal_fee:** General practitioner/dental specialist $3,950 (or two-payment option $2,025 on 28 Feb + $2,025 on 15 June = $4,050); academic $2,950; student $650; non-practising $100; 30-day licence to complete outstanding CE $600; change-of-status application $300; reinstatement application $300 (Schedule A 2025-2026).
- **ce_hours:** 90 credit hours per three-year cycle; cycle begins March 1 of the year after registration; maximum 54 hours in one subject area; non-clinical topics max 30/cycle; excess hours cannot be carried forward; credits must be submitted within 60 days of the activity (Bylaw for Continuing Education of Dentists, ratified 15 Dec 2016). Licence not renewed until shortfall is made up (s. IX).
- **ce_online_ok:** Not entirely by asynchronous self-study: 'Self Study Programmes with Independent Assessment' are capped at 30 credit hours per cycle and 'Self Study Programmes without Independent Assessment' at 15 (max 1 credit per programme), so at least 45 of the 90 hours must come from other categories such as 'Seminars, Lectures and Participation Activities' (max 6/day; the MDA CE portal labels this 'Synchronous Seminars, Lectures and Participation Activities', suggesting live webinars count), lecturing, publications, hospital rounds or approved study clubs. Approved sponsors include ADA CERP/AGD-approved providers and any provincial/national dental association, so live online courses from abroad are feasible, but 100% recorded/self-study CE is not. Exemptions/extensions are discretionary (grounds include 'out of Canada between six and twelve months').
- **cpr_required:** Yes, at every renewal: X(1)(d) 'evidence satisfactory to the Registrar of a valid certificate from an appropriate resuscitation programme (SCHEDULE C)'. MDA: programmes must be approved by Manitoba Health and 'only those with a hands-on component are valid and acceptable'. Schedule C lists Manitoba providers (St. John Ambulance, Canadian Red Cross, Heart & Stroke Foundation of Manitoba, Lifesaving Society, Criti Care, etc.). CPR expiry is tracked in the CE account and blocks renewal if lapsed.
- **practice_hours_required:** No minimum hours to renew an active licence. However: a GP applicant must show no three-year period without 'continuous and regular' practice since NDEB certification (V(1)(b)); a Non-Practising member may return to the GP class only if 'registered in the requested class in any of the previous three years' or in 'regular and continuous practice in an equivalent class in the previous three years' in a CDAC-accredited jurisdiction (XIII(2)); and anyone 'not practising dentistry for three or more years' must apply as a new registrant and 'undergo a competency evaluation approved by the Board' at their own cost (XV(8)-(9)).
- **address_or_residency_required:** No Manitoba residency requirement. Practice address is published on the public registry; members without a practice location may list a home address. Any change of name, address or telephone must be reported within 15 days (XVII). Renewal forms are mailed/e-mailed; all communications in English.
- **insurance_required:** Yes. GP and specialist classes: not less than $3,000,000 per claim under the CDSPI malpractice plan (V(1)(c), V(2)(e)); every renewal requires 'evidence satisfactory to the Registrar of professional liability insurance in form and amount appropriate to the Registrar' (X(1)(c)). Members ceasing practice complete a 'Request for Non-Practising Status of Malpractice Insurance' (CDSPI) via the Registrar.
- **inactive_status:** Non-Practising class (Bylaw I(1)(h), V(8)): fee $100 initial and $100 per year; must be 'is or is eligible to be registered in the General Practitioner or Dental Specialist class' (so all Section IV requirements, including legal entitlement to work in Canada, still apply) and sign a declaration not to practise, delegate or supervise in Manitoba and must 'include a reference to non-practising status in any communication involving the practice of dentistry'. No maximum duration stated. Change to this class costs $300 (change-of-status fee) via the Change of Status form, which again asks for citizenship/PR/work-permit proof. CE: the CE bylaw contains no explicit exemption for non-practising members; return to active status requires 'evidence satisfactory to the Registrar he or she has maintained the necessary continuing competency in the class requested' (XII(1)(c)). Alternatively a member may simply cancel (resign) at no cost and apply for reinstatement later.
- **reactivation:** Within 3 years of resignation/non-practising: Dentist Change of Status or Reinstatement form + $300 fee + certificates of standing/consents + review of CE record and CPR status + licence fee (XV(1), MDA reinstatement policy). After not practising for three or more years (or resigning with conditions, etc.): must 'complete the requirements as a new registration application' and 'shall undergo a competency evaluation approved by the Board. The costs of any competency evaluation are the sole responsibility of the applicant' (XV(8)-(9)); MDA warns 'These costs can be substantial' and the Board may impose practice conditions (XV(10)). The CE bylaw's 'Alternative Competency Assessment' refers to the NDEB Equivalency Process assessments (which include the hands-on NDECC). Default on fees blocks reinstatement.
- **remote_maintainability:** hard
- **details:** Even setting aside the entry barrier, keeping a Manitoba licence from abroad is onerous: active GP renewal costs $3,950/yr and needs a hands-on, Manitoba Health-approved CPR certificate valid every 28 February plus CDSPI malpractice cover; the cheap ($100/yr) Non-Practising class cannot be converted back to active once three years have passed without regular practice in a CDAC-accredited jurisdiction, after which a Board-approved competency evaluation is required; CE cannot be met entirely by self-study. Because Non-Practising registrants must still be 'eligible to be registered' in the GP class, the legal-entitlement-to-work requirement (IV(1)(b)) also governs that class, and the Change-of-Status form re-asks for citizenship/PR/work-permit documents.
