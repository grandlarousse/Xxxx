# Arizona (USA) — ajan bulgusu

Düzenleyici: Arizona State Board of Dental Examiners — https://dentalboard.az.gov

**Teori-only rota:** partial · **Göçmenlik engeli:** medium · **Uzaktan sürdürme:** moderate · **Güven:** medium

## Sınav yolları

- **Licensure by examination (regional hands-on clinical exam: ADEX / CRDTS / SRTA)** (el becerisi: evet): A.R.S. 32-1233: applicant must have passed (1) the written national dental board examinations (INBDE), (2) 'a clinical examination administered by a state or regional testing agency in the United States within five years preceding filing the application', and (3) the Arizona dental jurisprudence examination. R4-11-301(A)(4)(a): certificate/scorecard sent directly to the Board from the testing agency. ADA Licensure-by-State page (updated 7 Oct 2026) lists the accepted agencies as 'American Board of Dental Examiners (ADEX, CDCA-WREB-CITA), CRDTS+SRTA'. ADEX Dental Examination = computer-based DLOSCE component (required from 1 Jun 2026) + manikin clinical parts (periodontal, endodontic, prosthodontic, anterior/posterior restorative). Plus: DDS/DMD from a 'recognized dental school' (32-1232(A)), fingerprint clearance card (32-1232(B)), CPR certificate, photo, transcript, $300 jurisprudence fee, $110 prorated initial licence fee.
- **DLOSCE-only (computer-based OSCE) in full fulfilment of the clinical exam** (el becerisi: hayır): JCNDE 2026 DLOSCE Candidate Guide (updated 4/31/2026) lists 'Arizona' WITHOUT the asterisk that marks partial fulfilment ('*Partial fulfillment ... in conjunction with a hands-on assessment' applies only to Alaska and Iowa), i.e. Arizona accepted the DLOSCE alone. This became possible after SB1370 (2022) amended 32-1233 to delete the words 'western regional examining board examination' and 'psychomotor', leaving the exam-agnostic wording 'a clinical examination administered by a state or regional testing agency'. HOWEVER: standalone DLOSCE administration ended (no new candidates after 1 Aug 2026; last retake window closed 26 Sep 2026; all standalone administrations ceased after 9 Oct 2026), so a NEW candidate can now only take the DLOSCE as a component of the ADEX series. The Board's own website (which would state its current policy) could not be read (HTTP 403 to automated readers) and the ADA Oct 2026 summary lists only ADEX/CRDTS/SRTA. A candidate already holding a standalone DLOSCE pass could still apply within 5 years of the exam date (32-1233(2)).
- **Licensure by credential (A.R.S. 32-1240; R4-11-202)** (el becerisi: evet): For dentists already licensed in another US state/territory/DC: affidavit of at least 5,000 hours of dental practice in the 5 years before applying, CE-compliance affidavit, evidence of having passed a clinical exam at ANY time (R4-11-201(A), 'notwithstanding each respective statute's timing stipulation'), jurisprudence exam, fee $2,000 (50% off for underserved-area service commitment). Not available to a new graduate without a US licence, and a clinical examination must still have been passed at some point.
- **Universal licence recognition (A.R.S. 32-4302)** (el becerisi: evet): Only for a person who 'establishes residence in this state' (or a military spouse) and who has been licensed in another state for at least one year; the other state's exam requirements must have been met. Not applicable to a non-resident new graduate.
- **PGY-1 / residency in lieu of clinical exam** (el becerisi: hayır): NOT available. A.R.S. 32-1233 contains no residency or portfolio alternative; the clinical examination is mandatory ('An applicant for licensure shall have passed all of the following').
- **Restricted permit (A.R.S. 32-1237) / dental consultant licence (32-1234)** (el becerisi: hayır): Restricted permit requires an existing licence from another US state, 3 years of practice/residency and a contract with a recognised charitable clinic; dental consultant licence requires 25 years of US licensure. Neither is a route for a new graduate.

## Teori-only açıklaması
Statute (A.R.S. 32-1233, current text on azleg.gov): 'An applicant for licensure shall have passed all of the following: 1. The written national dental board examinations. 2. A clinical examination administered by a state or regional testing agency in the United States within five years preceding filing the application. 3. The Arizona dental jurisprudence examination.' SB1370 (2022) struck the former words 'The western regional examining board examination or a psychomotor clinical examination administered by another state or regional testing agency', so the statute no longer requires a psychomotor (hands-on) exam, and the JCNDE 2026 DLOSCE Candidate Guide lists Arizona among states accepting the DLOSCE 'in full or partial fulfillment of clinical examination requirements' with no partial-fulfilment asterisk (the asterisk, meaning 'in conjunction with a hands-on assessment', is applied only to Alaska and Iowa). So a theory-only route (INBDE + DLOSCE + online jurisprudence exam) existed in Arizona. It is now only 'partial' because: (a) per ADEX/ADA (14 Apr 2026 agreement) 'The DLOSCE will no longer be offered or administered as a standalone examination to new DLOSCE candidates, except in conjunction with the ADEX Dental Examination, after August 1, 2026. All standalone administrations of the DLOSCE will cease after October 9, 2026'; a new candidate must therefore register for the ADEX series, whose remaining components are manikin-based; (b) the ADA's Arizona summary (updated 7 Oct 2026) lists only ADEX, CRDTS and SRTA; (c) the Board's website, where any policy accepting a DLOSCE-component-only ADEX result would appear, could not be read. A candidate who passed the standalone DLOSCE on or before 26 Sep 2026 could still apply within five years of that exam date, subject to Board confirmation.

## DLOSCE notları
The statute and rules never name the DLOSCE; acceptance rests on the exam-agnostic wording of 32-1233(2) after SB1370 (2022) and on Board policy. JCNDE 2026 Candidate Guide (updated 4/31/2026): 'The DLOSCE is now accepted in the following states in full or partial fulfillment of clinical examination requirements for licensure ... Alaska* Arizona Colorado Indiana Iowa* Kentucky Oregon Washington ... *Partial fulfillment ... in conjunction with a hands-on assessment' (Arizona has no asterisk = full fulfilment). The same guide says the 2026 retake-only window ran 21 Apr-26 Sep 2026 and that 'Scheduling for the updated ADEX Dental Examination will open in June 2026'. ADEX 2026 Dental Computer-Based Candidate Manual: 'Dental Licensure Objective Structured Clinical Examination (DLOSCE) is a required component of the ADEX Dental Examination effective 6/1/2026'; all ADEX series components must be passed within 18 months. Nothing from Arizona could be found stating whether (i) a standalone DLOSCE score from before Oct 2026 is still accepted (statutorily it should be, within 5 years of the exam), or (ii) a candidate who passes only the DLOSCE portion of the ADEX series (without the manikin parts) would be licensed; the ADA Oct 2026 Arizona page lists only ADEX/CRDTS/SRTA, which suggests the Board now expects the full ADEX scorecard. Treat the DLOSCE-only route as unconfirmed and ask the Board in writing.

- **itin_or_no_ssn_alternative:** None found in statute. A.R.S. 25-320(P): 'Each licensing board or agency that issues professional, recreational or occupational licenses or certificates shall record on the application the social security number of the applicant and shall enter this information in its database in order to aid the department of economic security in locating parents or their assets or to enforce child support orders.' Bills to let agencies accept a federal tax identification number instead (HB2777 of 2023, proposed new A.R.S. 41-1080.02; HB2761 of 2024; HB2496 of 2025) were introduced, but 41-1080.02 does not exist in the current Arizona Revised Statutes (azleg.gov returns 404), so no alternative is codified. Whether the Dental Board accepts an 'affidavit of no SSN' (as some other Arizona boards do, e.g. the Nursing Board) could not be verified because the Board's application instructions are not readable by automated tools; this must be confirmed with the Board.

- **barrier_level:** medium
- **details:** No citizenship, permanent residence, residency or work authorisation requirement appears in A.R.S. Title 32 ch. 11 or A.A.C. Title 4 ch. 11. BUT Arizona has a state-wide lawful-presence law for licences, A.R.S. 41-1080(A): 'an agency ... shall not issue a license to an individual if the individual does not provide documentation of citizenship or alien status by presenting any of the following documents ... indicating that the individual's presence in the United States is authorized under federal law: 1. An Arizona driver license ... 5. A United States passport. 6. A foreign passport with a United States visa. 7. An I-94 form with a photograph. 8. A ... employment authorization document ...' etc. The Board implements this with its 'Arizona Statement of Citizenship and Alien Status' form (its instructions say the form is 'required for all applicants regardless of how you intend to apply', with List A/List B evidence; signed under penalty of perjury). A Turkish citizen holding a B-1/B-2 visitor visa (Turkey is not in the Visa Waiver Program) can satisfy item 6/7 with the passport+visa and I-94; no work authorisation is needed. Federal law agrees: 8 U.S.C. 1621(a)(2) makes 'a nonimmigrant' eligible for a state professional licence, and 1621(c)(2)(C) excludes from the public-benefit restriction 'the issuance of a professional license to, or the renewal of a professional license by, a foreign national not physically present in the United States'. 41-1080(B)(1) exempts 'a citizen of a foreign country or ... [one who] resides in a foreign country' only if 'the benefits that are related to the license do not require the individual to be present in the United States' - probably not satisfied for a licence to practise dentistry in Arizona, though the Board's form reportedly describes an exemption for foreign citizens/residents abroad (unverified). Renewal: 41-1080(C)-(D) waive re-documentation only for citizens/non-expiring work authorisation and require re-documentation when a limited work authorisation has expired; a visitor-visa holder may be asked to show current visa/I-94 at each triennial renewal (ambiguous). SSN: mandatory on the application by A.R.S. 25-320(P) with no codified ITIN/affidavit alternative (see above). Address: A.R.S. 32-1236(E) requires only 'a primary mailing address' and 'the address for each place of practice' - no Arizona address or residency is required (an Arizona residence is required only for the separate universal-recognition route, 32-4302). Overall: no citizenship/PR/work-permit barrier; lawful-presence check satisfiable with visa+I-94; the open SSN question makes the barrier 'medium'.

## Yüz yüze adımlar
(1) Fingerprint clearance card (A.R.S. 32-1232(B): 'Each candidate shall also obtain a valid fingerprint clearance card issued pursuant to section 41-1758.03'), issued by Arizona DPS. DPS process (azdps.gov): apply online on the Public Services Portal, pay $67 (+ vendor fee), then EITHER be printed electronically through DPS's vendor Fieldprint (US locations only) OR submit a paper FD-258 fingerprint card: 'you will need to arrange for your fingerprints to be taken by a third-party fingerprinting service or by a local law enforcement office ... Paper card fingerprints will not be accepted unless they are accompanied by the correct fingerprint affidavit [Non-IVP Fingerprint Verification Affidavit, completed by the technician] and properly sealed'. DPS itself does not fingerprint the public. In principle the paper route can be done outside Arizona; whether prints taken by a foreign police service are accepted is not stated, so the safest plan is to be printed during a US visit (Fieldprint or a US vendor) - allow several weeks for the card. (2) Arizona jurisprudence examination: per the Board's website (readable only via search snippets) it is 50 multiple-choice questions 'given online' and 'considered to be an open book test'; statutory fee $300 (32-1232(B)). Can be done from abroad. (3) Clinical examination (ADEX/CRDTS/SRTA) must be taken in person at a US test site - this is the unavoidable in-person (and hands-on) step for a new candidate. (4) Application: R4-11-301(A)(1) requires 'a sworn statement of the applicant's qualifications ... on a form provided by the Board' (notarisation likely but unverifiable), a photo no more than 6 months old, official transcript and INBDE/clinical scorecards sent directly to the Board, CPR certificate copy, the Statement of Citizenship form with copies of passport/visa/I-94 (copies acceptable). The Board acknowledges receipt by email. No interview, no Board appearance. (5) Processing time (R4-11-303): administrative completeness review 30 days, substantive review 90 days, overall 120 days. Summary: one US trip is needed for the clinical exam and (ideally) fingerprinting; everything else can be completed by mail/online.

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
Degree: 'diploma conferring a degree of doctor of dental medicine or doctor of dental surgery from a recognized dental school' (32-1232(A)); R4-11-301(A)(3) official sealed transcript sent directly from the school or a verified third-party transcript provider; Board may request the diploma, name-change documents, work history and a high-school diploma (R4-11-301(B)). INBDE: official scorecard sent directly from the National Board (R4-11-301(A)(5)). Jurisprudence exam: pass with at least 75% (R4-11-301(C)); online open-book; fee $300. CPR: 'a copy showing the expiration date of the applicant's current cardiopulmonary resuscitation healthcare provider level certificate from the American Red Cross, the American Heart Association, or another certifying agency that follows the same procedures, standards, and techniques' (R4-11-301(A)(6)). Background: DPS fingerprint clearance card (state + FBI check). Other-jurisdiction licence verifications sent directly (R4-11-301(A)(7)) and NPDB self-query if ever licensed elsewhere (A)(8). Denial grounds in 32-1232(C). No English-language test, no liability-insurance requirement and no residency requirement were found in the statute or rules. Timeline: 30 + 90 days (120 days overall) under R4-11-303.

## İlk ücretler
Jurisprudence examination fee $300 (A.R.S. 32-1232(B); R4-11-403(B)(1)(a)); prorated initial dentist licence fee $110 (R4-11-403(A)(2); statute caps it at one-third of the renewal fee); DPS fingerprint clearance card $67 plus the fingerprint vendor's fee; CPR course cost; INBDE and ADEX/regional clinical exam fees paid to those agencies (not researched here). Licensure by credential (not applicable to a new graduate): $2,000. First-renewal is prorated to the licensee's next birth month.

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Triennial: 'a license expires thirty days after the licensee's birth month every third year'; renewal application and fee due 'on or before the last day of the licensee's birth month every third year' (A.R.S. 32-1236(A)). First licence is prorated to the next birth month (32-1236(C)).
- **renewal_fee:** Statutory/rule ceiling $650 (32-1236(A); R4-11-403(A)(1) 'Dentist triennial renewal fee: $650'). The Board's online-renewal page (readable only via search snippet) states the fee actually charged is $510, set by Board vote on 4 Nov 2022. Late penalty $100 if both application and fee are not received by the last day of the month after the birth month (32-1236(D); R4-11-403(B)(3)). Retired/disabled licensee renewal fee $15 (R4-11-401).
- **ce_hours:** 63 credit hours per 3-year renewal period (R4-11-1203, as amended effective 12 Sep 2022): at least 36 h clinical/dental-medical subjects; no more than 15 h practice management; at least 3 h opioid education; at least 3 h infectious diseases/infection control; at least 3 h CPR-healthcare-provider/ACLS/PALS; at least 3 h ethics or Arizona dental jurisprudence; plus 12 h botulinum toxin/dermal filler CE only if the dentist provides those. A newly licensed dentist must complete the hours 'by the end of the first full renewal period' (R4-11-1201(B)) and need not file the CE affidavit at a renewal falling within a year of initial licensure (32-1236(B)). Retired licensees (age 65+): 24 h incl. 3 h CPR (R4-11-1208).
- **ce_online_ok:** Largely yes. R4-11-1209(A)(2) counts, without any cap, seminars/lectures delivered 'by means of audio-video technology in which the Licensee is provided all ... materials and the technology permits attendees to fully participate' (live interactive webinars). R4-11-1209(A)(4)(a) counts 'a correspondence course, video, internet or similar self-study course, if the course includes an examination and the Licensee ... passes the examination', but all (A)(4) activities together are capped at 21 hours for dentists per renewal period (R4-11-1209(B)(1)). So all 63 hours can be earned remotely (42+ via live interactive webinars, up to 21 via self-study), EXCEPT that the 3-hour CPR requirement states 'Coursework may be completed online if the course requires a physical demonstration of skills' (R4-11-1203(5)), i.e. a hands-on skills check is required. 'Recognized continuing dental education' (R4-11-101) must come from a recognised dental school, national/state dental association, ADA CERP or AGD PACE provider, study club, governmental agency, commercial dental supplier, non-profit, accredited hospital, or a course approved by another US state board - foreign providers qualify only if they fall in one of those categories (e.g. ADA CERP-recognised). Documentation of attendance must be kept; random audits require proof within 35 days of a notice sent by certified mail (R4-11-1202(F)-(G)). The Board may grant a CE extension for 'residence in a foreign country' (R4-11-1202(D)).
- **cpr_required:** Yes. 'Before receiving a renewal license ... each Licensee ... shall possess a current form of one of the following: 1. A cardiopulmonary resuscitation healthcare provider level certificate from the American Red Cross, the American Heart Association, or another certifying agency; 2. Advanced cardiac life support ... completed within two years ...; or 3. Pediatric advanced life support ... within two years' (R4-11-1202(B)). The CE rule adds that online CPR coursework counts only 'if the course requires a physical demonstration of skills' (R4-11-1203(5)), so a hands-on skills session (available worldwide through AHA/ARC international training centres or equivalent agencies) is needed roughly every two years.
- **practice_hours_required:** None for renewal or to keep the licence active. (The 5,000-hours-in-5-years requirement applies only to the licensure-by-credential route, R4-11-202(B)(2).)
- **address_or_residency_required:** No Arizona address or residency. 32-1236(E): 'Each licensee must provide to the board in writing both of the following: 1. A primary mailing address. 2. The address for each place of practice.' Changes must be notified within 10 days ($50 penalty, $100 after 30 days) (32-1236(I); R4-11-403(B)(4)). A foreign mailing address is not prohibited by the statute or rules.
- **insurance_required:** No mandatory professional liability insurance requirement was found in A.R.S. Title 32 ch. 11 or A.A.C. Title 4 ch. 11.
- **inactive_status:** Arizona has NO general 'inactive' category for dentists; the only reduced statuses are 'retired' and 'disabled' (32-1236(G)-(H); A.R.S. 32-1207(B)(3)(c)). 'Retired' is defined in R4-11-101 as a dentist who 'is at least 65 years old and has totally withdrawn from the active practice of dentistry'. Retired renewal fee $15 (R4-11-401); 24 CE hours per period incl. 3 h CPR (R4-11-1208); must relinquish prescribing privileges, surrender DEA registration and attest 'not currently engaged in the practice of dentistry' (32-1236(H)); may only 'contribute services to a recognized charitable institution' (32-1236(G)). No time limit. A young graduate cannot use it, so the licence must be kept fully active (full fee + 63 h CE + CPR) or allowed to lapse.
- **reactivation:** Lapsed licence: 'An expired license may be reinstated by submitting a complete renewal application within the twenty-four-month period immediately following the expiration of the license with payment of the renewal fee and a $100 penalty ... If a person does not reinstate a license pursuant to this subsection, the person must reapply for licensure pursuant to this chapter' (32-1236(D)) - i.e. after 24 months one is a new applicant and must again satisfy 32-1233 (a clinical examination passed within the previous five years) unless eligible for licensure by credential (current licence elsewhere + 5,000 practice hours in 5 years). No refresher-course route exists. Retired-to-active: no rule text found (Board policy unknown); CE extensions are available for foreign residence (R4-11-1202(D)).
- **remote_maintainability:** moderate
- **details:** An active Arizona dental licence can in principle be kept for decades from abroad: triennial renewal online, fee ~$510-$650, 63 CE hours obtainable through live interactive webinars (uncapped) plus up to 21 hours of self-study, no practice-hour requirement, no Arizona address or residency, no insurance mandate. The awkward parts are: (a) a current CPR healthcare-provider certificate with a physical skills demonstration (roughly every 2 years) is a condition of renewal; (b) no inactive/reduced status exists for a dentist under 65, so full fee and full CE apply throughout; (c) a lapse of more than 24 months forces a full new application including a clinical exam within the last five years; (d) A.R.S. 41-1080(C)-(D) may require a visitor-visa holder to re-document authorised presence at each renewal (ambiguous); (e) CE audits require documentation within 35 days of a certified-mail notice to the mailing address on file.
