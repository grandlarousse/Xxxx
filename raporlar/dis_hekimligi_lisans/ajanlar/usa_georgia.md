# Georgia (USA) — ajan bulgusu

Düzenleyici: Georgia Board of Dentistry (GBD), 2 MLK Jr. Drive SE, 11th Floor East Tower, Atlanta GA 30334; under the Georgia Department of Community Health — https://gbd.georgia.gov

**Teori-only rota:** no · **Göçmenlik engeli:** medium · **Uzaktan sürdürme:** moderate · **Güven:** medium

## Sınav yolları

- **Licensure by examination (INBDE + board-approved regional clinical exam + Georgia jurisprudence exam)** (el becerisi: evet): Standard route for a CODA graduate (O.C.G.A. § 43-11-40; Rule 150-3-.01). Requires (1) passing INBDE (or NBDE I & II at 75+), (2) passing 'all sections with a score of 75 or higher on any clinical examination administered by the Georgia Board of Dentistry, or a testing agency designated and approved by the Board', and (3) a jurisprudence exam (75+, open-book, paper exam included in the application packet). Accepted clinical exams per ADA page (updated 7 Oct 2026): ADEX (CDCA or CITA) patient exam on/after 1/1/2015 or ADEX manikin-based exam on/after 1/1/2021; CRDTS patient exam after 6/30/2009 or CRDTS manikin exam on/after 1/1/2021; SRTA exam on/after 1/1/2021. The rule states: 'Regional examinations must include psychomotor patient-based or simulated patient-based (manikin) procedures performed as part of the assessment of clinical competencies' covering periodontics, endodontics, class II amalgam/composite, class III composite and crown preparation (clinical abilities testing), plus prosthetics, oral diagnosis and oral surgery (written or clinical). All accepted exams are hands-on (manikin or patient).
- **DLOSCE / OSCE-only route** (el becerisi: hayır): NOT available. Georgia is not among the DLOSCE-accepting states and Rule 150-3-.01 mandates psychomotor (patient or manikin) components in five areas. A standalone DLOSCE pass or an ADEX-reported DLOSCE component pass cannot substitute for the hands-on sections.
- **PGY-1 residency in lieu of clinical exam** (el becerisi: hayır): NOT available. Neither O.C.G.A. § 43-11-40 nor Rule 150-3-.01 provides any residency/postgraduate-training substitute for the clinical examination; the only references to residency in the rules concern credentials-pathway practice definitions and CE credit.
- **Provisional licensure by credentials (O.C.G.A. § 43-11-41)** (el becerisi: hayır): NOT available to a new graduate: requires five years immediately preceding application in full-time clinical practice (defined by rule, minimum hours) and/or full-time clinical faculty, plus an active licence in good standing in another state, affidavit of citizenship, CPR, background check. No new clinical exam, but the underlying out-of-state licence would itself have required one.
- **Restricted licences (faculty licence, public health licence, volunteer licence)** (el becerisi: hayır): Separate application forms exist on gbd.georgia.gov (dentist faculty licensure, $325 fee; public health licensure; volunteer dentistry). These are restricted to practice in a Georgia dental school, public-health setting or volunteer setting and presuppose employment/presence in Georgia (and therefore work authorisation); their exam requirements were not read in this research. Not a general licence and not suitable for the candidate's goal.

## Teori-only açıklaması
O.C.G.A. § 43-11-40(a)(3): 'In order to be granted a license under this Code section, all applicants must pass a clinical examination approved by the board and a jurisprudence examination on the laws of this state...'. Rule 150-3-.01(3): 'Each candidate for a license to practice dentistry must pass all sections with a score of 75 or higher on any clinical examination administered by the Georgia Board of Dentistry, or a testing agency designated and approved by the Board.' The board's accepted-exam list (ADA Georgia Laws & Rules page, updated 7 Oct 2026) is limited to ADEX (CDCA/CITA), CRDTS and SRTA patient- or manikin-based exams, and states: 'Regional examinations must include psychomotor patient-based or simulated patient-based (manikin) procedures performed as part of the assessment of clinical competencies, and shall include evaluations in the following areas: 1. periodontics, clinical abilities testing; 2. endodontics, clinical abilities testing; 3. posterior class II amalgam or posterior class II composite preparation and restoration, clinical abilities testing; 4. anterior class III composite preparation and restoration, clinical abilities testing; 5. crown preparation, clinical abilities testing; 6. prosthetics, written or clinical abilities testing; 7. oral diagnosis, written or clinical abilities testing; and 8. oral surgery, written or clinical abilities testing.' (Source: Rule 150-3-.01 as summarised by ADA). There is no DLOSCE, OSCE, portfolio or residency alternative anywhere in the statute or rules. A hands-on manikin/patient exam is therefore unavoidable.

## DLOSCE notları
Georgia does not accept the DLOSCE in any form (it is absent from the ADA/JCNDE 2026 list of accepting states and from the Georgia board's accepted-exam list). Rule 150-3-.01 requires psychomotor clinical-abilities testing in five hands-on areas, which the DLOSCE does not provide. The board has published nothing about the ADEX-integrated DLOSCE; Georgia accepts the ADEX series as a whole (patient-based on/after 1/1/2015 or manikin-based on/after 1/1/2021), and an applicant must pass all sections, so an ADEX-reported DLOSCE pass alone, or an earlier standalone DLOSCE score, would not suffice.

- **in_jurisdiction_address_required:** False
- **barrier_level:** medium
- **details:** Georgia's Illegal Immigration Reform and Enforcement Act (O.C.G.A. § 50-36-1) treats a professional licence as a public benefit. Board rule para (4): 'In accordance with O.C.G.A. § 50-36-1, all applicants applying for licensure must submit an Affidavit Regarding Citizenship and submit a copy of secure and verifiable documentation supporting the Affidavit with his or her application.' The application's sworn affidavit offers only two options: '1) I am a United States citizen 18 years of age or older...' or '2) I am not a United States citizen, but I am a legal permanent resident of the United States 18 years of age or older, or I am a qualified alien or non-immigrant under the Federal Immigration and Nationality Act 18 years of age or older with an alien number issued by the Department of Homeland Security or other federal immigration agency. Please submit a copy of your current immigration document(s) which includes either your Alien number or your I-94 number and, if needed, SEVIS number.' A B-1/B-2 visitor is a 'non-immigrant under the INA' and receives an I-94 number at entry, so option 2 is literally satisfiable with a tourist I-94 plus a passport (a foreign passport with a current I-94 is on the O.C.G.A. § 50-36-2 secure-and-verifiable-document list reproduced on pp. 14-15 of the application). The agency then verifies the status through the federal SAVE program under § 50-36-1. Citizenship, PR and work authorisation are not required by the text, but: (a) the applicant must hold a current lawful nonimmigrant status at the time the affidavit is submitted (a visitor whose I-94 has expired after departure could not truthfully swear option 2); (b) whether the board treats a short-term B-2 status as adequate, or limits the licence term to the authorised stay, is discretionary and unverified; (c) the SSN field has no published alternative. Physical address required on the consent form (no P.O. box) but no Georgia address requirement found.

## Yüz yüze adımlar
(1) Hands-on clinical exam (ADEX/CRDTS/SRTA) must be taken in person at a US test site (manikin-based versions exist; no patient needed). (2) Application must be NOTARIZED ('1. NOTARIZED APPLICATION: Completed application form accompanied by the appropriate fee'); the form also has a notary block on the affidavit signature page and a separate notarised reference section, and the applicant must 'send a notarized copy of my clinical examination results to the Board' -- notarisation could be done at a US notary during the visit (foreign/consular notarisation not addressed). (3) Criminal background check: O.C.G.A. § 43-11-40(d) requires the applicant 'to provide the board with any and all information necessary to run a criminal background check, including but not limited to classifiable sets of fingerprints'; the application contains a consent form (name, physical address, sex, race, DOB, SSN) authorising 'periodic criminal history background checks for the duration of my licensure'. The fingerprinting method (Georgia electronic GAPS site vs mailed ink cards) was not verified; plan on doing it during the US visit. (4) Jurisprudence exam: open-book paper exam included in the application packet ('may be taken as an open book exam'), so it can be completed from abroad. (5) No interview. (6) CPR certification copy (course usually hands-on). (7) Processing: 'could take up to a minimum of 30 days after submission of a completed application'; incomplete applications void after one year; jurisprudence score valid one year.

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
INBDE pass (scores released by JCNDE to the board); board-approved hands-on clinical exam, all sections 75+; Georgia jurisprudence exam 75+, open-book, in English, score valid 1 year (Rule 150-3-.01(2)); official transcript/diploma from CODA-accredited school; verification of any other licences held; current CPR certification (Rule 150-3-.08: one- and two-rescuer CPR and obstructed-airway management for adults, children and infants per AHA/Red Cross/ASHI/NSC/EMS Safety or board-approved agency); criminal background check with fingerprints, fees paid by applicant; Affidavit Regarding Citizenship with secure and verifiable documents (O.C.G.A. § 50-36-1); SSN on application; notarised application and notarised copy of clinical exam results; references (questionnaire asking e.g. 'Would you feel comfortable going to this person for your dental needs?'); PDMP enrollment within 30 days of DEA registration (if obtaining a DEA number). No English-language test (exams must be in English). No professional liability insurance requirement found. Remedial rules: after three failures of 1-2 sections a board-approved remedial course is required; after a fourth failure no further attempts recognised; failing 3+ sections three times requires a one-year ADA-accredited course of study.

## İlk ücretler
Application fee $125 non-refundable (check or money order); background-check fees borne by applicant (amount unverified); clinical exam fees paid to the testing agency (full ADEX series $2,995 per known facts); notary and licence-verification fees; licence/renewal fee schedule is in Rule 150-12-.01 (not verified in this research). Returned-check charge $40. Processing minimum ~30 days after a complete application.

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Biennial. Rule 150-3-.05(1): 'Licenses shall expire at the close of December 31 in all odd-numbered years, and shall be administratively revoked for failure to renew on July 1 of the following even-numbered year.' Online renewal available via the board's renewal website.
- **renewal_fee:** Unclear. Set by board under O.C.G.A. § 43-11-46(a) and Rule 150-12-.01 ('Fees for license renewal shall be assessed as set forth in Rule 150-12-.01'); the rule text could not be retrieved (rule-compilation sites returned 403). Late/penalty fees apply.
- **ce_hours:** 40 hours per biennium (O.C.G.A. § 43-11-46.1; Rule 150-3-.09(1): 'a minimum of forty (40) hours of continuing education during each biennium'); records kept 3 years; random audits; CE includes a board-required opioid/prescribing component (seen in rule text, details not read). CPR course earns up to 4 CE hours; teaching credit available.
- **ce_online_ok:** Unclear. The portions of Rule 150-3-.09 read did not state a cap on online/self-study hours, but the full rule was not read; the rule references credit for 'in-person CPR course' (4 hours), implying some live-format distinction. Treat as 'probably mostly online, verify cap with the board'.
- **cpr_required:** Yes. O.C.G.A. § 43-11-46(c) and Rule 150-3-.08: dentists 'shall maintain at all times during licensure ... current certification in one and two rescuer CPR and management of obstructed airway for adults, children and infants' from AHA, Red Cross, ASHI, NSC, EMS Safety Services or other board-approved agency; 'Current certification is also a condition for license renewal.' These providers' certifications normally require a hands-on skills check; the rule awards CE credit for an 'in-person CPR course'. A purely online CPR certificate is unlikely to be accepted (unverified).
- **practice_hours_required:** None to renew an active licence. However, for REINSTATEMENT (after lapse or inactive status) Rule 150-3-.05 provides that 'after five (5) years have passed without the applicant being actively engaged in the practice of dentistry ... the Board may, in its discretion, require passage of an examination administered by the Georgia Board of Dentistry or a Regional Testing Agency' and may require physician documentation of ability to practise safely.
- **address_or_residency_required:** No Georgia residency or in-state address requirement found. A physical (non-P.O. box) mailing address is required on forms. For non-citizens, O.C.G.A. § 50-36-1 lawful-presence affidavit/verification may be required again at renewal (not verified for the dental board; this is standard for Georgia agencies).
- **insurance_required:** No mandatory professional liability insurance found in the statute, rules or application.
- **inactive_status:** Rule 150-3-.07 (amended): an active licensee 'may apply to the Board for inactive status by submitting the required form and paying the proper fee' ($50 application fee for dentists). 'A licensee granted inactive status is exempt from the payment of the biennial renewal fee and continuing education requirement.' No practice allowed while inactive. No maximum duration stated. A lapsed licence must first be reinstated before inactive status can be requested. Return to active is 'by following the Board's reinstatement policy' (i.e., the $1,675 reinstatement process).
- **reactivation:** Reinstatement application (used for lapsed/administratively revoked AND inactive licences): $1,675 non-refundable fee; proof of 40 hours of board-approved CE obtained within the last two years (plus a further 40 hours for the upcoming renewal period); current CPR; licence verifications from every jurisdiction; malpractice/discipline disclosures; CPR; and per Rule 150-3-.05, if five years have passed without active practice the Board 'may, in its discretion, require passage of an examination administered by the Georgia Board of Dentistry or a Regional Testing Agency' (i.e., a new hands-on clinical exam) and physician documentation of fitness. Reinstatement is 'only in the discretion of the Board'. A licence not renewed by the July 1 penalty deadline 'shall have the same effect as a revocation' (O.C.G.A. § 43-11-46(b)).
- **remote_maintainability:** moderate
- **details:** Keeping the licence ACTIVE from abroad is feasible on paper: biennial online renewal, 40 CE hours (online cap unverified), no practice hours, no insurance, no in-state address. Awkward parts: CPR certification from an approved provider (normally hands-on skills session) every renewal; and, for a non-citizen, the possibility that the O.C.G.A. § 50-36-1 lawful-presence affidavit and immigration documents (current I-94) must be resubmitted at each renewal, which a visitor living abroad cannot easily satisfy. Inactive status is cheap ($50, no CE/fee) and unlimited in duration, but returning from it costs $1,675 plus 80 CE hours and, after 5+ years without active practice, a possible new hands-on clinical exam at the board's discretion, so inactive status is a poor long-term parking option; a lapse is worse (treated as revocation). Note Georgia is unattainable for this candidate anyway because initial licensure requires a hands-on exam.

## Açık sorular
1) Whether the board/SAVE will accept a B-1/B-2 visitor's I-94 as satisfying the 'non-immigrant under the INA' option of the O.C.G.A. § 50-36-1 affidavit for a professional licence, and whether the licence term would be limited to the authorised stay; also whether non-citizens must re-submit the affidavit and current immigration documents at each biennial renewal (standard for Georgia agencies, not verified for GBD). 2) Whether an application can be filed without an SSN (no ITIN/'no SSN' option on the form). 3) Fingerprinting method (GAPS electronic in Georgia vs mailed FD-258 cards) and whether it can be done from abroad. 4) The exact biennial renewal fee and late-penalty amounts (Rule 150-12-.01 not retrievable). 5) Whether Rule 150-3-.09 caps online/self-study CE hours and whether an online-only CPR certificate satisfies Rule 150-3-.08. 6) Whether notarisation by a foreign notary or US consulate is accepted. None of these change the core conclusion: Georgia requires a hands-on (manikin or patient) regional clinical exam, so there is no theory-only route.
