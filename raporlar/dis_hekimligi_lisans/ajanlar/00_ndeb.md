# Kesişen konu: Canada / NDEB route for a recent Yeditepe University (CODA-accredited since 1 Feb 2024) graduate who is a Turkish citizen with no Canadian status: accredited-programme recognition, Certification Process steps/documents/fees/dates, exam delivery locations, nature and validity of the NDEB certificate, and the CDAC-CODA reciprocal agreement.

Güven: high

## Bulgular

IMPORTANT CORRECTION TO THE "KNOWN FACTS": the NDEB Certification Process no longer consists of a Written Examination plus a Virtual OSCE. Since March 2023 a single computer-based examination, the Virtual OSCE, "replace[d] the Written Examination and OSCE as the examination required for certification as a dentist in Canada" (NDEB news, 25 Oct 2022: https://ndeb-bned.ca/2022/10/25/the-virtual-osce-ndebs-new-certification-examination/). The current NDEB accredited-graduates page shows the flow as "Apply to the Certification Process → Virtual OSCE → NDEB Certification → Provincial Licensure" (https://ndeb-bned.ca/en/accredited), and By-law 2 (approved 10 July 2026, effective 15 July 2026) s.6.1 grants a Certificate to any Certification Candidate "who has passed the Virtual OSCE" and provided proof of graduation (https://ndeb-bned.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-07-08_-By-law-2-Certification-and-Equivalency-Process_Approved-July-10-2026.pdf). The Virtual OSCE is 200 computer-delivered questions (50 single-answer MCQ + 150 case-based items with photos/radiographs/charts); no manikin or patient component (https://ndeb-bned.ca/certification-process/virtual-osce). So for this candidate the entire Canadian national-certification route is ONE computer-based exam.

(a) IS YEDITEPE TREATED AS AN ACCREDITED PROGRAMME? YES, confirmed on the official CDAC site (new domain cdac-cadc.ca; the legacy cda-adc.ca/cdacweb URL now resets connections / returns 503 and could not be read). CDAC's page "International Oral Health Education Programs Recognized by CDAC" (page dated 2025-08-22) states: "CDAC recognizes two CODA-accredited international programs under our reciprocal agreement. Turkey: Yeditepe University – Yeditepe University's dental education program has been accredited by CODA as of February 1, 2024. Graduates of the dental program at Yeditepe University who obtained their dental diploma after February 1, 2024 will be considered graduates of a CODA-accredited program. Students who graduated prior to February 1, 2024 will not be considered graduates of a CODA-accredited program." The same page lists Saudi Arabia: King Abdulaziz University (CODA as of 1 Aug 2019, same diploma-date rule) (https://www.cdac-cadc.ca/international/international-oral-health-education-programs-recognized-by-cdac/). CDAC's "Internationally Educated Professionals" page adds: "Graduates from international programs accredited by the organizations above are also recognized by CDAC. International dentistry professionals are thus eligible to participate in the National Dental Examining Board of Canada (NDEB) Certification Process as graduates from Canadian accredited programs" (https://www.cdac-cadc.ca/international/internationally-educated-professionals/). NDEB By-law 2 defines "Accredited Program" as "a degree granting undergraduate dental program accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation of Canada or recognized by the Commission on Dental Accreditation of Canada pursuant to a mutual recognition agreement", and "Non-Accredited Program" as one "not ... recognized by [CDAC] pursuant to a mutual recognition agreement" (By-law 2 PDF above). The NDEB accredited-graduates page tells applicants to find accredited programmes via "CODA Accredited Dental Programs (US and international)" (https://ndeb-bned.ca/en/accredited). CODA's own programme search lists "Yeditepe University, Turkey: The Commission on Dental Accreditation granted accreditation to Yeditepe University's dental education program on February 1, 2024. Students who were enrolled in the program at the time accreditation was granted ... and who successfully complete the program, will be considered graduates of a CODA-accredited program. Students who graduated from the program prior to the granting of accreditation ... will not" (https://coda.ada.org/find-a-program/search-dental-programs). No NDEB news item specifically about Yeditepe was found (a site-restricted search of ndeb-bned.ca for "Yeditepe" returned nothing); the NDEB's precedent post for KAU (6 Sept 2019) says graduates "who obtained their dental diploma as of August 1, 2019 will be eligible to apply for NDEB certification through the pathway for graduates of accredited dental programs" (https://ndeb-bned.ca/activity-log-backup/?p=2274). GRADUATION DATE: CDAC keys on the diploma date (after 1 Feb 2024); CODA keys on enrolment at 1 Feb 2024 plus completion. A recent graduate (2025/2026) satisfies both. Separate NDEB timing rule: eligibility as a Certification Candidate under By-law 2 s.7.1.2 requires that "not more than sixty (60) months have elapsed since the date of graduation"; s.7.4: a person who does not pass the Virtual OSCE while so designated "shall automatically cease to be a Certification Candidate"; after 60 months the only route is a Special Consideration application to the Executive Committee requiring evidence of continuous practice for the past 5 years and CE for the past 3 years (By-law 2 s.8; Policies & Procedures s.3 at https://ndeb-bned.ca/resources/by-laws-and-policies/; how-to-apply page category "more than 5 years ago" at https://ndeb-bned.ca/certification-process/new-how-to-apply/). Maximum three attempts at the Virtual OSCE (s.7.5).

(b) CERTIFICATION PROCESS STEPS, DOCUMENTS, FEES, TIMELINES (graduate of an accredited programme outside Canada). Steps (https://ndeb-bned.ca/certification-process/new-how-to-apply/): (1) confirm eligibility category — for this candidate "Received a BDS/DDS/DMD from an accredited dental program within the last 5 years" (or "within 4 months of completing" if still a final-year student; the Virtual OSCE "can be taken as early as four months before you graduate"); (2) documents; (3) formatting rules; (4) create an NDEBConnect account, pay the application fee, upload photo, mail documents before the application deadline. Required documents for the "within the last 5 years" category: (i) Identification — "Notarized photocopy of one of the following: Canadian provincial or territorial card; Canadian passport; foreign passport that meets the requirements" (government-issued, legal name exactly as in profile, DOB, photo, signature, valid/not expired, issued within 10 years) — mailed to NDEB; (ii) the signed Certification Process Form (2026 form checklist: form, notarized valid government photo ID, proof of name change if any, sworn affidavit for gaps in licensing if any; Statement of Good Standing sent directly by any licensing body if ever licensed outside Canada; proof of graduation sent by the university after graduation, "not required to be submitted before taking the examination"; disclosure of any suspended/revoked/probation licence) (https://ndeb-bned.ca/activity-log-backup/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2026_Certification-Process-Form.pdf); (iii) Statements of Good Standing from "all licensing bodies in which you were licensed", sent by the regulator from an official traceable email to verification@ndeb-bned.ca (not required for academic/internship-only licences); (iv) "Gaps in licensing": "If after completing your dental education in any country, you were not licensed for a period of twelve (12) months or more, you must submit an original sworn affidavit attesting to the fact that you were not licensed, including the reason"; (v) Proof of Graduation: "An original official transcript of marks be sent directly from your university to the NDEB in a sealed envelope" (or electronically only via MyCreds, Parchment, National Student Clearinghouse, My eQuals); the final transcript "must indicate the date the degree was conferred"; (vi) "Verification of Education with University": "Your degree and graduation date/expected graduation date are verified directly with the university you have selected during application ... If your graduation information is not confirmed at least 15 days prior to the examination date, you ... may be withdrawn" (By-law 2 s.7.2: verification "shall be provided by the Dean or a Dean's Designate"). Document formatting: notarization must be by a Notary Public/lawyer/Commissioner of Oaths whose statement and seal are in English or French — "If you are unable to have document(s) notarized by a notarizing officer whose credentials are in English or French, you may have an Officer of a Canadian Consulate or Embassy, or a New Zealand Consulate or Embassy make a notarized photocopy"; translations must be by a certified translator, stapled to a photocopy of the original (same page). LANGUAGE: the NDEB imposes no English/French test; the FAQ says only that "The DRA may require additional documentation, jurisprudence tests, and evidence of language proficiency prior to licensure" (https://ndeb-bned.ca/resources/faq/). The exam-day ID "must be in English or French. Photocopies and digital images will not be accepted" (2026 Virtual OSCE Protocol, effective 19 Jan 2026: https://ndeb-bned.ca/activity-log-backup/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-VIRTUAL-OSCE-PROTOCOL_Revised.pdf). IMMIGRATION STATUS: none required by NDEB — FAQ: "Does the NDEB have any residency requirements for participation in their processes? The NDEB does not have residency requirements for participation in NDEB examinations"; "applicants are not required to be permanent residents or Canadian citizens to apply ... or participate in examinations"; "The NDEB cannot provide assistance in obtaining visas or other documents required for travel for the purposes of participating in the examinations" (https://ndeb-bned.ca/resources/faq/). The only place citizenship/PR matters is Stage-2 priority registration for the NDECC (Equivalency Process only) and the BTDPC bridge programme (Policies s.7.1; https://ndeb-bned.ca/2026/06/25/new-entry-to-practice-pathway-for-internationally-trained-dentists/) — neither applies to this candidate. No SIN or Canadian address is requested anywhere in the Certification Process documents; the certificate "will be mailed to the address listed in your NDEBConnect account" (https://ndeb-bned.ca/2025/05/09/what-to-expect-after-completing-the-virtual-osce/). FEES (CAD, effective 1 July 2025, "subject to change"): Application fee (one-time) $450; Virtual OSCE $1,750; verification of score $200; replacement certificate $75; proof of certification $30 per letter; misconduct/conduct appeals $1,200 (https://ndeb-bned.ca/certification-process/fees/; FAQ and Virtual OSCE page also state $1,750). From 1 Sept 2026 withdrawal fees are 50% of the exam fee before the registration deadline and 100% after, and registration has two periods (first opens on a set date and closes when full; second about 6–8 weeks before the exam for seats freed by withdrawals) (https://ndeb-bned.ca/2026/08/13/changes-to-the-exam-registration-process/). TIMELINES: application deadlines for the Certification Process ("documents must be received by the application deadline") — exam 1 Mar 2027: application opens 5 Oct 2026, deadline 5 Jan 2027; exam 20 May 2027: opens 26 Jan 2027, deadline 5 Apr 2027; exam 22 Nov 2027: opens 6 May 2027, deadline 18 Aug 2027 (https://ndeb-bned.ca/certification-process/dates-and-locations/). Results "are released within 8 weeks of the exam"; once a pass and proof of graduation are confirmed "you can be certified. Your certificate will be mailed ... and your name will be added to the National Register" (https://ndeb-bned.ca/certification-process/registering-for-the-exam/). Overall "From the time an individual is eligible to take the certification exams it can take between 6-18 months" (https://ndeb-bned.ca/certification-process/getting-your-certificate-and-license/). Accounts with no exam activity for 24 months are set inactive and need a reactivation fee (Policies s.5). New By-laws effective 1 July 2026 add an Ineligibility and Misrepresentation clause; false/incomplete information voids results (https://ndeb-bned.ca/2026/07/31/new-ndeb-by-laws-what-you-need-to-know/).

(c) WHERE THE VIRTUAL OSCE IS DELIVERED; 2026-27 DATES. "The Virtual OSCE is administered at locations across Canada and at some locations in the USA, Ireland and Australia" (https://ndeb-bned.ca/examination-dates-and-locations/). "In May and November, the Virtual OSCE will be held at Prometric test centres in Canada and the US. If your exam is taking place at a Prometric test centre you will need to schedule your seat time with Prometric after you have registered" (https://ndeb-bned.ca/certification-process/registering-for-the-exam/). There is NO delivery in Turkey or continental Europe; the only non-North-American sites are Australia and Ireland (and Ireland only at the March sitting). Scheduled sessions: 23 Nov 2026 — United States/Canada and Australia (registration closed 26 Oct 2026); 1 Mar 2027 — United States/Canada (registration opens 3 Nov 2026 10:00 ET, deadline 18 Jan 2027 21:00 ET) and Australia/Ireland (opens 10 Nov 2026, deadline 18 Jan 2027); 20 May 2027 — United States/Canada (opens 26 Jan 2027, deadline 19 Apr 2027); 22 Nov 2027 — Canada only (opens 25 May 2027, deadline 25 Oct 2027) (https://ndeb-bned.ca/certification-process/dates-and-locations/ and https://ndeb-bned.ca/examination-dates-and-locations/). Location transfers are free before the registration deadline. Check-in: government photo ID (passport acceptable) in English or French; Prometric sites may wand/fingerprint candidates (2026 Protocol PDF above).

(d) IS THE NDEB CERTIFICATE A LICENCE? DOES IT EXPIRE? Not a licence: "Does NDEB Certification automatically give me a license to practise in Canada? No, you must apply for a license with the Dental Regulatory Authorities (DRAs) in the province where you want to practice. The DRA may require additional documentation, jurisprudence tests, and evidence of language proficiency prior to licensure" (https://ndeb-bned.ca/en/accredited and FAQ); "The provincial Dental Regulatory Authorities (DRAs) are responsible for issuing dental licenses" (https://ndeb-bned.ca/certification-process/getting-your-certificate-and-license/); CDAC: "The final step ... is licensure. Licensing of dentists is overseen by the provincial regulatory authorities" (https://www.cdac-cadc.ca/public/pathway-to-dentistry/). Expiry: By-law 2 contains no expiry, renewal, or validity period for the Certificate; the only removal mechanism is s.11.1: "the NDEB shall revoke or cancel a person's Certificate and delete from the register the name of any person whose provincial registration has been cancelled or suspended and restore such name ... if and when such cancellation or suspension is reversed or terminated"; s.7.8: a certified person "may not retake any Examination unless requested to do so by a Provincial Dental Regulatory Authority" (By-law 2 PDF). The NDEB's statutory mandate is "to issue certificates to dentists who successfully meet the standard" (https://ndeb-bned.ca/resources/by-laws-and-policies/). Provincial DRAs access certification data from the National Register. No explicit "does not expire" sentence was found on ndeb-bned.ca; the conclusion that the certificate itself is open-ended rests on the by-law's silence plus the revocation clause. Whether a province will register a certificate-holder many years after certification is a provincial currency/recency question, not an NDEB one.

(e) CDAC/NDEB STATEMENTS ON CODA-ACCREDITED PROGRAMMES OUTSIDE THE US. CDAC: "CDAC recognizes two CODA-accredited international programs under our reciprocal agreement" — Yeditepe (1 Feb 2024) and King Abdulaziz (1 Aug 2019) (https://www.cdac-cadc.ca/international/international-oral-health-education-programs-recognized-by-cdac/); CDAC's reciprocity table shows USA (CODA) general dentistry "Recognized" and states that graduates of programmes accredited by its partner agencies "are also recognized by CDAC" and "eligible to participate in the ... NDEB Certification Process as graduates from Canadian accredited programs" (https://www.cdac-cadc.ca/international/internationally-educated-professionals/). NDEB precedent: 2019 news item on KAU, "the first time an international predoctoral dental program based outside the US has received such a status", with graduates "eligible to apply for NDEB certification through the pathway for graduates of accredited dental programs" (https://ndeb-bned.ca/activity-log-backup/?p=2274). NDEB By-law 2 definitions (quoted above) make recognition by CDAC under a mutual-recognition agreement sufficient. The CDAC page was last updated 22 Aug 2025 and is the most recent official statement found; no 2026 change to the reciprocal agreement was found. (Web-search budget was exhausted near the end; all key claims above were nevertheless read directly from official NDEB/CDAC/CODA pages.)

## Aday için sonuçlar

1. Pathway: the candidate is a graduate of an "Accredited Program" for NDEB purposes (CDAC recognises Yeditepe under the CODA reciprocal agreement; By-law 2 definition). They use the Certification Process, not the Equivalency Process (no AFK/ACJ/NDECC), and there is only ONE exam: the computer-based Virtual OSCE. The "Written Examination + Virtual OSCE" assumption in the brief is outdated (Written Exam and OSCE were retired in March 2023). This fully meets the "theory/computer-based only, no hands-on, no residency" requirement at the national level.
2. Status/ID: the NDEB has no citizenship, PR, work-permit, SIN, Canadian-address or language-test requirement; a notarized copy of a Turkish passport is explicitly acceptable ID. The practical hurdles are logistical: (i) Turkish notaries' statements/seals are in Turkish, so the notarized passport copy should be made by an officer of the Canadian Embassy/Consulate (Ankara/Istanbul) or by a notary who can issue an English/French-language notarial certificate; (ii) Yeditepe must send the final transcript (with conferral date) in a sealed envelope directly to the NDEB and the Dean/designate must answer the NDEB's verification request; (iii) if the candidate has ever been registered/licensed in Turkey (e.g., with the Turkish Dental Association/Ministry of Health), a Statement of Good Standing must be emailed by that body from an official address; if unlicensed for 12+ months after graduation, a sworn affidavit (in English/French, notarized) is required.
3. Travel: the Virtual OSCE is NOT offered in Turkey or Europe (except Ireland in March). The candidate must travel to Canada or the US (Prometric, May/Nov; Canadian venues in March), or Ireland/Australia in March, on a visitor visa they obtain themselves (NDEB explicitly gives no visa help). A Turkish citizen will need a Canadian TRV or a US B1/B2 (or an Irish short-stay visa) for the exam trip.
4. Budget/timing: CAD 450 application + CAD 1,750 per attempt (max 3 attempts), plus notarization, courier and travel. Next realistic sitting: 1 Mar 2027 (apply with all documents received by 5 Jan 2027; register from 3 Nov 2026), then 20 May 2027 (docs by 5 Apr 2027) and 22 Nov 2027 (docs by 18 Aug 2027, Canada only). Results within 8 weeks; certificate mailed abroad to the NDEBConnect address.
5. Hard deadline: the Virtual OSCE must be passed within 60 months of graduation (By-law 2 s.7.1.2/7.4); after that the candidate would need Special Consideration, which demands 5 years of continuous licensed practice and 3 years of CE — effectively unavailable to someone not practising. So act early.
6. Longevity: the NDEB certificate has no expiry or renewal in the by-laws and can be revoked only if a provincial registration is cancelled/suspended; once certified the candidate's name sits on the National Register indefinitely. But the certificate is NOT a licence. Whether a province will register (and later keep inactive/reactivate) a non-resident Turkish citizen is a provincial question — Ontario is already known to require citizenship/PR/work permit; the other provinces' immigration-status, language, jurisprudence and currency rules must be checked by the provincial-registration agent. The NDEB stage itself raises no status barrier.
