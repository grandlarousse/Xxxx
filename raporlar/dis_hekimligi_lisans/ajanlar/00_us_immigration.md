A. THE SSN REQUIREMENT (42 U.S.C. 666(a)(13)) AND HOW STATES HANDLE "NO SSN"
=====================================================================
A1. Federal text. 42 U.S.C. 666(a)(13)(A) requires each state's child-support plan to have "procedures requiring that the social security number of any applicant for a professional license, driver's license, occupational license, recreational license, or marriage license be recorded on the application" (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/666). The statute says the number must be "recorded"; it does not itself say an applicant must possess one.

A2. Federal guidance (OCSE PIQ-99-05, 14 July 1999). The ACF/OCSE page hosting PIQ-99-05 returned an empty bot-challenge response (https://www.acf.hhs.gov/css/policy-guidance/social-security-numbers-license-applications-and-other-documents), so the primary text was NOT read. Its content is, however, described by an official state source actually read: Texas Attorney General Opinion KP-0516 (10 Feb 2026) states that prior Texas practice relied on "the nonbinding federal guidance in PIQ-99-05", which read sec. 466(a)(13) as not requiring a person to obtain an SSN to get a licence and recommended a sworn no-SSN affidavit (https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/opinions/ken-paxton/kp-0516). Washington DOH's application packets implement exactly that reading (see A4).

A3. Important federal backdrop (8 U.S.C. 1621, read at https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/8/1621): (i) a "professional license ... provided by an agency of a State" is a "State or local public benefit" (sec. 1621(c)(1)(A)); (ii) aliens who are "a nonimmigrant under the Immigration and Nationality Act" ARE eligible (sec. 1621(a)(2)) — a B-1/B-2 visitor is a nonimmigrant; (iii) the term "public benefit" does not even apply "to the issuance of a professional license to, or the renewal of a professional license by, a foreign national not physically present in the United States" (sec. 1621(c)(2)(C)); (iv) states may extend eligibility to unlawfully-present aliens only by a post-1996 state law that "affirmatively provides for such eligibility" (sec. 1621(d)). Net: federal law never bars a B-2 visitor (or a foreign national applying from abroad) from a state dental licence; only STATE law can.

A4. States verified to ACCEPT a no-SSN affidavit or an ITIN (dental-relevant):
- WASHINGTON (DLOSCE state): DOH packets state: "If you have a Social Security Number, the law requires you to disclose it ... 42 U.S.C. 666(a)(13); RCW 26.23.150 ... You are not required to have or obtain a Social Security Number to apply for or obtain a license from the Department of Health. If you do not have a Social Security Number, you are still eligible to apply for and obtain a credential if you meet the requirements. Please see the Declaration of No Social Security Number Form." (Dental Therapist packet DOH 640-001, Sept 2025: https://doh.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-10/640001.pdf; identical language in DOH 670-120, June 2025: https://doh.wa.gov/sites/default/files/legacy/Documents/Pubs/670120.pdf). This is a department-wide policy covering dentists. No lawful-presence or citizenship requirement appears in the dentist statutes/rules quoted on the ADA Washington page (updated 8 Oct 2026: https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Washington_Licensure). The DOH checklist adds that a fingerprint-based WSP/FBI check "may be required if you have lived in another state or if you have a criminal record in Washington State."
- COLORADO (DLOSCE state): SB21-077 (signed 27 May 2021, effective 7 Sept 2021; https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb21-077) amended C.R.S. 24-34-107(1) so that every DORA licence application requires name, address and SSN but "IF THE APPLICANT DOES NOT HAVE A SOCIAL SECURITY NUMBER, THE DIVISION, BOARD, OR AGENCY SHALL REQUIRE THE APPLICANT'S INDIVIDUAL TAXPAYER IDENTIFICATION NUMBER, OR ANOTHER DOCUMENT VERIFYING THE APPLICANT'S IDENTITY, AS DETERMINED BY SUCH DIVISION, BOARD, OR AGENCY" and struck the former lawful-presence/secure-and-verifiable-document language (signed act: https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2021a_077_signed.pdf). So Colorado does not even require an ITIN if another identity document is accepted. The ADA Colorado page (updated 7 Oct 2026) contains no SSN, citizenship, lawful-presence or fingerprint requirement (https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Colorado_Licensure).
- OREGON (DLOSCE state) — MIXED: ORS 25.785(1) requires boards to record the SSN, but 25.785(2) says a board "may accept a written statement from an individual who has not been issued a Social Security number by the United States Social Security Administration" (https://www.oregonlaws.org/ors/25.785). However, the Oregon Board of Dentistry's own Privacy Act Notification (rev. 12/2013) says providing the SSN "is a mandatory requirement under ... ORS 25.785 and under Federal Law USC section 666(a)(13)(a). Failure to provide your Social Security Number will be a basis to refuse to issue your license" (https://www.oregon.gov/dentistry/Documents/Form_Privacy_Act_Notification.pdf). The statute permits a no-SSN statement; whether OBD exercises that discretion must be confirmed with the Board.
- CALIFORNIA: BPC 30(a)(2)(A) requires "either the individual taxpayer identification number or social security number" and 30(a)(2)(B) forbids boards from requiring disclosure of "citizenship status or immigration status"; 30(c) says a board "shall not process an application for an initial license unless the applicant provides ... individual taxpayer identification number or social security number" (https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_30). BPC 135.5 (SB 1159, 2014, enacted "pursuant to subsection (d) of Section 1621 of Title 8") bars denial of licensure "based on his or her citizenship status or immigration status" (https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_135.5). Caveat: California requires an ITIN if no SSN — see Part C on whether a non-resident can get one. (California is not a DLOSCE state.)
- NEVADA: AB 275 (enrolled, signed June 2019) declares that a person "regardless of his or her immigration or citizenship status, is eligible to receive a professional or occupational license ... pursuant to 8 U.S.C. 1621(d)", bars denial based solely on immigration/citizenship status, and provides that an applicant "who does not have a social security number must provide an alternative personally identifying number, including, without limitation, his or her individual taxpayer identification number" (https://archive.leg.state.nv.us/Session/80th2019/Bills/AB/AB275_EN.pdf).
- ILLINOIS: IDFPR "Social Security Number Affidavit" (form IL486-2003) — "Applicants who do not have a social security number to submit to the IDFPR must complete this form" (https://idfpr.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/idfpr/forms/online/SSNAffidavit.pdf).
- MAINE: Board of Dental Practice "Social Security Number Affidavit" with an ITIN field "(if applicable)" for applicants who "do not have a Social Security Number issued by the United States Social Security Administration" (https://www1.maine.gov/dental/documents/ssn_affidavit_opor.pdf).
- IDAHO: Idaho Code 73-122(2)-(3): the SSN requirement "shall apply only to applicants who have been assigned a social security number"; a no-SSN applicant must present "written verification from the social security administration that the applicant has not been assigned a social security number", a foreign passport/birth certificate, and "such proof as the department may require that the applicant is lawfully present in the United States" (https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title73/T73CH1/SECT73-122/). (Note: an SSA "Form SSA-L676" refusal letter is normally obtained in person at a US SSA office — a trip item.)
- MICHIGAN (ambiguous): MCL 338.3434a(1) says an agency "shall not issue or renew a license unless the applicant's social security number is on file", but (3) exempts "an applicant who demonstrates he or she is exempt under law from obtaining a social security number" (https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-338-3434a). Whether a non-work-authorised foreign national counts as "exempt under law" is for the agency.

A5. States verified to make the SSN STRICTLY MANDATORY (no affidavit):
- TEXAS: AG Opinion KP-0516 (10 Feb 2026): "Family Code subsection 231.302(c)(1) unambiguously requires that occupational licensing authorities request and each applicant provide their social security number. As such, agencies cannot solicit or accept attestations that an applicant does not have a social security number ... Attorney General Opinion JC-0409 as well as its progeny are overruled" (https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/opinions/ken-paxton/kp-0516). Texas is therefore closed to a no-SSN applicant.
- NORTH DAKOTA: NDCC 43-50-02: "No issuer of an occupational or professional certificate, permit, or license may issue such a certificate, permit, or license, or renewal thereof, to any individual who has not first provided the individual's social security number" (https://ndlegis.gov/api/cencode/t43c50.pdf).
- INDIANA (a DLOSCE state): the ADA Indiana page (updated 8 Oct 2026) quotes 828 IAC 1-3-1.1(c): "All applicants must submit the applicant's United States Social Security number in order to be eligible for licensure" (https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Indiana_Licensure). Indiana is effectively closed.
- OREGON (in practice, per OBD notice — see A4).
- Historical note: Florida AG Opinion 96-85 (29 Oct 1996) upheld a municipality's right to require an SSN for an occupational licence as a tax-identification measure (https://www.myfloridalegal.com/node/2720); Florida DOH dental rules were not examined.
- Arizona, Kentucky, Alaska, Iowa: the ADA pages for these states (updated 7–8 Oct 2026) contain no SSN language either way; board application forms must be checked (https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Arizona_Licensure , .../Kentucky_Licensure , .../Alaska_Licensure , .../Iowa_Licensure).

B. LAWFUL-PRESENCE VERIFICATION LAWS vs. STATES THAT PROHIBIT IMMIGRATION-STATUS REQUIREMENTS; DOES A B-2 VISITOR PASS?
=====================================================================
B1. States with lawful-presence/SAVE statutes covering professional licences (primary text read):
- ARIZONA, A.R.S. 41-1080(A): no agency "shall ... issue a license to an individual if the individual does not provide documentation of citizenship or alien status" from a list that expressly includes "6. A foreign passport with a United States visa" and "7. An I-94 form with a photograph" (https://www.azleg.gov/ars/41/01080.htm). A B-1/B-2 visitor with a valid visa and I-94 therefore satisfies 41-1080(A). Subsection (B)(1) also exempts "a citizen of a foreign country" residing abroad where "the benefits that are related to the license do not require the individual to be present in the United States" — of doubtful application to a licence to practise. Renewal: (C) excuses re-documentation only after proof of citizenship or "nonexpiring work authorization"; (D) requires re-documentation if a "limited form of work authorization ... has expired" — the statute is silent on a licensee who never had work authorisation, so expect the Board to ask for a current passport/visa/I-94 at each renewal (open question). Arizona also requires a DPS fingerprint clearance card (A.R.S. 32-1232(B), 41-1758.03; ADA Arizona page) — see Part D.
- GEORGIA, O.C.G.A. 50-36-1 (FindLaw text, "current as of March 28, 2024"; https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-50-state-government/ga-code-sect-50-36-1/): "public benefit" expressly includes "Occupational license" and "Professional license"; every applicant must (f)(1)(A) "provide at least one secure and verifiable document, as defined in Code Section 50-36-2" and (f)(1)(B) "execute a signed and sworn affidavit verifying the applicant's lawful presence" — the affidavit alternatives include being "a qualified alien or nonimmigrant under the federal Immigration and Nationality Act" with an alien number; (h) "eligibility for public benefits shall be made through the SAVE program". No provision limiting a nonimmigrant's licence to the period of stay was found. A B-2 visitor is a "nonimmigrant" and can execute the affidavit (passport + visa/I-94 are the SAVE documents).
- SOUTH CAROLINA, S.C. Code 8-29-10: every agency "shall verify the lawful presence in the United States of any alien eighteen years of age or older who has applied for state or local public benefits, as defined in 8 USC Section 1621" (which includes professional licences); the applicant executes an affidavit "that he or she is a qualified alien or nonimmigrant under the Federal Immigration and Nationality Act ... and lawfully present", and eligibility "shall be made through the Systematic Alien Verification of Entitlement (SAVE) program" (https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t08c029.php). A B-2 nonimmigrant passes.
- MISSOURI, RSMo 208.009: bars public benefits to aliens "unlawfully present" and requires federal (SAVE) verification, but its "public benefit" definition lists grants, contracts, loans and welfare-type benefits — professional licences are not expressly named (https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=208.009). Treat as "probably not applicable to dental licences" pending board confirmation.
- NEBRASKA, Neb. Rev. Stat. 4-108/4-110: verification of lawful presence for "public benefits" is required "unless exempted"; 4-110(1) exempts "any purpose for which lawful presence in the United States is not restricted by law" (https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=4-108 ; https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=4-110). Whether the dental board treats licences as restricted is unverified.
- VIRGINIA, Va. Code 63.2-503.1 applies to "state or local public assistance pursuant to this subtitle" (social services), not to professional licences (https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title63.2/chapter5/section63.2-503.1/). Virginia should not be listed as a licence lawful-presence state on this basis.
- NOT VERIFIED (hosts blocked this session): Alabama Code 31-13-7, Oklahoma 56 O.S. 71, Tennessee 4-58-103, Utah 63G-12-402, Indiana IC 12-32-1. These are widely reported SAVE/lawful-presence statutes; treat as "probably require SAVE verification" until read. Even so, under 8 U.S.C. 1621(a)(2) a B-2 nonimmigrant is "lawfully present" for these purposes.
- SAVE itself: USCIS says SAVE provides "point in time immigration status and U.S. citizenship information" to >1,300 agencies including those issuing "occupational and professional licenses", and "does not determine eligibility for a benefit or license" (https://www.uscis.gov/save/about-save). Practical consequence: SAVE confirms a B-2 admission from the I-94, but the check is point-in-time — a board that re-verifies at renewal while the licensee is abroad may see no current admission. 8 U.S.C. 1621(c)(2)(C) (licence issuance/renewal to a foreign national not physically present is outside the "public benefit" definition) is the legal answer to that, but state boards may not know it; raise it in writing.

B2. States that AFFIRMATIVELY PROHIBIT immigration-status requirements (primary text read):
- CALIFORNIA: BPC 135.5 and 30(a)(2)(B) (above).
- COLORADO: C.R.S. 24-76.5-103(3)(k) (added by SB21-077): verification of lawful presence "is not required ... for an applicant for a license, certificate, or registration to practice a regulated profession or occupation", and (3.5) declares this "a state law within the meaning of 8 U.S.C. sec. 1621(d)" (https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2021a_077_signed.pdf).
- NEVADA: AB 275 (2019) (above).
- NEW JERSEY: P.L.2020, c.75, C.45:1-7.8 (approved 1 Sept 2020): "lawful presence in the United States shall not be required to obtain a professional or occupational license, provided that the applicant meets all other requirements for licensure" (https://pub.njleg.gov/Bills/2020/PL20/75_.PDF; Governor's release: https://nj.gov/labor/lwdhome/press/2020/20200901_govleglicenses.shtml).
- WASHINGTON: no statute found, but DOH policy (A4) shows no lawful-presence or SSN gate in practice.
- NOT VERIFIED this session (search budget exhausted; NCSL page blocked): Maryland, Illinois, Minnesota, Massachusetts, New York, Oregon, Utah "status-neutral licensing" laws. Illinois' and Maine's no-SSN affidavit forms (A4) are verified; the rest need a follow-up.

B3. Does a B-2 visitor "pass" lawful-presence verification? Yes while admitted: (i) 8 U.S.C. 1621(a)(2) makes any INA nonimmigrant eligible for state licences; (ii) Arizona lists "foreign passport with a United States visa" and "I-94" as qualifying documents; (iii) Georgia and South Carolina affidavits expressly cover a "nonimmigrant ... lawfully present"; (iv) SAVE verifies the I-94 admission. Two caveats: a B-2 visitor is lawfully present but has no work authorisation (so cannot practise), and 8 U.S.C. 1621(c)(2)(A) — the exemption for a nonimmigrant "whose visa for entry is related to such employment" — does not apply to B visitors, so the state statute's verification mechanics apply in full.

C. CAN A NON-RESIDENT WITH NO US INCOME GET AN ITIN?
=====================================================================
- IRS Form W-7 instructions: "An ITIN is a nine-digit number issued by the IRS to individuals who are required for federal tax purposes to have a U.S. taxpayer identification number but who don't have and aren't eligible to get a social security number"; "The ITIN is for federal tax purposes only"; "Individuals applying for an ITIN must include a U.S. federal tax return unless they meet an exception" (https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw7). The reason boxes (a–h) are all tax-based (treaty benefit, filing a US return, dependent/spouse, nonresident student, "other" with Exceptions 1–5: passive income with third-party withholding, other income such as scholarships/gambling, mortgage interest, FIRPTA dispositions, T.D. 9363 corporate e-filing). A state-licence requirement is NOT a listed reason or exception. Practical reading: a Turkish resident with no US-source income and no US return to file has no qualifying basis; the ITIN route works only if the applicant creates a genuine federal tax purpose (e.g., US-source income reportable on Form 1040-NR, or a treaty-based exception) — and the IRS can reject a W-7 without one.
- Acceptance agents in Turkey: the IRS lists acceptance agents located in Ankara, Istanbul (Kartal), Bursa, Bolu and Denizli (https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/acceptance-agents-turkey). The W-7 instructions distinguish AAs (must still send original/certified documents to the IRS) from CAAs (can certify the passport so originals need not be mailed) — check each Turkish agent's designation on the IRS page before engaging.
- Consequence for state choice: states that accept a sworn "no SSN" declaration WITHOUT an ITIN (Washington; Colorado's "another document verifying identity"; Illinois/Maine affidavits) are safer than ITIN-mandatory states (California BPC 30(c)).

D. FBI / STATE FINGERPRINT CHECKS FROM ABROAD
=====================================================================
- FBI Identity History Summary (IdHS): the FBI's request form I-783 (rev. 03/19/2025) instructs: "Mail the signed requestor information form, fingerprint card, and payment of $18 U.S. dollars to ... FBI CJIS Division – Summary Request, 1000 Custer Hollow Road, Clarksburg, West Virginia 26306"; payment by certified check, money order or credit-card form; the form has "Country of Citizenship", "Country of Residence" and "*Country" address fields (foreign mailing addresses are contemplated) and asks whether the requester is a "U.S. Citizen or Legal Permanent Resident" (Yes/No) (https://forms.fbi.gov/criminal-history-summary-checks-review/q384893984839334.pdf). The form warns: "This is not a national background check ... If you are requesting a background check for employment or licensing within the U.S., you may be required by state statute or federal law to submit your request through your state identification bureau, the requesting federal agency, or another authorized channeling agency." The FBI's web pages (fbi.gov, edo.cjis.gov FAQ) could not be read this session, so the FBI's own statements that ink prints may be taken by any fingerprinting agency abroad and that channelers serve only US-located requesters are NOT VERIFIED here (they appeared only in third-party summaries). Identity History Summary assistance: identity@fbi.gov, +1-304-625-5590 (https://www.edo.cjis.gov/).

The safe, documentable position: attending a licensing examination, a jurisprudence exam, a board interview or fingerprinting appointment is a short, non-remunerated activity of a visitor who maintains a foreign residence — consistent with the USCIS B-1 criteria above — and nothing in the licence-application statutes read (AZ, GA, SC, CO, WA, NJ, CA, NV) requires work authorisation to apply. ## Aday için sonuçlar

1. The Yeditepe candidate's B-1/B-2 status is NOT a legal bar anywhere under federal law: 8 U.S.C. 1621(a)(2) makes any nonimmigrant eligible for a state professional licence, and 1621(c)(2)(C) removes licence issuance/renewal to a foreign national who is not physically present from the public-benefit restriction altogether. Only state law can block, and it does so through two gates: the SSN gate and the lawful-presence gate.
2. SSN gate — pick states with a documented no-SSN pathway that does NOT depend on an ITIN. Washington (DOH "Declaration of No Social Security Number"; "You are not required to have or obtain a Social Security Number") and Colorado (C.R.S. 24-34-107: ITIN "or another document verifying the applicant's identity") are the cleanest among the DLOSCE states. Avoid Indiana (US SSN mandatory), Texas (KP-0516: no affidavits accepted) and North Dakota (no licence without SSN). Treat Oregon as uncertain until the Board confirms it will exercise ORS 25.785(2); its own notice says no SSN = refusal.
3. ITIN is not a reliable workaround: the IRS issues ITINs only for a federal tax purpose (return or listed exception); "state licensing" is not one. States whose only alternative to an SSN is an ITIN (California BPC 30(c); Nevada AB 275) are therefore harder than they look unless the candidate first acquires a genuine US tax nexus. Turkish IRS acceptance agents exist (Ankara, Istanbul, Bursa, Bolu, Denizli) but cannot create a qualifying reason.
4. Lawful-presence gate — in states that verify (Arizona, Georgia, South Carolina, and reportedly AL/OK/TN/UT), the candidate passes while in the US on a valid B visa (Arizona's statute literally lists "a foreign passport with a United States visa" and an I-94). The risk is at renewal while abroad: SAVE checks are point-in-time, and Arizona's renewal clauses only address citizens and work-authorised aliens. Washington, Colorado, California, Nevada and New Jersey have no such gate at all, so renewals from Turkey raise no status question there. Cite 8 U.S.C. 1621(c)(2)(C) in writing if any board questions a renewal from abroad.
5. Fingerprints — Arizona (DPS clearance card), Kentucky (KSP/FBI) and Iowa (DCI/FBI packet) require fingerprint-based checks; Colorado and Oregon pages quote none; Washington's is conditional. An FBI Identity History Summary can be requested by mail from abroad with an ink card and US$18 (FBI form I-783), but the FBI itself warns that licensing checks usually must go through the state bureau/channeler, so plan to have ink cards rolled (in Turkey, or during the exam trip) in the format each state vendor accepts; confirm acceptance of foreign-rolled cards with the board before travelling.
6. Immigration conduct — present the trip honestly as exam/board-paperwork travel (USCIS B-1 criteria: legitimate business purpose, limited period, funds, residence abroad); keep exam registrations and board correspondence to hand; never perform any dental work or paid activity. The 9 FAM text could not be verified in this session and should be checked by the candidate or another agent before relying on a specific FAM citation.
7. Combined with the known fact that standalone DLOSCE administrations end after 9 Oct 2026, the realistic shortlist on the immigration/SSN axis is Washington first, Colorado second, Arizona third (passport+visa satisfies 41-1080 but fingerprint card and renewal re-verification add friction); Indiana and Texas are out; Oregon and Kentucky need written board confirmation on SSN policy before any money is spent.
