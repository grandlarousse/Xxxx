# Kesişen konu: Keeping a dental licence active for decades while living abroad; the Dentist & Dental Hygienist (DDH) Compact; US state renewal patterns (CE, CPR/BLS, inactive status, reactivation, active-practice rules); Canadian provincial renewal norms (insurance, CE/QA, currency of practice, non-practising registers)

Güven: medium

## Bulgular

A. DDH COMPACT (ddhcompact.org, pages read 2 Nov 2026)

1. Operational status: the homepage states verbatim: "The Dentist and Dental Hygienist Compact has reached activation status, however compact privileges are not yet being issued. The implementation process for the compact will take 18 to 24 months before compact privileges are able to issued." (https://ddhcompact.org/). The FAQ repeats that the Commission "is currently setting up the necessary procedures, rules and systems ... a data system and an application process ... This usually takes about 18 months" (https://ddhcompact.org/faq/). The News page (March 2025) shows an RFI for the Compact Data System; no news item announces privileges being issued as of the last post (May 2026) (https://ddhcompact.org/news/). Conclusion: as of Nov 2026 NO compact privileges exist; the compact cannot yet be used by anyone.

2. Member states: News page: "On May 13th, 2026, Governor Kevin Stitt signed Senate Bill 540 making Oklahoma the thirteenth state to enact the Dentist and Dental Hygienist Compact" (https://ddhcompact.org/news/). ADHA: "As of January 2026, 12 states have enacted compact legislation" (https://www.adha.org/advocacy/license-portability/). The ddhcompact.org Compact Map is an interactive graphic whose text export shows only the legend ("No Active Legislation / Legislation Pending / Legislation Enacted") so the named list could not be extracted (https://ddhcompact.org/compact-map/). The first ten (2023-24) are widely reported as Iowa, Tennessee, Washington, Kansas, Wisconsin, Ohio, Virginia, Minnesota, Maine, Colorado (ADA News Sept 2024, not read in this session: https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2024/september/ddh-compact-commission-holds-first-meeting/); the 11th and 12th (2025) and the exact current list are UNCLEAR. Of the candidate's DLOSCE states, Colorado, Washington and Iowa are reported members; Arizona, Oregon, Kentucky and Indiana are not (unverified).

3. Eligibility for a compact privilege (FAQ, verbatim list): "An unencumbered license in a state that has joined the compact; Graduated from a predoctoral dental education program accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation ...; Passed the National Board Examination; Completed a clinical assessment for licensure; Passed a background check prior to obtaining their qualifying license; Passed any jurisprudence requirements established by the remote state; Pay all required fees." (https://ddhcompact.org/faq/). "A qualifying license is an unrestricted license ... issued by a state participating in the compact ... currently active and unencumbered." The FAQ also says foreign-trained dentists (those without a CODA predoctoral DDS/DMD) "are not eligible to apply for compact privileges" — the Yeditepe graduate (CODA-accredited predoctoral programme since 1 Feb 2024) meets the education criterion.

4. Clinical assessment definition: the Commission adopted a "Rule on Clinical Assessment" on 10 Nov 2025 (first proposed 21 Jan 2025). Its operative text reads: "1.1 Clinical Assessment: A. As set forth in Section 2-D, satisfaction of the Clinical Assessment requirement shall not be interpreted to include pathways that provide licensure solely upon graduation from an accredited institution." (https://ddhcompact.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2025/11/Draft-Clinical-Assessment-Rule-Adopted-1.pdf, 1,320-char document). The rule does NOT say the assessment must be hands-on/manikin/patient-based; it only excludes graduation-only licensure pathways. Thus a dentist licensed by a compact state on the basis of DLOSCE (a state-recognised clinical assessment for licensure in Colorado/Washington) would, on the text of the adopted rule, appear to satisfy the criterion; a Canadian NDEB-only dentist has no compact-state licence so the compact is irrelevant to Canada. CAUTION: the Rulemaking page lists a further "Draft Rule on Clinical Assessment – For consideration at 2/19/2026 Full Commission Meeting" and "Rule Hearing at 6/26/2026" (https://ddhcompact.org/rulemaking/); I could not read the 2026 draft, so the definition may have been amended in 2026 (UNCLEAR).

5. Background check: the FAQ requires that the licensee "passed a background check prior to obtaining their qualifying license" — i.e. the home-state licence must have involved a criminal background check (model law requires participating states to run them). Whether every member state's dental board fingerprints applicants (e.g. Colorado historically did not) is UNCLEAR.

6. Fees: not published on the pages read; secondary sources (NM legislative analysis, HB0044_420.pdf; ADHA) say remote-state boards/the Commission will set privilege fees, expected below full licence fees (UNCLEAR, not official).

7. Residence/practice in home state: the FAQ lists no requirement that the licensee reside or practise in the home state (it only requires the active unencumbered qualifying licence); "Enhances license portability when changing state of residence" is a stated benefit. No primary-state-of-residence rule was seen (contrast the Nurse Licensure Compact), but the model statute itself was not read: UNCLEAR.

General pattern drawn from these pages: (i) CE ranges 20-72 h per 2-3-year cycle; all pages read allow online/self-study/home-study CE at least in part, with caps in WA/AZ; (ii) CPR/BLS is the most common hands-on hurdle (mandatory in CO, KY, IA (explicitly hands-on); unclear in AZ, OR, WA, IN); (iii) inactive/retired statuses exist in CO (no CE while inactive), AZ (retired), IN (inactive), KY (retired), TX (retired); (iv) reactivation after expiry typically requires CE catch-up and fees; reinstatement windows of ~3 years appear in AZ and IN, after which full re-application (possibly re-examination) applies; (v) "active practice" periods (CO 1 yr, IN 2 of 3 yrs, WA 4 yrs, AZ practice hours) are prerequisites only for licensure by credentials/endorsement, not for renewing an exam-based licence. No page read imposes an in-state address or in-state practice as a renewal condition. Mandatory in-person CE requirements were not found in any page read (UNCLEAR for other states).

C. CANADA (provincial renewal norms)

- Saskatchewan (CDSS): licensing page read directly: "Proof of Professional Liability Insurance is a requirement for licensure. Contact CDSPI to apply" and applicants must provide "Confirmation of malpractice insurance of at least $3 [million]" (https://saskdentists.com/dentists/licensing/). Bylaws read directly: membership classes include "non-practising members" (incl. non-practising life members); renewal requires that the member "has paid the scheduled fees, completed all required forms, provided proof of the required insurance"; "all regular members, faculty members, and practising life members shall attain fifty" CE credit hours over successive three-year periods (Part 11; surplus not carried forward) (https://saskdentists.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/CDSS_Bylaws_and_Code_Of_Ethics_20148.pdf). Practice-hour currency requirement: none found in the extract (UNCLEAR). Immigration-status requirement: not checked on this page (reported earlier as not required — unverified).
- British Columbia (BCCOHP): the regulator's dentist renewal FAQ (search result; direct fetch was blocked) states: dentists must have "the minimum 900 practice hours in the three preceding calendar years" to renew as practising; 90 CE credits per 3-year cycle; a dentist "may hold non-practising status indefinitely, but must meet the Quality Assurance requirements to transfer and renew to a practising status"; a non-practising dentist "is not required to have active malpractice coverage" (https://portal.oralhealthbc.ca/registration-renewal/annual-renewal/faq-dentists — not read directly; UNCLEAR on exact current wording). Implication: a BC practising registration cannot be kept for decades without ~300 practice hours/year somewhere acceptable to the College; the realistic long-term holding status is non-practising with QA conditions on return.
- Ontario (RCDSO): all RCDSO URLs tried returned 404; PLP (Professional Liability Program) mandatory participation and renewal details therefore UNVERIFIED in this session (known fact: RCDSO also requires citizenship/PR/work permit, so Ontario is closed to the candidate anyway).
- Alberta (ADA&C): regulator site returned empty responses; the Dentists Profession Regulation (Alta Reg 254/2001, amended to 204/2025) was fetched but the text extract did not contain hours/currency language, so Alberta's "currency of practice" rule (reported ~900 h/3 yrs) is UNCLEAR.
- Manitoba: fee schedule 2025-26 exists (https://www.manitobadentist.ca/docs/default-source/default-document-library/schedule-a-fees/schedule-a-fees.pdf) but was not read; known fact: Manitoba requires immigration status.
Pattern: every Canadian province checked requires professional liability insurance for a practising licence (CDSPI typically), a 3-year CE/QA cycle (SK 50 h, BC 90 credits), and BC (and reportedly AB) adds a practice-hours currency test; non-practising categories exist (SK, BC) and in BC can be held indefinitely but do not permit practice and carry QA re-entry conditions.

## Aday için sonuçlar

1. The DDH Compact is not a usable route today: no compact privileges are being issued and the Commission estimates 18-24 months from activation, so nothing can be obtained before ~2027. It is in any case only a secondary benefit: it presupposes an active, unencumbered home-state licence in a member state (Colorado, Washington and Iowa are reported members among the DLOSCE states; Arizona, Oregon, Kentucky, Indiana are not, unverified). The adopted Nov-2025 clinical-assessment rule does not require a hands-on exam; it only excludes "licensure solely upon graduation" pathways, so a Colorado/Washington licence obtained on a DLOSCE basis should count — but a further 2026 draft rule may have changed this and should be re-checked. The candidate meets the CODA-predoctoral and National Board (INBDE) criteria; the home-state background check criterion must be satisfied at initial licensure. The compact imposes no residence/work-authorisation requirement on its face (model statute not read). A Canadian NDEB-only certification has no compact relevance.
2. For keeping a US licence active from Turkey for decades, Colorado is the strongest fit among the DLOSCE states: 30 h CE per two years, inactive and retired statuses that need no CE to maintain, reactivation by catching up 30 h, and no in-state address or practice requirement — but renewal in ACTIVE status requires current BLS, which is a short hands-on course obtainable at AHA international training centres (available in Turkey). Indiana (20 h/2 yrs, home-study accepted, inactive licence) and Oregon (40 h/2 yrs, online renewal) are also easy on paper (CPR status unclear). Kentucky and Iowa impose mandatory CPR (Iowa explicitly hands-on) every cycle; Washington has the heaviest CE (63 h/3 yrs) with caps on online self-study. Arizona renews triennially with a retired status. Avoid New York (residency plus citizenship/PR), California and Texas (hands-on exams) as these are both closed and hard.
3. Plan for decades abroad: hold the licence active only while CE/BLS are convenient, otherwise move to inactive/retired (CO, AZ, IN, KY) and watch reinstatement windows (AZ and IN show ~3-year windows before a lapse becomes a full re-application). "Active practice" rules affect only future licensure-by-credentials applications to other states, not renewal of the original licence, so the candidate's non-practice abroad does not jeopardise the home licence but will block most credentials-based transfers (CO 1 yr, IN 2 of 3 yrs, WA 4 yrs, AZ practice hours).
4. Canada: a practising licence in BC cannot be kept without ~900 practice hours per 3 years, and Saskatchewan (and all provinces) require malpractice insurance (CDSPI) for a practising licence; a non-practising register (BC indefinitely, SK) is the realistic long-term holding status, with QA/re-entry conditions. Canadian registration therefore scores worse than a US inactive-status licence on criterion (3), even where immigration status is not required (BC/SK, unverified).
