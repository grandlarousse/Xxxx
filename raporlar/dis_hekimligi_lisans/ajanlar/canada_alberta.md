# Alberta (Canada) — ajan bulgusu

Düzenleyici: College of Dental Surgeons of Alberta (CDSA) – regulator under the Health Professions Act (HPA) and the Dentists Profession Regulation, Alta Reg 254/2001 (amended to AR 204/2025) — https://cdsab.ca/

**Teori-only rota:** yes · **Göçmenlik engeli:** high · **Uzaktan sürdürme:** hard · **Güven:** medium

## Sınav yolları

- **Main register – accredited graduate (NDEB Certification + CDSA Ethics & Jurisprudence Exam)** (el becerisi: hayır): Dentists Profession Regulation s.3(1): applicant must (a) have a DDS/DMD 'from a dentistry program approved by the Council' and (b) 'have successfully completed the registration examination approved by the Council and the ethics and jurisprudence examination approved by the Council'. CDSA Application Requirements page: 'A degree from a dentistry program accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation of Canada or the American Dental Association; otherwise, complete the NDEB Equivalency Process' – so a CODA-accredited Yeditepe graduate is treated as an accredited graduate. Registration examination = NDEB Certification (now the single computer-based Virtual OSCE; no hands-on). CDSA Ethics & Jurisprudence Exam: 'administered online approximately every two weeks... multiple choice format but not open book... graded automatically with results provided immediately... passing grade is 75% or higher... 90 minutes'. s.3(2): requirements must have been completed within 5 years of a complete application, otherwise demonstrate current competence. No regional/ADEX/CRDTS style hands-on exam exists in Alberta. Hands-on BLS/CPR (not a clinical exam) is nevertheless required at application and annually.
- **Equivalent jurisdiction (labour mobility) – s.5** (el becerisi: hayır): 'An applicant currently registered in good standing in another jurisdiction recognized by the Council under section 28(2)(b) of the Act as having substantially equivalent registration requirements may be registered on the appropriate register.' Transfer from another Canadian province (Certificate of Registration + Letter of Good Standing, valid 8 weeks). Not available to a new graduate who holds no licence; still requires the Alberta E&J exam and the identity/immigration documents.
- **Equivalent competence – s.6 (Registrar discretion)** (el becerisi: hayır): For applicants who do not meet s.3 or s.5: Registrar may find qualifications 'substantially equivalent' and 'may require the applicant to undergo an examination, testing and assessment activity' and 'may direct the applicant to undergo any education or training activities'. Discretionary; may involve hands-on assessment (e.g. CDSA Supportive Placement/Pathway Program). Not needed by an accredited-programme graduate with NDEB certification.
- **Courtesy register – s.7** (el becerisi: hayır): Short-term register ('for up to 30 days') for dentists registered elsewhere coming to Alberta for a specific purpose; fee $500 per calendar year. Not an initial full licence and requires existing registration elsewhere.
- **Education and research register – s.4** (el becerisi: hayır): Requires a letter from the Dean, Faculty of Medicine & Dentistry, University of Alberta confirming employment as a dentist there; practice limited to teaching/research duties. Not available without U of A employment (which itself needs work authorisation).
- **PGY-1 / residency route** (el becerisi: hayır): Not a licensure pathway in Alberta: no residency substitutes for NDEB certification; GP residents register on the main register with pro-rated fees ($2,807) but still need NDEB certification and work authorisation.

## Teori-only açıklaması
Dentists Profession Regulation (AR 254/2001) s.3(1): 'An applicant for registration as a regulated member on the main register must (a) have a degree of doctor of dental surgery or doctor of dental medicine from a dentistry program approved by the Council, and (b) have successfully completed the registration examination approved by the Council and the ethics and jurisprudence examination approved by the Council.' The registration examination approved by Council is NDEB Certification (CDSA requires 'National Dental Examination Board (NDEB) of Canada proof of certification'); for graduates of accredited programmes (CDSA: 'accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation of Canada or the American Dental Association') NDEB certification is the computer-based Virtual OSCE only. The CDSA Ethics & Jurisprudence Exam is 'administered online... multiple choice format', 90 minutes, pass mark 75%. No manikin/patient clinical exam and no residency exist in the Alberta rules. CAVEAT: the exam route is theory-only, but registration still requires hands-on HCP/CPR (BLS) with AED certification and, decisively for this candidate, proof of Canadian citizenship, permanent residency or an immigration authorisation permitting practice of dentistry, which the candidate cannot provide (see immigration).

## DLOSCE notları
Not applicable: Alberta does not use the US DLOSCE or any US regional exam. The computer-based exam that satisfies s.3(1)(b) is NDEB Certification (Virtual OSCE at Prometric). The integration of DLOSCE into ADEX is irrelevant to Alberta.

PGY-1 kabul: False

A visitor on a tourist/business visa has no authorisation to engage in the practice of dentistry, so cannot satisfy the proof-of-identity/status requirement. Citizenship or PR is not strictly required (a work permit authorising dentistry suffices) but some form of work authorisation is. There is no non-practising/inactive register category in the regulation (s.2: main register; education and research register; courtesy register), so there is no registration-only category that could be held without status. No in-Alberta address requirement was found; CDSA requires up-to-date contact information only.

## Yüz yüze adımlar
(1) Ethics & Jurisprudence Exam: 'administered online approximately every two weeks'; after submitting the Applicant Portal application the candidate emails registration@cdsab.ca for dates; the exam page is password-protected and 'The password for the exam will be issued the day of the scheduled exam' – appears to be sat remotely online; whether it can be written from outside Canada/time-zone constraints not confirmed. (2) Mandatory online Orientation & Registration session after approval ('only after completing this session may Applicants begin work the following day') – online. (3) Notarisation: proof of identity, degree certificate and the Statutory Declaration must be 'notarized or certified by a Commissioner of Oaths'; FAQ: 'Any lawyer who advertises themselves as a Notary Public can notarize documents. Members of the Legislative Assembly, Members of Parliament and Judges can also notarize' – acceptability of a foreign notary not stated. (4) Criminal Record & Vulnerable Sector Check 'performed within the last 12 months' with CDSA consent form and an 'RCMP Vulnerable Sector Check Letter Request' – Canadian vulnerable-sector checks are issued by Canadian police for the applicant's residential address (fingerprints only if a name/DOB match); whether a foreign police certificate is accepted for an applicant residing abroad is not stated. No Live Scan/fingerprint card process is published. (5) BLS/CPR with AED: copy of valid certificate; CDSA accepts only courses 'that include a hands-on component... which must be completed in-person' (online-only not accepted) – can be done anywhere but needs physical attendance. (6) No interview. Processing times 'will vary'; Certificates of Standing valid 8 weeks, CRC/VSC valid one year.

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
Online application via CDSA Applicant Portal (Thentia); notarised proof of identity (Canadian ID or CIC authorisation permitting practice of dentistry); notarised Statutory Declaration; notarised copy of dental degree (or sent directly by university) and transcripts; NDEB certificate copy; BLS/HCP-CPR with AED certificate (hands-on course); Criminal Record Check + Vulnerable Sector Check within 12 months; Certificates of Standing/Letters of Good Standing from every jurisdiction ever registered (valid 8 weeks); consent to release information; English Language Proficiency – required 'when the dental school... was conducted in a language other than English' (Yeditepe: unless CDSA accepts the programme as English-medium, an ELP test per the CDSA English Language Proficiency policy will be needed); certified translations of non-English documents; CDSA Ethics & Jurisprudence Exam (online, MCQ, closed-book, 90 min, 75% pass; failing candidates are offered a rewrite); mandatory online Orientation & Registration session; professional liability insurance is bundled into the practice permit through the CDSA Professional Liability Program (Navacord). s.3(2): education/exam requirements must have been completed within 5 years of a complete application or the applicant must demonstrate current competence.

## İlk ücretler
2026 CDSA fee structure (Bylaw 23, billing cycle Jan 1–Dec 31): Application $500; First Time Registration – General Practitioner $1,000; Practice Permit pro-rated: $4,725 (Jan–Mar), $4,371 (Apr–Jun), $2,807 (Jul–Sep), $2,453 (Oct–Dec); New Graduates registering June 1–30 $2,807; Courtesy Register $500/calendar year; Certificate/Letter of Standing $375 each; Registration Appeal $5,000. Permit fee includes the professional liability insurance premium (partial refund on cancellation). Not shown separately: E&J exam fee (none listed), CRC/VSC and notary costs borne by applicant. Plus NDEB fees (external). Timeline: 'Application processing times will vary'; E&J exam offered about every two weeks.

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Annual; practice permit runs January 1 – December 31 (billing cycle); renewal due before December 31 each year.
- **renewal_fee:** 2026: Main Register permit $4,725.00 if paid prior to December 31, $5,225.00 after December 31 (includes bundled professional liability insurance premium via the CDSA Professional Liability Program/Navacord). 60-day late payment fee $100 also listed.
- **ce_hours:** Not verified – the public Continuing Education page refers to a 'two-year reporting cycle' under the CDSA Continuing Competence Program and to a separate CCP document describing 'established continuing competence requirements for annual practice permit renewal'; the credit-hour minimum is in that document, which was not read. Mandatory 'Regulatory-Directed Learning' items: (1) HCP/CPR (BLS) with AED, hands-on, yearly; (2) Protecting Patients from Sexual Abuse and Misconduct course 'required as part of every two-year reporting cycle'.
- **ce_online_ok:** Partially. CDSA lists Self-Study Providers and online CE is self-reported through the Registrant Portal; the sexual-abuse course is online. But HCP/CPR (BLS) with AED must include an in-person hands-on component ('On-line courses that do not have a hands-on in-person components are not acceptable') – so 100% online is NOT possible. Any caps on self-study credits are in the CCP document (not verified).
- **cpr_required:** Yes – 'All registrants of the CDSA must have yearly HCP/CPR including AED or equivalent certification... only accepts... training courses that include a hands-on component. The hands-on component refers to a practical element, which must be completed in-person.' Updated certificate must be uploaded with every annual practice permit payment.
- **practice_hours_required:** None found for renewal. For (re)registration, s.3(2) requires that education/exam requirements were completed within 5 years of a complete application, otherwise the applicant must 'demonstrate... that the applicant is currently competent to practice'.
- **address_or_residency_required:** No in-province address requirement found; registrants must keep contact information (name, address, phone, email) current in the Registrant Portal. However DPR s.11 allows the Registrar at renewal to require 'evidence of being a Canadian citizen or being lawfully permitted to work in Canada', photo ID and English proficiency.
- **insurance_required:** Yes, mandatory. 'All of Alberta's professional health regulators are required to ensure their registrants have professional liability insurance... The Professional Liability Program integrates insurance coverage with registration as a dentist in Alberta' – premium is built into the practice permit fee; cancellation of registration requires cancelling the Navacord policy; 5 years of Extended Reporting Period coverage included on retirement, extendable to 10 years.
- **inactive_status:** None. The Dentists Profession Regulation s.2 has only three categories: main register, education and research register, courtesy register – no non-practising/inactive/retired register. The only option is 'Cancellation of Practice Permit' (for 'retirement, moving out of the province or outside of Canada, sabbatical, maternity leave, or post-graduate studies') via the Registrant Portal; until the signed cancellation is received 'you are considered a registrant with the same responsibilities and obligations of meeting continuing competency requirements, maintaining malpractice insurance and payment of annual dues'. (A 'Volunteer Register $100' line appears in the 2026 fee schedule but no eligibility rules were found.)
- **reactivation:** After cancellation a dentist must re-apply for registration (new application $500 + first-time registration $1,000 + permit; Certificates of Standing from all jurisdictions; new CRC/VSC; current CPR; E&J exam presumably again). DPR s.3(2): if the education/exam requirements were completed more than 5 years before the new application, the applicant must demonstrate current competence, and under s.6 the Registrar 'may require the applicant to undergo an examination, testing and assessment activity' and 'may direct... education or training activities' (e.g. supervised Supportive Placement Program). Specific refresher/re-examination rules for long-lapsed dentists were not published on the pages read.
- **remote_maintainability:** hard
- **details:** Keeping an Alberta registration active from abroad requires: an annual permit of about CAD 4,725 (bundled liability insurance), yearly in-person hands-on BLS/CPR, two-year CE cycle with online sexual-abuse course and self-reported credits, and potentially renewed proof of citizenship/work authorisation under s.11(b). There is no inactive or non-practising category, so the only alternative to full annual fees is cancelling the permit and re-applying later (subject to the 5-year currency rule in s.3(2) and possible competence assessment). For a candidate without Canadian work authorisation the question is moot because initial registration is not available.
