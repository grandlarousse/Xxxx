# Saskatchewan (Canada) — ajan bulgusu

Düzenleyici: College of Dental Surgeons of Saskatchewan (CDSS), operating under The Dental Disciplines Act (SS 1997, c D-4.1) and the CDSS Regulatory Bylaws (2024, approved by Council 25 Jul 2024, Minister of Health 6 Nov 2024) — https://saskdentists.com/

**Teori-only rota:** yes · **Göçmenlik engeli:** none · **Uzaktan sürdürme:** moderate · **Güven:** medium

## Sınav yolları

- **NDEB Certification Process (graduates of CDAC-recognised/accredited programmes) + CDSS General Dentist registration and full practising licence** (el becerisi: hayır): The standard and only ordinary route for a new graduate. CDSS Regulatory Bylaw 2(7)(a): the applicant must (ii) 'provide a notarized copy of a degree from a general dentistry school, faculty, or college that is accredited or considered accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation of Canada' and (iv) 'provide a currently valid certificate of qualification from the National Dental Examining Board of Canada for general dentists'. The NDEB Certification Process for graduates of accredited programmes now consists of the Virtual OSCE only (200 computer-delivered items: 50 single-answer MCQs + 150 case-based questions; NDEB: 'An NDEB certificate is issued upon successful completion of the Virtual OSCE, and after proof of graduation is provided to the NDEB office'). The Written Examination is no longer listed as a separate component (NDEB fees page lists only Virtual OSCE $1,750 + $450 application fee; the FAQ refers to candidates who 'already' took the Written Examination and OSCE). No manikin/patient exam at any stage. CDSS then requires its own Jurisprudence and Ethics examination (CC&QA Program 1(c): 'Applicants for registration are required to write and pass an examination in jurisprudence and ethics'; Schedule I 'Provincial Eligibility Fee (Jurisprudence Exam/Competency) $375'), a criminal record check with vulnerable sector screening, good-character declaration, $3M liability insurance and fees. NDEB eligibility category 'Received a BDS/DDS/DMD from an accredited dental program within the last 5 years' (also 'more than 5 years ago'); NDEB's accredited-programme list explicitly includes 'CODA Accredited Dental Programs (US and international)'.
- **NDEB Equivalency Process (for graduates of NON-accredited programmes) + CDSS registration** (el becerisi: evet): Bylaw 2(7)(a)(iii): degree from a non-accredited school plus successful completion of 'the National Dental Examining Board equivalency examination process' (AFK, ACJ and the NDECC, which includes hands-on clinical skills components). Not needed by this candidate if Yeditepe is treated as accredited; listed for completeness.
- **Labour mobility / transfer from another Canadian province (DDA s.20(1.1); Bylaw 2(5)(b))** (el becerisi: hayır): A dentist 'registered as... the equivalent of a dentist in good standing pursuant to the legislation of another jurisdiction in Canada' can be registered on providing consent for release of information, evidence of current certification and good standing from each jurisdiction. Not available to a new graduate who holds no Canadian licence; whether hands-on was needed depends on the original province's route.
- **Academic Registrant (restricted practising licence)** (el becerisi: hayır): Bylaw 2(9): degree in dentistry, entitlement to practise in a jurisdiction outside Saskatchewan, and an appointment to a teaching position at the University of Saskatchewan College of Dentistry or Saskatchewan Polytechnic confirmed by the Dean; practice limited to that institution; becomes void on termination of employment. No NDEB certificate required but requires employment (and therefore Canadian work authorisation in practice). Not relevant to this candidate.
- **Locum Registrant (3-month restricted licence, once per year) / Temporary Registrant (CE presentations, research, forensic team)** (el becerisi: hayır): Bylaw 2(11) locum: same education + NDEB certificate requirements as 2(7), licence expires after a consecutive three-month period, eligible only once per year ($1,500). Bylaw 2(12) temporary: degree + entitlement to practise outside Saskatchewan, solely for presenting courses/clinical presentations/research at U of S or for forensic identification ($650). Neither is a durable licence.
- **Nonpracticing Registrant (registration only, no licence)** (el becerisi: hayır): Bylaw 2(13): must satisfy the general qualifications in 2(5) (application, good character, recognised education, 'any examination requirements prescribed in these bylaws', CRC, fees) and have the reason for registration being SOLELY (ii) 'providing dental treatment or services in a jurisdiction outside of Saskatchewan wherein the provision of such treatment or services is under the auspices of a humanitarian project or organized dentistry association and wherein that jurisdiction requires the applicant to have a status of registration in another jurisdiction' or (iii) 'a leave of absence with respect to medical, education, or sabbatical'. 'Nonpracticing registrants shall not be issued a licence and shall not be entitled to practice dentistry in the Province of Saskatchewan.' Fee $100/yr; no insurance required (Bylaw 3(3)). There is NO DLOSCE, ADEX/regional-exam, PGY-1, portfolio or licensure-by-credentials pathway in Saskatchewan.

## Teori-only açıklaması
Saskatchewan has no hands-on clinical examination and no residency requirement for graduates of accredited programmes. The Dental Disciplines Act s.20(1) lets Council register and license a person who 'has successfully completed: (i)... an educational program in dentistry recognized by the council of the College of Dental Surgeons of Saskatchewan' and '(d) has successfully completed any examination requirements prescribed in the bylaws'. CDSS Regulatory Bylaw 2(7)(a) (General Dentist Registrants) requires the applicant to '(ii) provide a notarized copy of a degree from a general dentistry school, faculty, or college that is accredited or considered accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation of Canada; or (iii) provide a notarized copy of a degree in a non-accredited general dentistry school... and has successfully completed the National Dental Examining Board equivalency examination process; and (iv) provide a currently valid certificate of qualification from the National Dental Examining Board of Canada for general dentists; and (v) have been approved by the Registrar for registration.' Bylaw 2(7)(b): 'Upon fulfilling the criteria according to subsection 2(6) and clause 2(7)(a), a General Dentist Registrant shall be issued a full practicing licence to practice dentistry in the Province of Saskatchewan.' The only examination the bylaws/policies prescribe beyond the NDEB certificate is the CDSS Jurisprudence and Ethics examination (CDSS Continuing Competency and Quality Assurance Program, s.1(c): 'Applicants for registration are required to write and pass an examination in jurisprudence and ethics'). The NDEB certificate for accredited-programme graduates is obtained by passing the computer-based Virtual OSCE (NDEB: 'The Virtual OSCE consists of 200 questions. There are 50 standard single answer multiple choice questions and 150 case based questions'; 'An NDEB certificate is issued upon successful completion of the Virtual OSCE, and after proof of graduation is provided'). Caveat: the route depends on Yeditepe being 'accredited or considered accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation of Canada' - NDEB's own accredited-programme list links 'CODA Accredited Dental Programs (US and international)'; another agent is verifying CDAC/NDEB treatment of Yeditepe.

## DLOSCE notları
Not applicable. Neither the Dental Disciplines Act, the CDSS Regulatory Bylaws (2024) nor the CDSS application form mention the DLOSCE, ADEX, INBDE or any US regional examination; the only accepted entry credential for a general dentist is 'a currently valid certificate of qualification from the National Dental Examining Board of Canada' (Bylaw 2(7)(a)(iv)). The NDEB notes the reverse flow only: 'NDEB certification or components of the certification process are also being accepted for licensure in some US states.' DLOSCE scores (standalone or taken with ADEX) have no role in Saskatchewan.

## Yüz yüze adımlar
(1) NDEB Virtual OSCE: computer-based but delivered at test sites on fixed dates ('UNITED STATES CANADA' sittings 23 Nov 2026, 1 Mar 2027, 20 May 2027; 'CANADA' 22 Nov 2027; separate Australia/Ireland sittings) - requires a short trip to Canada or the US (visitor status suffices; no Turkish site). NDEB identification is a notarized photocopy of a passport mailed to Ottawa; proof of graduation for accredited programmes outside Canada is an official transcript sent directly by the university or via MyCreds/Parchment/National Student Clearinghouse/My eQuals - all doable from abroad. (2) CDSS application form: the applicant's declaration is an AFFIDAVIT 'To be signed in front of a Notary Public or Commissioner of Oaths' with seal ('Taken and declared before me in the City of ___, Province of ___') - can presumably be sworn before a notary abroad, but CDSS acceptance of a foreign notary is not stated; notarized/certified copies of diploma(s), NDEB certificate and government photo ID are also required (notary abroad possible). (3) Criminal Record Check with Vulnerable Sector Screening: 'from all jurisdictions where you have been residing in the past 12 months', originals, dated within 3 months of application; the CDSS policy describes obtaining it 'from the local police service or Royal Canadian Mounted Police' (up to six weeks) and contemplates an 'extended fingerprint process for the Vulnerable Sector Screening'. A Canadian VSS is only issued by the police service of the applicant's Canadian residence, so a Turkey-resident applicant would have to supply a Turkish police certificate/equivalent - how CDSS handles non-Canadian residents is not published. (4) CDSS Jurisprudence and Ethics examination ($375 'Provincial Eligibility Fee'): required for all applicants; delivery format (online vs in-person at the Saskatoon office) is not published. (5) No interview is required unless the CRC is 'not clear' (then the Criminal Record Check Review Committee may invite the applicant to appear or accept a written submission). (6) No routine fingerprinting, no state-side Live Scan. Processing: 'allow a minimum of 10 business days for processing once all documents are received'; application and certificates of standing become stale after three months. Email submission of the application is accepted ('Or email to cdss@saskdentists.com'), with originals of the CRC mailed.

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
Jurisprudence and Ethics examination (CDSS CC&QA Program 1(c); fee $375 under Schedule I 'Provincial Eligibility Fee (Jurisprudence Exam/Competency)'; format unpublished). Criminal Record Check incl. Vulnerable Sector Screening dated within 3 months (CRC Policy for Initial Applications, effective 1 Jan 2024). Professional liability insurance of at least $3,000,000 per occurrence with a Council-approved carrier (Bylaw 3(1)); CDSPI is the default and 'will notify the CDSS when they receive your application'. CPR: application question 33 'Are you current with cardiopulmonary resuscitation life support training?'; CDSS CPR standard: 'As a mandatory requirement for licensure, all dentists are required to remain current with respect to cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR Level - C) and automated external defibrillator (AED) training certification' (CPR-C recertify every 3 years). Attestations that the applicant has read the Code of Ethics, CDA Principles of Ethics, Bylaw s.4(3) standards of conduct, CC&QA Program, Sedation, Radiation, IPC, Informed Consent, Advertising standards, HIPA. No English-language test is mentioned anywhere (NDEB notes some DRAs may require language evidence; CDSS does not list one). No French requirement. Timeline: NDEB application deadlines roughly 2-4 months before each sitting (e.g. exam 1 Mar 2027, application deadline 5 Jan 2027, registration deadline 18 Jan 2027); NDEB says certification takes 6-18 months from eligibility; CDSS minimum 10 business days after a complete file; CDSS will not issue a licence while any professional-conduct case is open elsewhere.

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Annual. Bylaw 2(18)(a): 'The licensure of each registrant of the College shall automatically expire on January 15 of the following year for which the licence was issued'; renewal fee payable by 15 January; Registrar must give at least 45 days' notice of the fee.
- **renewal_fee:** $2,850 per calendar year (Schedule I effective 1 Jan 2027; $1,425 for a licence first issued Sep-Dec; New Graduate Registrant $1,425). Late-payment penalty $1,000; penalty for late submission of CE credits for renewal $1,000. Nonpracticing Registrant (incl. 'Out of Province') $100/yr. Advanced-skill authorisations extra (e.g. sedation $50-$500, CBCT $50, neuromodulator $250, clinic facility permit $250 - only if applicable).
- **ce_hours:** 90 credit hours over a rolling three-year cycle (CDSS Continuing Education Policy, amended Sept 2026, s.3(1): 'regular registrants must accumulate 90 credit hours over a rolling three-year cycle period'); academic full-time 45, part-time 22.5. New registrants licensed after 1 May start the cycle on their licensing date (up to 3 years + 7 months for the first 90 credits). Specialists: 50% specialty-related. Credits self-reported through the member portal with attendance certificates; transcripts from other jurisdictions not accepted as verification.
- **ce_online_ok:** Yes, in practice 100% can be done remotely. CE Policy 3(1)(a): attendance at courses approved by the College counts 'hour for hour credit ("live online" lectures will be counted as "live in-person" lectures)' with no cap; (f) 'educational tapes or journals (media & online, self-study, webinar courses) approved and evaluated by the College - a maximum of 15 credit hours per calendar year'; (g) review of CDSS bylaws and standards up to 4/yr; surveys 3+3 credits; published articles 2-5 each. No in-person component is mandated, although the Continuing Education Committee may disapprove courses 'of questionable content' and 'Registrants of the College must participate in any regulatory continuing education course that the Council deems mandatory' (CE Policy 6(5)).
- **cpr_required:** Yes. CE Policy 6(1) and CPR standard (Jan 2025): 'all dentists are required to remain current with respect to cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR Level - C) and automated external defibrillator (AED) training certification'; 'CPR C = Recertify after 3 years' (BLS-HCP annually only if sedation-authorised). CPR-C is normally a hands-on skills course; whether a non-Canadian provider's certificate is accepted is not stated.
- **practice_hours_required:** None for renewal. Bylaw 2(18)(a)(i) renewal conditions are: good standing, renewal forms, liability insurance, 'satisfy current licence requirements, as amended from time to time', pay outstanding amounts and the annual fee. However Bylaw 2(5)(c) applies to any APPLICANT for registration 'who has not been practicing dentistry for a period of two or more consecutive years preceding the date of application' (explanation of absence, disclosure of limitations, 'a detailed return to practice plan', investigation fee ($375), and 'such assessments, examinations, and courses as prescribed by Council'), and the form asks 'Have you been actively practicing dentistry / treating patients in the last 2 years?'.
- **address_or_residency_required:** No. No residency requirement for registrants; the form accepts a 'Province/State' and 'Postal/Zip Code' mailing address, and Schedule I expressly prices a 'Nonpracticing Registrant - Out of Province'. The only residency item is the informational question about residing in Saskatchewan more than 183 days a year. Registrants must keep the College informed of any change of practice/contact details (form q.62).
- **insurance_required:** Yes for every licence category: Bylaw 3(1): 'All General Dentist Registrants, Specialist Dentist Registrants, Academic Registrants, Locum Registrants, and Temporary Registrants... shall obtain and provide verification of professional liability insurance... in the amount not less than three million dollars ($3,000,000) per occurrence with a carrier(s) approved by Council.' Bylaw 3(3): Nonpracticing Registrants 'are not required to obtain any insurance'. CDSPI (default carrier) eligibility is simply 'A dentist who is licensed or possesses a certificate to practice dentistry in a province or territory of Canada other than... Alberta, Ontario or Quebec'; 2026 premium for $3M about $1,685 member / $2,107 non-member; note CDSPI coverage does NOT apply to services rendered outside Canada where a dental statute exists, so it is purely a cost of holding the licence while abroad.
- **inactive_status:** 'Nonpracticing Registrant' (Bylaw 2(13)): $100/yr ('Non-License Annual Fee for Nonpracticing Registrant' and '- Out of Province'), no insurance, no licence, no entitlement to practise in Saskatchewan. Eligibility is narrow: must satisfy the general qualifications of 2(5) (incl. examination requirements and CRC) AND have the reason for registration being SOLELY (ii) providing dental treatment/services abroad 'under the auspices of a humanitarian project or organized dentistry association' where that jurisdiction requires registration elsewhere, or (iii) 'a leave of absence with respect to medical, education, or sabbatical'. No maximum duration is stated (only a professional-corporation permit is capped at two years); the Registrar must cancel the registration 'when directed to do so by Council'. CE obligations for nonpracticing registrants are not spelled out; the CE Policy appendix implies CE attaches to licensed members ('Members who choose to maintain a license during any type of leave are subject to maintaining their CE credits'; those 'un-licensed... for more than 4 months automatically start a new cycle when they re-license'). A Turkish-resident dentist working in ordinary private practice in Turkey would not obviously fit either stated reason.
- **reactivation:** From nonpracticing or lapsed status the dentist re-applies for General Dentist registration/licensure under Bylaws 2(5)-2(7) (new application form, CRC, insurance, fees; certificates of standing from every jurisdiction). If the applicant 'has not been practicing dentistry for a period of two or more consecutive years preceding the date of application', Bylaw 2(5)(c) adds: explanation of absence, disclosure of self-identified conditions/limitations, 'a detailed return to practice plan', the prescribed investigation fee (Schedule I Investigation Fee $375), and completion of 'such assessments, examinations, and courses as prescribed by Council' - Council has discretion to require a clinical assessment/refresher; no fixed list is published. If the dentist has practised continuously abroad, 2(5)(c) is not triggered. Unpaid-renewal lapse: Bylaw 2(18)(e) lets the Registrar renew 'upon payment of the annual fee and the prescribed penalty for late payment' ($1,000); CE shortfall penalty $1,000. Expelled members must apply for reinstatement under DDA s.41.
- **remote_maintainability:** moderate
- **details:** Keeping a FULL practising licence active from Turkey is legally possible: nothing in the Act, bylaws or policies requires Canadian residence, a Saskatchewan address, practice hours or in-person CE. The recurring burden is: about $2,850/yr licence fee + roughly $1,700-2,100/yr liability insurance (mandatory even if never practising in Canada), 90 CE credits per rolling 3 years (achievable online: live-online lectures count hour-for-hour without cap; self-study capped at 15/yr), CPR-C/AED kept current (3-yearly, hands-on course), a Criminal Record Check with Vulnerable Sector Screening every five years (CRC Policy for Renewal; obtaining a Canadian VSS from abroad is problematic), renewal by 15 January each year and compliance with any Council-mandated regulatory course. The cheap alternative, Nonpracticing Registrant at $100/yr with no insurance and no licence, exists but its eligibility wording (humanitarian/organised-dentistry work abroad or medical/education/sabbatical leave) is narrow and the Registrar decides; and returning to a full licence after 2+ years without practising triggers the Bylaw 2(5)(c) return-to-practice investigation with possible assessments/examinations. Because of the insurance cost, the hands-on CPR, the five-yearly VSS and the discretionary return-to-practice assessment, the rating is 'moderate' rather than 'easy'.
