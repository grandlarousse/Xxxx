# New Brunswick (Canada) — ajan bulgusu

Düzenleyici: New Brunswick Dental Society (NBDS) / Société dentaire du Nouveau-Brunswick (regulator under An Act Respecting The New Brunswick Dental Society, 1985, c.73) — https://www.nbdent.ca/licensing-for-dentists-and-dental-assistants/

**Teori-only rota:** yes · **Göçmenlik engeli:** high · **Uzaktan sürdürme:** moderate · **Güven:** medium

## Sınav yolları

- **NDEB Certification (Virtual OSCE, computer-based) + NBDS registration** (el becerisi: hayır): Standard route for graduates of CDAC/CODA-accredited programmes (Yeditepe grads after 1 Feb 2024 qualify per CDAC/NDEB). NBDS Rule 1(2): applicant must '(i) Hold a degree from an accredited school of dentistry or complete the NDEB equivalency process if graduated from a non accredited school. (ii) Hold the National Dental Examining Board of Canada certificate.' The NDEB certificate for accredited graduates is obtained by passing only the computer-based Virtual OSCE (GNB guide, March 2026: 'six-hour computer-based examination ... at Prometric test centres in Canada and the United States'). No NB jurisprudence exam, no regional/manikin exam and no residency is required. An in-person interview with the Registrar precedes licence approval (Rule 1(6)). Caveat: Rule 1(7)(iv): 'Any applicant who passed the NDEB exam over 3 years before applying for initial licensure in Canada must take the following NDEB exams: AFK, ACJ, NDECC within the 3 years prior to the licensing application' (NDECC is hands-on) - so apply within 3 years of the NDEB pass.
- **Labour mobility transfer (dentist already licensed and in good standing in another Canadian province/territory)** (el becerisi: hayır): Licensing page: 'If you are currently licensed and in good standing in another province or territory and wish to register to practice in New Brunswick under the Fair Registration Practices in Regulated Professions Act, please reach out to licensing@nbdent.ca'. Not available to a new graduate with no Canadian licence; also subject to Rule 1(7) lapse rules (30 CE hours per lapsed year if lapsed <3 years).
- **NDEB Equivalency Process (AFK + ACJ + NDECC) - non-accredited graduates only** (el becerisi: evet): Only for graduates of non-accredited programmes; NDECC is a 1.5-day hands-on clinical assessment in Ottawa. NOT needed for a Yeditepe (CODA-accredited) graduate, but it becomes mandatory under Rule 1(7)(iv) if the NDEB certificate is more than 3 years old at the time of first Canadian licensure application.
- **Temporary licence for educational or research purposes (educational register)** (el becerisi: hayır): Act s.11(1)(b) educational register / Rule 1(2)(iv) mentions 'temporary license for educational or research purpose'. Not a route to full practice licensure; details not published on the licensing page.
- **PGY-1 / residency in lieu of examination** (el becerisi: hayır): No such pathway exists in New Brunswick; the NDEB certificate is mandatory (Rule 1(2)(ii)).

## Teori-only açıklaması
NBDS Rule 1 'Requirement for Registration for Dentists' (Rules compilation dated Dec 2025), s.2: 'The dentist must meet the following requirements and provide proper documentations with the application proving eligibility: i) Hold a degree from an accredited school of dentistry or complete the NDEB equivalency process if graduated from a non accredited school. ii) Hold the the National Dental Examining Board of Canada certificate. iii) Apply for Malpractice Insurance ... minimum amount of three million dollars. iv) [good standing in other jurisdictions] v) Be legally allowed to work as a dentist in Canada vi) Do not pose risk to vulnerable population.' The licensing page lists the same documents (passport photo, certified ID, certified diploma, NDEB certificate, Vulnerable Sector Criminal Check). For accredited graduates the NDEB certificate requires only the computer-based Virtual OSCE (GNB guide Step 3: 'six-hour computer-based examination ... Successful candidates receive an NDEB certificate'). No hands-on regional exam, no residency, and no NB jurisprudence exam were found in Rule 1 or on the licensing page. Therefore the examination route is theory-only; the blocking factor is Rule 1(2)(v) (work authorisation), not the exams. Time limit: Rule 1(7)(iv) requires AFK/ACJ/NDECC (hands-on) if the NDEB exam was passed more than 3 years before the initial Canadian licence application.

## DLOSCE notları
The ADA/JCNDE DLOSCE and ADEX are not mentioned anywhere in the NB Dental Act, NBDS Rules or licensing page; they have no role in New Brunswick. The required OSCE is the NDEB Virtual OSCE (computer-based, Prometric), which is the sole examination for graduates of accredited programmes. Rule 1(7)(iv): NDEB pass older than 3 years at the time of first Canadian licensure application triggers AFK, ACJ and NDECC (hands-on).

## Yüz yüze adımlar
(1) NDEB Virtual OSCE: computer-based at Prometric centres in Canada and the US only - requires travel (visitor status suffices for the exam). (2) In-person meeting with the Registrar: Rule 1(6) 'The applicant must submit all documents to the Registrar prior to the scheduling of an interview at which time the license will be approved'; licensing page: 'the applicant will be required to attend an in-person meeting with the registrar prior to license approbation' (Fredericton, NB) - can be done during a short visit once all documents are in. (3) Vulnerable Sector Criminal Check from the RCMP or a local (Canadian) police service - Canadian police generally issue vulnerable sector checks only to residents of their jurisdiction, so obtaining one from abroad or as a visitor is doubtful (unverified). (4) Certified copies of ID and diploma (certification by a notary/commissioner/lawyer; can be done abroad). (5) No fingerprinting requirement published (though a vulnerable sector check may require fingerprints if a name/DOB match occurs). (6) No jurisprudence exam. (7) Malpractice insurance binder via CDSPI (by phone/online). (8) Application is online in English or French (Rule 1(1)).

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
No language proficiency test ('The NBDS does not require a language proficiency test for registration' - GNB guide; application may be in either official language, Rule 1(1)). No jurisprudence exam found. Malpractice insurance: Rule 1(2)(iii) - apply under the CDA master policy administered by CDSPI, minimum $3 million; binder effective on licensure date. Vulnerable Sector Criminal Check (RCMP/local police). Letter of good standing from any other jurisdiction where licensed. CE for initial licensure: Rule 2 'CE requirement initial licensure or reinstatement' - any applicant 'not currently licensed with another Canadian Regulatory Authority, shall submit ... proof of thirty (30) hours of continuing education credits per each year or the pro-rated portion thereof, in which the applicant has not been licensed and in good standing' (new graduates are exempt until graduation, then pro-rated). CPR: Rule (emergency equipment/CPR) item 7: 'Minimal CPR requirement for dentists: BLS for health care providers and oxygen training (Annual certification)'. NDEB certificate must be less than 3 years old at first Canadian licensure application (Rule 1(7)(iv)). Timeline: NDEB Virtual OSCE offered multiple times per year; NBDS approval follows receipt of all documents and the Registrar interview (no published processing time).

## İlk ücretler
NBDS 2026: $3,890.03 CAD total = one-time registration fee $150.00 + annual licence fee $3,167.90 + CDA membership fee $497.50 + HST. Pro-rating: registrants licensed after 1 September pay 60% of the annual licence fee for the first year; recent graduates licensed after 30 June pay 50%. Annual fees are not refunded; licences are valid until 31 December. Separate: NDEB Virtual OSCE fee (see ndeb-bned.ca), CDSPI malpractice premium, vulnerable sector check, certified copies. (GNB guide: total ~$15,850 CAD applies to the NDEB Equivalency route, not to accredited graduates.)

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Annual; licence expires 31 December each year (licensing page: 'licenses are valid until December 31st').
- **renewal_fee:** 2026 annual licence fee $3,167.90 + CDA membership $497.50 + HST (approx. $3,665 + HST per year; the $150 registration fee is one-time).
- **ce_hours:** 60 NBDS-recognised CE credits per two-year cycle for general dentists (Rule 2 / CE Requirements March 2025); minimum 20 credits 'Clinical Dental'; caps per cycle: Dental Practice Management max 20, Dental/Medical (first aid, CPR, ACLS, emergencies) max 10, wellness/service-type category max 12. Specialists: at least 50% in the specialty. Non-accredited clinical courses count for 1/3 of credits; reading with a corrected quiz = 1/2 credit per hour. Credits must be reported through the NBDS online account with supporting documents. New graduates exempt until graduation, then pro-rated.
- **ce_online_ok:** 'Online courses' are an expressly listed learning modality and accredited sources include ADA/AGD/Dentaltown/universities, so the 60 credits appear obtainable online/self-study from abroad; no explicit cap on online credits was found in the March 2025 CE document (not read in full - low-residual uncertainty). The only quasi-live component is the separate annual BLS/CPR certification.
- **cpr_required:** Yes - NBDS Rule on emergency preparedness item 7: 'Minimal CPR requirement for dentists: BLS for health care providers and oxygen training (Annual certification)'. BLS-HCP certification normally includes a hands-on skills session; whether NBDS verifies it at renewal (vs. office inspection) is not stated.
- **practice_hours_required:** None found for renewal. Lapse rules apply only if the licence is allowed to lapse: Rule 1(7)(iii) <3 years lapsed = 30 CE hours per lapsed year; (ii) >3 years = Registrar-set conditions.
- **address_or_residency_required:** No NB residency or in-province address requirement found; the register records the member's name and address (Act s.11(1)(a)). Whether continued Canadian work authorisation is checked at each renewal is not stated in the public documents (unclear).
- **insurance_required:** Yes - Rule 1(2)(iii): malpractice insurance under the CDA master policy administered by CDSPI, minimum $3 million; required as a condition of licensure (an actively licensed dentist is expected to keep it; whether CDSPI will insure a licensee practising/living outside Canada is unverified).
- **inactive_status:** The Bylaws s.2.1 list categories of membership including '(c) The non-practising members, consisting of: (i) non-practising life members; and ...' and s.(2) refers to a person 'applying for registration [who] is a non-practising member or otherwise has not been fully licensed'. Eligibility, fee, CE and time limits for non-practising membership were NOT verified (bylaw text not read in full) - treat as unclear; ask licensing@nbdent.ca.
- **reactivation:** Rule 1(7): (iii) licence lapsed less than three years: demonstrate good standing + 'complete thirty (30) hours of continuing education per lapsed year' relevant to all facets of general dentistry (Rule 2 'CE requirement initial licensure or reinstatement' repeats the 30 h/yr pro-rated rule for anyone not currently licensed with another Canadian regulator); (ii) lapsed more than three years: 'must be approved by the Registrar's office upon satisfying the conditions set by the registrar' (conditions unspecified - could include assessment/refresher). (iv) For initial licensure, an NDEB pass older than 3 years requires AFK + ACJ + NDECC (hands-on). Failure to meet the 2-year CE cycle leads to non-renewal until requirements are met.
- **remote_maintainability:** moderate
- **details:** If a licence could be obtained, keeping it active from abroad would require: ~$3,665 + HST per year, 60 online-obtainable CE credits per 2 years reported online, annual BLS-HCP/oxygen certification (hands-on), and maintaining $3M CDSPI malpractice coverage. No practice-hour, residency or in-person renewal steps were found. The real obstacle is initial eligibility (work authorisation) and the unverified question of whether a work permit must remain valid at renewal; the non-practising membership category exists in the Bylaws but its terms were not verified.
