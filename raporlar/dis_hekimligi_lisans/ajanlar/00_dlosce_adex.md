# Kesişen konu: US dental licensure without a hands-on (manikin/patient) clinical exam and without residency, as of 2 Nov 2026: status of the DLOSCE after the ADA/ADEX integration, the actual rule text of the DLOSCE-accepting states (AZ, CO, IN, KY, OR, WA), any INBDE-only or OSCE-only pathways, and the 2026-27 ADEX exam structure.

Güven: medium

## Bulgular

BOTTOM LINE: As of 2 November 2026 there is no officially documented way for a NEW graduate to satisfy any US state's clinical-examination requirement with a computer-based exam alone. The standalone DLOSCE (the only computer-only clinical exam ever accepted "in full" by any state) is no longer available to new candidates; it now exists only as one component of the ADEX Dental Examination, which also requires five manikin hand-skill exams. Every other state pathway found requires a hands-on exam (ADEX/CRDTS/SRTA/state exam), a PGY-1 residency, or (historically) a patient-based portfolio. Three "grey-zone" possibilities remain untested and unconfirmed by any official source (see Implications/Open questions): (1) Colorado's board policy that accepts an "OSCE requirement in lieu of a clinical examination"; (2) Washington's rule accepting "the complete NDEB of Canada clinical examination" for graduates of approved schools; (3) asking KY/WA/OR/AZ/CO whether an ADEX-reported DLOSCE pass (obtained inside the ADEX series, without the manikin parts) would be accepted on its own.

(a) ADA/ADEX AGREEMENT AND WHAT HAPPENED TO THE DLOSCE
- ADA press release, 14 Apr 2026 (https://www.ada.org/about/press-releases/ada-and-adex-advance-dental-licensure-examinations-through-new-agreement): ADA licensed the DLOSCE to ADEX "for incorporation into the ADEX Dental Examination". Verbatim: "ADEX will sunset its DSE OSCE no later than Aug. 1, 2026. Upon sunset of the DSE OSCE, all ADEX Dental Examination administrations will include the DLOSCE. The DLOSCE will no longer be offered or administered as a standalone examination to new DLOSCE candidates, except in conjunction with the ADEX Dental Examination, after August 1, 2026. All standalone administrations of the DLOSCE will cease after October 9, 2026." Also: "The ADEX Dental Examination will continue to assess candidates' clinical hand skills alongside their treatment-planning and decision-making abilities." Identical text on ADEX's site (https://adextesting.org/?p=17141).
- JCNDE DLOSCE page (https://jcnde.ada.org/dlosce), read 2 Nov 2026: "All administrations are conducted through ADEX." The 2026 DLOSCE Candidate Guide "is for historical reference purposes only". Results/audits via DENTPIN only for candidates who "took the DLOSCE through the ADA on or before September 25, 2026".
- JCNDE "Recent and Forthcoming Changes" PDF (updated 10/22/2026; DLOSCE section last updated 10/21/2026) (https://jcnde.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/jcnde/files/updates_licensureexaminationsandadmissiontests.pdf): "All future administrations will be conducted through ADEX."
- 2026 DLOSCE Candidate Guide (updated 4/31/2026) (https://jcnde.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/jcnde/files/dlosce_candidate_guide.pdf): the only 2026 administration window listed is "2 (retake only*) Apr 21, 2026 – Sept 26, 2026", with "*Retake only windows are available only to candidates who have attempted the DLOSCE previously and failed." State list (p.2): "Alaska*, Arizona, Colorado, Indiana, Iowa*, Kentucky, Oregon, Washington" with "*Partial fulfillment of state's clinical licensure examination requirement, in conjunction with a hands-on assessment." Eligibility for "Dentist – Graduate of CODA Accredited Program": ADA member, or "submit a copy of their diploma or provide other appropriate verification of their degree." Results: "DTS will provide a history of results to dental and dental hygiene boards and any additional recipients indicated" (via DENTPIN request). The guide says "Acceptance of the NBEs is at the discretion of individual licensing jurisdictions."
- JCNDE 2026 Annual Report (https://www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/files/about/annual-reports/ar_jcnde.pdf): DLOSCE incorporated into ADEX "through an exclusive licensing agreement"; "The ADEX Dental Examination will continue to assess candidates' clinical hand skills"; roadmap includes "incorporating the DLOSCE into a new item bank, test administration platform, and results reporting platform."
- Can a candidate register for ADEX and take ONLY the DLOSCE? ADEX FAQ (https://adextesting.org/faqs/), verbatim: "Can I register for the DLOSCE separately from the full Dental Examination series? The computer-based DLOSCE exam is an integral component of the ADEX Dental Examination series and registration package. Separate registration of the DLOSCE component is NOT an option." Also: "Will State Boards accept OSCE results? State Boards will accept the DSE OSCE and DLOSCE as valid components of the ADEX Dental Examination series." Registration for the full series ($2,995 plus facility fees) includes the DLOSCE; "Once any component of the examination series has been attempted (clinical or computer-based), a refund will not be issued for any remaining untaken examination components" (https://adextesting.org/adex-dental/).
- Is the DLOSCE result reported separately? Yes, as a pass/fail per component. ADEX Candidate Guide v20260727 (https://adextesting.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Candidate-Guide_20260727.pdf), "Examination Results & Reporting": "Examination results are reported as pass/fail only, not numeric values"; "An official Score Results Report is a cumulative record of all historical results for any American Board of Dental Examiners administered computer-based and/or clinical exams attempted"; "Although most state dental boards have direct access to examination results through our secure portal, additional reporting by the Candidate may be required." ADEX FAQ: "we have a responsibility to report all historical Pass/Fail results for any attempted computer-based or clinical exam type to the state dental boards." So a DLOSCE pass obtained inside the series would appear on the ADEX record even if the manikin parts were never attempted; whether a board would license on that alone is NOT addressed by any official source found. Note the ADEX 18-month rule and 3-fail rule apply to the series as a whole (ADEX DLOSCE Candidate Manual v20260727, https://adextesting.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-DEN-CAN-Manual-Computer-DLOSCE_20260727.pdf).
- Earlier standalone DLOSCE scores: no official statement was found invalidating them. State rules continue to list the DLOSCE (KY, WA) with their usual 5-year validity windows (AZ, KY, OR, WA all 5 years; see (b)). JCNDE still issues result reports via DENTPIN for exams taken through the ADA on or before 25 Sep 2026. This is moot for this candidate, who cannot obtain a new standalone score.
- Eligibility/identity details relevant to this candidate: the ADEX Candidate Guide explicitly lists "Yeditepe University, Turkey, eligibility limited to graduation after February 1, 2024 [date of CODA accreditation]" under "Also, CODA-accredited" (documentation: diploma or official transcript). The candidate-profile section lists "Social Security Number (SSN)**" and "DENTPIN**" with the footnote "** Required information for results reporting and licensure application in some states." The DLOSCE manual says the DENTPIN "helps protect your identity by replacing other unique identifiers such as Social Security Numbers (US) and Social Insurance Numbers (Canada)." ID: the DLOSCE manual accepts "driver's license, passport, or passport card with signature" as primary ID and says "All IDs, with the exception of passports, must be in English"; the Candidate Guide's heading says primary ID "Must be a valid form of U.S. government-issued photo ID" (inconsistent wording; confirm with ADEX). DLOSCE is administered at Prometric centres; FAQ: "Once authorized, you may attempt the computer-based exam wherever available, regardless of the state from which licensure is sought... Prometric has test center locations all across the world. However, not all computer-based exams are offered at all locations." Clinical (manikin) exams are offered "throughout the United States, and limited locations in Jamaica, Mexico, Nova Scotia, Puerto Rico and Saudi Arabia."

(b) STATE RULE TEXT ON DLOSCE ACCEPTANCE (ADA Licensure-by-State pages updated 7-9 Oct 2026, plus primary sources)
- KENTUCKY (clearest text): 201 KAR 8:533 §2(1)(b) (quoted on https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Kentucky_Licensure, updated 8 Oct 2026): "Successfully complete all components of one (1) of the following clinical examinations within the five (5) years preceding the filing of the application: 1. CITA; 2. CRDTS; 3. CDCA; 4. SRTA; 5. WREB; or 6. The Dental Licensure Objective Structured Clinical Examination (DLOSCE) of the JCNDE." No hands-on add-on is required when the DLOSCE route is used. Other §1 conditions: CODA DDS/DMD, INBDE, fingerprint background check, NPDB; §2(2): an applicant applying more than 2 years after graduation must hold a licence in another US jurisdiction or complete a board-approved CE plan. The ADA's own summary header for Kentucky nevertheless lists only ADEX and CRDTS+SRTA.
- WASHINGTON: WAC 246-817-120 (quoted on the ADA Washington page https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Washington_Licensure and on WA DOH https://doh.wa.gov/licenses-permits-and-certificates/professions-new-renew-or-update/dentist/license-requirements): (1)(b) a practical exam containing at least Restorative, Endodontic, Periodontal, Prosthodontic and Comprehensive treatment planning/diagnostic skills; (2)(a) accepted practical exams include "(vii) The Joint Commission on National Dental Examinations dental licensure objective structured clinical examination (DLOSCE)"; (2)(b) "The commission will accept the complete National Dental Examining Board (NDEB) of Canada clinical examination as meeting its standards if the applicant is a graduate of an approved dental school defined in WAC 246-817-110(2)(a)"; (3) all sections with the same agency or max two agencies in coordination; (4) results valid 5 years. DOH page lists the DLOSCE among "complete practical-clinical examination[s]" and offers a PGY-1 alternative (must serve low-income patients in Washington). CAVEAT: WAC 246-817-110(2)(a) (https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=246-817-110) defines approved schools as "dental schools in the United States or Canada, approved... by the Commission on Dental Accreditation"; (2)(b) lets other schools apply for DQAC approval. WAC 246-817-160 (https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=246-817-160) imposes two extra years of CODA education on graduates of "a dental school or college not accredited by [CODA]" — Yeditepe is CODA-accredited but not "in the United States or Canada", so DQAC's treatment is uncertain.
- OREGON: OAR 818-021-0010(3) (https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_818-021-0010; same text on https://www.oregon.gov/dentistry/pages/apply-license.aspx): "An applicant must pass a Board examination consisting of a clinical portion administered by the Board, or any clinical Board examination administered by any state, or regional testing agency, national testing agency or other Board-recognized testing agency and a jurisprudence portion administered by the Board. Clinical examination results will be recognized by the Board for five years." The Board's application info defines "Clinical Examination" as "any regional testing agency, state dental licensing authority, a national testing agency or other Board-recognized testing agency" (https://www.oregon.gov/dentistry/Documents/Form_Dental_General_Information.pdf). The DLOSCE is not named explicitly; the JCNDE (a national testing agency) fits the wording and the JCNDE guide lists Oregon as full acceptance.
- ARIZONA: ARS 32-1233 (https://www.azleg.gov/ars/32/01233.htm): "2. A clinical examination administered by a state or regional testing agency in the United States within five years preceding filing the application." AAC R4-11-201/R4-11-301 (ADA Arizona page https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Arizona_Licensure, updated 7 Oct 2026) require a certificate/scorecard "sent to the Board directly from a clinical examination administered by a state or testing agency that meets the requirements of A.R.S. § 32-1233(2)". The ADA page lists only ADEX and CRDTS+SRTA; no DLOSCE mention. A 2022 bill (SB1370, https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/55leg/2r/bills/sb1370p.htm) would have inserted "psychomotor clinical examination"; the current statute does not contain that word. The Arizona board's website could not be accessed (HTTP 403 / blocked). Arizona's acceptance therefore rests on the JCNDE list and board practice, not on explicit rule text.
- COLORADO: CRS 12-220-403 and 3 CCR 709-1 Rule 1.6(B)(1)(c): JCNDE exam plus "an examination or other methodology, as determined by the Board, designed to test the applicant's clinical skills and knowledge, which may include residency and/or portfolio models." Board policy as quoted on the ADA Colorado page (https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Colorado_Licensure, "Amended 10/9/2026"): accepts "all U.S. dental clinical examinations, performed on mannikins or patients... which currently includes the exams developed by ADEX and administered by CDCA and CITA; and the exams developed and administered by Delaware, CRDTS, SRTA, WREB and all OSCE's. All parts of a clinical examination are required to be successfully completed and no part of an examination is considered optional... Periodontal testing for dentists is required... The Colorado Dental Board accepts successful completion of a PGY-1, portfolio, or OSCE requirement in lieu of a clinical examination for dentists. Dentists submitting successful completion of a PGY-1 or portfolio model for consideration are required to be first fully licensed... in the state/jurisdiction where the PGY-1 or portfolio was completed" (the prior-licensure condition is stated for PGY-1/portfolio, not for OSCE). The board's own policy document could not be retrieved (DPO site and SOS rule PDF not accessible); the text is internally ambiguous ("all parts... required", "periodontal testing required" vs "OSCE in lieu of a clinical examination").
- INDIANA: contrary to the JCNDE list, Indiana's own instructions do NOT list the DLOSCE. 828 IAC 1-1-7 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/indiana/828-IAC-1-1-7, readopted 2019): "passed all parts of one of... CRDTS, NERB, SRTA, WREB" or a board exam. Indiana PLA "Dentist by Examination" instructions (https://www.in.gov/pla/professions/dentistry-home/dentistry-licensing-information/): accepted clinical exams are CDCA/NERB/WREB/CITA, CRDTS, SRTA only; "Mandatory Disclosure of U.S. Social Security Number... Failure to disclose your U.S. social security number will result in the denial of your application." The ADA Indiana page (updated 8 Oct 2026) lists only ADEX and CRDTS+SRTA.
- ALASKA (partial): 12 AAC 28.940 requires an OSCE plus endodontics and prosthetics "on a mannequin or live patient" and an operative exam (ADA Alaska page https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Alaska_Licensure). IOWA (partial): 650 IAC 11.2(2)(d) approves only patient-based/manikin regional exams and the University-of-Iowa-only portfolio (https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Iowa_Licensure). Both require hands-on components.

(c) ANY STATE LICENSING ON INBDE ALONE, OSCE/WRITTEN ALONE, OR VIA 2025-26 "NO CLINICAL EXAM" LEGISLATION?
None found. Every state checked requires a hands-on exam or a residency: Minnesota – all components of a regional exam or a PGY-1 clinical waiver under Minn. Stat. 150A.06 subd. 3b (https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Minnesota_Licensure, 9 Oct 2026); Vermont – ADEX/CRDTS/SRTA or PGY-1 (https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Vermont_Licensure, 20 Oct 2026); Maine – all ADEX sections incl. manikin restorative/perio (https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Maine_Licensure); Hawaii – all ADEX sections (https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Hawaii_Licensure); Michigan – DSE OSCE plus endo, fixed prostho, perio and restorative portions, and even Canadian-licensed endorsement applicants must have passed the ADEX clinical (https://www.ada.org/~/media/Project/ADA%20Organization/ADA/ADA-org/Files/Education/Licensure%20by%20State/Michigan_Licensure); California – proof of passing the ADEX sections "Diagnostic Skills Examination / Dental Licensure Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) (computer based), Prosthodontic (manikin based), Endodontic (manikin based), Restorative..., Periodontal..." with the note "The DLOSCE... has replaced the DSE OSCE as of August 1, 2026. Candidates registered for the ADEX AFTER June 1, 2026, will now take the DLOSCE instead" (https://www.dbc.ca.gov/applicants/licensure_by_adex.shtml). Search summaries (not read directly) indicated New Mexico 16.5.6 NMAC requires a clinical exam "which includes hands-on periodontal and restorative procedures", and ASDA's status page reports 49 states + DC accepting non-patient (manikin) alternatives, i.e., the reform trend is manikin-based, not exam-free. No 2025-2026 statute or rule eliminating the clinical exam for dentists was found before the search budget was exhausted.

(d) ADEX 2026-2027 EXAM STRUCTURE (exam season 1 Aug 2026 – 31 Jul 2027)
ADEX Dental page (https://adextesting.org/adex-dental/): "The ADEX Dental Examination series consists of both computer simulations and clinical examinations performed on simulated patient manikins. All candidates will challenge five hand-skill components and the ADA's Dental Licensure OSCE (DLOSCE)." Components (Candidate Guide v20260727 and DLOSCE manual v20260727): computer-based DLOSCE (required component effective 1 Jun 2026) plus clinical Periodontal (required effective 1 Aug 2026), Endodontic, Fixed Prosthodontic, Anterior Restorative, Posterior Restorative – all manikin/typodont based (typodont fee $150 per procedure). Fee $2,995 + facility fee; DLOSCE retake $695; all components must be passed within 18 months of the first attempt of any component; three successive failures of any component fail the whole series. There is no "DLOSCE-only" registration option (FAQ quoted above). DSE OSCE registration discontinued from June 2026; DSE initial attempts had to be completed by 31 Dec 2026; retakes now DLOSCE only.

## Aday için sonuçlar

1. The "theory-only US licence" plan is, on the official record, closed for a new graduate as of November 2026. The standalone DLOSCE ended (new candidates 1 Aug 2026; all administrations 9 Oct 2026), and ADEX will not let anyone register for the DLOSCE alone. Every state found requires either a manikin/patient exam, a residency (PGY-1) or a patient-based portfolio.
2. The least-bad US option that avoids live patients is a manikin exam: the ADEX series (5 typodont procedures + DLOSCE) is accepted in 48 states; it still involves hand-skills testing, which the candidate wants to avoid, but it needs no patient and no residency. Clinical sittings exist outside the mainland US only in Jamaica, Mexico, Nova Scotia, Puerto Rico and Saudi Arabia.
3. Three untested grey-zone routes exist, none supported by an official statement; each requires a written answer from the board before relying on it: (a) Colorado – the board policy text says it "accepts successful completion of a PGY-1, portfolio, or OSCE requirement in lieu of a clinical examination for dentists" and lists "all OSCE's"; ask whether an ADEX-reported DLOSCE pass alone, or the NDEB Virtual OSCE, satisfies this; (b) Washington – WAC 246-817-120(2)(b) accepts "the complete NDEB of Canada clinical examination", but only for graduates of schools "in the United States or Canada" approved by CODA (WAC 246-817-110(2)(a)); ask DQAC whether Yeditepe (CODA-accredited, located in Turkey) counts, and whether the current NDEB Written + Virtual OSCE is the "complete NDEB clinical examination"; (c) Kentucky/Washington/Oregon/Arizona – their rules still name the DLOSCE (KY, WA) or "national testing agency" (OR); ask whether a DLOSCE pass reported by ADEX, without the manikin components, satisfies the rule. ADEX's own FAQ frames the DLOSCE only as "a valid component of the ADEX Dental Examination series", so expect boards to say no.
4. If the grey-zone routes fail, the candidate's realistic choices are: (i) take the ADEX manikin series and license in a state whose residency/SSN rules fit (separately verified); (ii) pursue Canada (NDEB Written + Virtual OSCE), which is genuinely computer-only, and then a province that does not demand PR/work authorization; or (iii) wait for further US reform (JCNDE is re-analysing DLOSCE/INBDE content in 2026-28, but nothing suggests a return to a standalone DLOSCE).
5. Paperwork flags for whichever route is used: the ADEX candidate profile asks for an SSN and DENTPIN "required for results reporting and licensure application in some states"; Indiana makes SSN disclosure mandatory; the ADEX Candidate Guide's ID wording ("U.S. government-issued" photo ID) conflicts with the DLOSCE manual's acceptance of a passport, so confirm that a Turkish passport is accepted before registering. Kentucky requires application within 2 years of graduation (otherwise another US licence or a board-approved CE plan).

## Açık sorular

1. Will any of KY, WA, OR, AZ or CO accept an ADEX-reported DLOSCE pass on its own (series registered, manikin components never attempted)? No official source addresses this; ADEX's FAQ treats the DLOSCE only as a component of the series. Needs a written answer from each board.
2. Colorado: the Colorado Dental Board's actual policy document ("Policy 1A"/clinical-exam policy, amended 10/9/2026) could not be retrieved (DPO pages and the SOS 3 CCR 709-1 PDF were not accessible); the ADA-quoted text is ambiguous about whether "OSCE in lieu of a clinical examination" means a standalone OSCE (e.g., DLOSCE or NDEB Virtual OSCE) or only the OSCE component of a regional exam, and whether "Periodontal testing for dentists is required" overrides it.
3. Washington: does DQAC treat Yeditepe (CODA-accredited but located in Turkey) as an "approved dental school" under WAC 246-817-110(2)(a) ("in the United States or Canada")? If yes, does the current NDEB Written Examination + Virtual OSCE count as "the complete NDEB of Canada clinical examination" under WAC 246-817-120(2)(b)? Not verified.
4. Arizona: the state board website was inaccessible (HTTP 403/blocked), so the board's current published list of accepted clinical exams and its reading of "state or regional testing agency" (whether JCNDE/ADEX-administered DLOSCE qualifies without manikin parts) is unverified.
5. Indiana: the official IAC site could not be reached; the LII text (readopted 2019) and the PLA instructions (no DLOSCE) were relied on. A search summary indicated the Oct 2026 board agenda readopted 828 IAC 1-1 "without changes", consistent with no DLOSCE, but this was not read directly.
6. Validity of pre-9 Oct 2026 standalone DLOSCE scores: no official sunset statement found; presumed usable within each state's 5-year window, but not confirmed by any board.
7. Whether the DLOSCE (as an ADEX component) can be sat at Prometric centres outside the US (ADEX says exams can be attempted "wherever available" but "not all computer-based exams are offered at all locations") – unconfirmed for the DLOSCE specifically.
8. ADEX identity/SSN practicalities for a non-US candidate: the Candidate Guide says primary ID must be "U.S. government-issued" while the DLOSCE manual accepts a passport; the SSN field is marked required "in some states". Needs confirmation with ADEX.
9. The web-search budget was exhausted before every state's 2025-26 legislation could be checked individually (e.g., New Mexico's 16.5.6 NMAC text was seen only in a search summary). A follow-up could sweep the remaining ADA state pages for any "no clinical exam" pathway, though none is known to exist.
