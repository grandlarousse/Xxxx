## Sınav yolları

- **Full Dentist Licence via NDEB certificate (standard route for graduates of a recognised/accredited programme)** (el becerisi: hayır): Bylaw 6.05: eligibility standards for a full dentist licence are (a) a degree or equivalent qualification from a 'recognized general dentistry education program' and (b) 'an NDEB certificate issued no more than 3 years before the date of the applicant's application for a licence'. Schedule D (II.1) recognises programmes accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation of Canada and, for 'Jurisdictions outside Canada', 'CODA Accredited Dental Programs (US and international)', plus Australian/NZ/Irish programmes. NDEB certification for graduates of accredited programmes currently consists of the computer-based Virtual OSCE only (200 questions: 50 single-answer MCQ + 150 case-based; sat at test centres in Canada, some US, Ireland and Australia locations; fee CAD 1,750 + CAD 450 one-time application fee; max 3 attempts). NDEB's 'Getting Your Certificate' page: 'An NDEB certificate is issued upon successful completion of the Virtual OSCE, and after proof of graduation is provided'. No hands-on/manikin/patient component, no residency, no BC jurisprudence exam, no language test in the bylaws or application instructions. Plus: criminal record check (name-based, submitted by BCCOHP), notarised statutory declaration + notarised photo ID, proof of CAD 3M liability insurance, fees.
- **Full Dentist Licence by Canadian labour mobility (already licensed in another province)** (el becerisi: hayır): Bylaw 6.03(2)(a)(i): an applicant holding an equivalent, unrestricted licence/registration in another Canadian jurisdiction is eligible without re-proving the Division 4 credentials; must still submit identity, insurance, declaration, certificate of professional conduct (dated within 60 days) and fees. Not available to a new graduate with no Canadian licence.
- **Full Dentist Licence based on licensure outside Canada (equivalency determination)** (el becerisi: hayır): Bylaw 6.03(2)(a)(ii): a licence in a jurisdiction outside Canada granted on eligibility standards the licence committee considers 'substantially equivalent' and not subject to restrictions. Triggers an equivalency determination (6.12) and the committee MAY require (6.14(1)(c)) 'an interview', 'an examination or assessment of the applicant's knowledge and clinical skills' or 'a trial practice period under supervised observation'. Discretionary; a clinical-skills assessment could be imposed. Not relevant to a new graduate holding an NDEB certificate.
- **Provisional licence (while completing equivalency / exams)** (el becerisi: hayır): Bylaw 6.15: issued only under direction of the Licence Committee for applicants who meet 6.03(1)(a)(ii)-(vi) but not (i), for up to 1 year, renewable once; expires when a practising licence is issued. Not a standalone route to full licensure.
- **Limited (Academic) Dentist Licence** (el becerisi: hayır): Bylaw 6.07: dentistry degree + appointment as full/associate/assistant professor at UBC Faculty of Dentistry or another approved institution. No NDEB certificate required but practice restricted to the academic setting and ceases with the appointment. Requires a Canadian academic job (and therefore work authorisation in practice), so not useful here.
- **Temporary licence (max 30 days)** (el becerisi: hayır): Bylaw 6.16: requires an existing equivalent licence in another jurisdiction; only for education/training or delivering an examination; not fee-for-service practice.
- **PGY-1 residency in lieu of NDEB certificate** (el becerisi: evet): NOT a pathway in BC. The bylaws contain no residency substitute; an NDEB certificate (or equivalency determination) is mandatory for the Full Dentist Licence.
- **ADEX / CRDTS / DLOSCE / US regional exams** (el becerisi: evet): NOT accepted in BC. 'NDEB certificate' is defined (6.01) as a certificate issued by the National Dental Examining Board 'or another examining body approved by the board'; no other examining body is listed anywhere in the bylaws or on the BCCOHP site.

## Teori-only açıklaması
BCCOHP Bylaws (effective 1 April 2026), s. 6.05: "For the purposes of section 6.03(1)(a)(i), the eligibility standards for a full dentist licence are (a) a degree or equivalent qualification from a recognized general dentistry education program, and (b) an NDEB certificate issued no more than 3 years before the date of the applicant's application for a licence under section 6.03." Schedule D, II.1: "For the purpose of licensing full dentist licensees under section 6.05, the board recognizes general dentistry education programs accredited by the following organizations: Canada - Commission on Dental Accreditation of Canada; Jurisdictions outside Canada - CODA Accredited Dental Programs (US and international) ..." so a CODA-accredited programme located in Turkey is a recognised programme on the face of the bylaw (consistent with NDEB's own eligibility page listing "CODA Accredited Dental Programs (US and international)"). The NDEB certificate is obtained through the Certification Process, which for accredited graduates currently consists of the Virtual OSCE only - "The Virtual OSCE consists of 200 questions. There are 50 standard single answer multiple choice questions and 150 case based questions" - a computer-delivered exam at test centres ("administered at locations across Canada and at some locations in the USA, Ireland and Australia"). NOTE: NDEB's current fee page and exam calendar list no separate "Written Examination" for the certification process, and the Virtual OSCE FAQ treats the Written Examination/OSCE as legacy exams, so the brief's "Written Examination + Virtual OSCE" description appears outdated: it is now Virtual OSCE alone. Bylaw 6.03(1) lists everything else required (identity, contact info, good character, liability insurance, vaccination attestation, certificates of conduct if previously licensed elsewhere, foreign criminal-record equivalents if previously practised abroad, Schedule C declaration, fees, CRC authorisation). There is no jurisprudence examination, no language test, no CPR requirement and no residency anywhere in Part 6 of the bylaws or in the BCCOHP application instructions. No hands-on clinical examination is required at any stage.

## DLOSCE notları
Not applicable/not accepted. BC licensure is based solely on the NDEB certificate (bylaw 6.01/6.05); the DLOSCE, ADEX, CRDTS etc. play no role. BC's NDEB-based route is already fully computer-based, so the DLOSCE/ADEX integration issue does not arise. (The NDEB itself notes that "NDEB certification or components of the certification process are also being accepted for licensure in some US states", which is the reverse direction.)

## Yüz yüze adımlar
(1) NDEB Virtual OSCE: computer-based but sat in person at a test centre; NDEB states it 'is administered at locations across Canada and at some locations in the USA, Ireland and Australia' - the candidate must travel (e.g. visitor visa to Canada/US) for one exam day; registration windows and 3-4 sittings per year are listed on https://ndeb-bned.ca/examination-dates-and-locations/. NDEB application documents (notarised passport copy, Certification Process form) are mailed to Ottawa; no visit required. (2) BCCOHP application: entirely online portal plus mailed originals. Items needing a notary/commissioner for oaths: the Statutory Declaration (form says 'DECLARED before me at the city of ___, in (country) ___', so a foreign notary is contemplated), the Photo Identification Notarization Form with notarised photocopies of two photo IDs, and notarised copies of the degree and NDEB certificate (notarisation of these two is waived if the university/NDEB sends confirmation directly). Originals must be mailed to BCCOHP in Vancouver. No interview, no in-person jurisprudence exam (none exists), no fingerprinting by default. (3) Criminal record check: BCCOHP submits a name-based check to the BC Ministry of Public Safety (fee CAD 28, ~4 weeks); fingerprints are required only if the Vulnerable Sector check flags a date-of-birth/gender match with a pardoned sex offender; in that case 'If you reside outside of Canada, the Ministry ... will ask you to fill out a form indicating the date that you will return to Canada and attend for fingerprinting' (fingerprints must be taken at a police/RCMP detachment in Canada; 90-day window). (4) Applicants who have practised dentistry outside Canada must also supply, from that jurisdiction, records 'comparable to a criminal record check' dated within 60 days (bylaw 6.03(1)(a)(iv)) - obtainable remotely (e.g. Turkish adli sicil kaydi via e-Devlet). Overall: the whole licence can be obtained from abroad with one trip to sit the Virtual OSCE; a second trip is only needed if fingerprints are demanded.

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
No jurisprudence/ethics exam (none in bylaws or application instructions). No English-language test (none in bylaws; application lists none). No CPR/BLS requirement in the bylaws or the Standards for the Oral Health Team (standards only require a maintained medical emergency kit in the facility). Required: (a) NDEB certificate issued no more than 3 years before the BCCOHP application; (b) degree from recognised programme; (c) criminal record check authorisation under HPOA s. 41(2)(b)/Criminal Records Review Act (name-based; CAD 28; ~4 weeks); (d) notarised Statutory Declaration (Schedule C wording) and notarised photo-ID form; (e) 'information ... confirming ... the applicant's compliance with the applicable requirements for liability insurance under section 8.14' - bylaw 8.14(2): full dentist licensees 'must be insured against liability arising from an error, omission, or negligent act in the provision of health services included in the practice of dentistry in an amount of at least $3,000,000 per occurrence' (proof of insurance must be uploaded for a Full Dentist licence); (f) attestation that the applicant 'has received all mandatory vaccinations against transmissible illness required by or under an enactment' (no specific vaccine enactment identified for dentists at present); (g) good-character confirmation; (h) certificates of professional conduct within 60 days from any other regulator where previously licensed; (i) fees. Timeline: BCCOHP states 'The general application processing time is eight weeks'; CRC ~4 weeks; NDEB says certification takes 6-18 months from eligibility; Virtual OSCE registration deadlines are ~5 weeks before each sitting and results precede certificate issuance after proof of graduation (official transcript sent directly from the university).

## İlk ücretler
BCCOHP (Schedule A, 2026/27): Full dentist application fee CAD 3,322 (non-refundable) + licence fee CAD 1,749 for a full year (CAD 875 half-year if the application is submitted after 1 October) + criminal record check CAD 28 = approx. CAD 5,099 (or CAD 4,225 half-year). NDEB: one-time application fee CAD 450 + Virtual OSCE CAD 1,750 (effective 1 July 2025; max 3 attempts). Plus notary fees, courier, and the first year's CAD 3M professional liability insurance premium. A proposed fee increase for 2027/28 is under consultation on the BCCOHP site.

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Annual. Bylaw 6.20: 'A practising licence expires on the March 31 immediately following the date it was issued or last renewed.' Renewal opens in February; must be completed and paid by 31 March (licensing year 1 April - 31 March).
- **renewal_fee:** Full Dentist: CAD 1,749 renewal + CAD 5.60 (one-fifth of the 5-yearly CRC fee) = CAD 1,754.60 per year (2026/27). Late reinstatement within 60 days (by 30 May): + CAD 332 late fee. After 60 days: reinstatement fee CAD 2,581 (includes renewal) + CAD 28 new CRC = CAD 2,609. A 5% increase applied for 2026/27 and a further increase for 2027/28 is under consultation.
- **ce_hours:** No credit-hour requirement. The modernised Quality Assurance Program (QAP, first cycle 13 April 2026 - 31 March 2027, then annually 1 April - 31 March) replaced the legacy CE-credit programmes: 'oral health professionals are not required to report a specific number of credits or hours'. Components: A (online guided self-inventory, standards self-reflection, minimum 2 professional goals, CPD planning; ~4 h) and B (2-3 online standards modules with knowledge checks; ~4-6 h); CPD activities chosen by the licensee to support their goals must be kept on record for 3 years but no quantity is prescribed. Component C (individualised support) only if selected by audit/referral.
- **ce_online_ok:** Yes. Components A and B are stated as 'Format: Online' and are completed through the BCCOHP dashboard; CPD is self-selected and can be online. Only Component C ('Format: Online, telephone, or in-person (as indicated)') could involve an in-person element, and only if the licensee is selected. No live/in-person CE component for ordinary licensees.
- **cpr_required:** No. Neither the April 2026 bylaws (no occurrence of 'CPR', 'resuscitation' or 'life support') nor the Standards for the Oral Health Team (March 2026) impose a CPR/BLS certification requirement for dentists at application or renewal (sedation providers are subject to separate sedation standards). No hands-on skills session is required.
- **practice_hours_required:** None. The QAP background statement expressly says that 'quantified continuing professional development, currency and active practice hours' from the legacy programmes 'are not included in the Program'. Bylaw 6.22 renewal requirements list only the declaration, CRC authorisation every 4+ years, insurance confirmation, vaccination attestation and fees. (A legacy 'continuous practice hours' prompt on the renewal portal applied to the pre-2026 dental hygiene programme.)
- **address_or_residency_required:** No BC residency or BC address requirement. Licensee must keep a home address and contact details on file and update them within 14 days (bylaw 6.33 for applicants; 8.15 for business contact info). The HPOA requires 'business contact information' for the public register, but 'If you do not have a business mailing address, you must provide at least a phone number and an email address' (BCCOHP renewal FAQ).
- **insurance_required:** Yes, mandatory and continuous. Bylaw 8.14(2): a full dentist licensee 'must be insured against liability arising from an error, omission, or negligent act in the provision of health services included in the practice of dentistry in an amount of at least $3,000,000 per occurrence'; the Schedule C renewal declaration includes 'I will ensure that I maintain professional liability insurance as required by the BCCOHP for the entire period of my registration'. At renewal the licensee attests to coverage (proof uploaded only when changing to practising/Full Dentist status or if requested). Availability and cost of a BC-compliant CAD 3M policy for a licensee who lives abroad and does not practise is an open question.
- **inactive_status:** None. The non-practising class was abolished on 1 April 2026: 'under the BCCOHP bylaws being developed to align with the HPOA, the non-practising status will no longer exist as of April 1, 2026' (BCCOHP notice, 13 Feb 2026). Bylaw 6.02 lists only practising classes plus provisional and temporary licences. The only alternatives are to keep renewing the Full Dentist licence (full fee, QAP, insurance) or to resign/let it lapse and become a 'former licensee'.
- **reactivation:** Lapsed less than 60 days (by 30 May): online reinstatement with renewal items + CAD 332 late fee (bylaw 6.25). Lapsed more than 60 days: paper reinstatement application within 3 years of last being a licensee (bylaw 6.26(2): 'within 3 years of the date on which the former licensee was last a licensee or registrant'), with Schedule C declaration, insurance, vaccination attestation, new CRC (CAD 28), certificates of professional conduct and foreign criminal-record equivalents if practised elsewhere meanwhile, fee CAD 2,581; reinstated in the same class with the same conditions (6.27). No clinical exam or refresher is prescribed for reinstatement within 3 years. After more than 3 years: 'you will have to re-apply for a new dentist licence' (BCCOHP licence resources page), i.e. a fresh 6.03 application, and because 6.05(b) requires an NDEB certificate issued within the previous 3 years, an old certificate triggers an equivalency determination (6.12-6.14) in which the licence committee may require an interview, 'an examination or assessment of the applicant's knowledge and clinical skills' or 'a trial practice period under supervised observation' - potentially hands-on. Conclusion: do not let the licence lapse for more than 3 years.
- **remote_maintainability:** moderate
- **details:** The licence can be kept ACTIVE indefinitely from Turkey: annual online renewal (credit card or mailed cheque), QAP Components A/B online (~8-10 h/yr), no practice hours, no CPR, no residency, no address in BC, CRC re-check every 5 years is name-based and arranged by BCCOHP (fingerprints only if the Vulnerable Sector check flags a name/DOB match, which would require attending a Canadian police detachment within 90 days). The awkward elements are: (1) mandatory CAD 3,000,000 professional liability insurance must be maintained every year even without practising, since no non-practising class exists after 1 April 2026; (2) the full-rate renewal fee (CAD 1,754.60/yr, rising); (3) attestation of 'mandatory vaccinations ... required by or under an enactment'; (4) possible Component C follow-up ('online, telephone, or in-person') if selected. Lapse beyond 3 years effectively means starting over with possible equivalency assessment.
