# Ontario (Canada) — ajan bulgusu

Düzenleyici: Royal College of Dental Surgeons of Ontario (RCDSO) — https://www.rcdso.org

**Teori-only rota:** yes · **Göçmenlik engeli:** high · **Uzaktan sürdürme:** moderate · **Güven:** high

## Sınav yolları

- **General certificate of registration via NDEB Certification Process (graduates of CDAC/CODA-accredited programmes)** (el becerisi: hayır): O. Reg. 205/94 s. 16(1) para 2: applicant 'has successfully completed the National Dental Examining Board of Canada examinations leading to a Certificate of the National Dental Examining Board of Canada'. For accredited-programme graduates the NDEB Certification Process currently consists of the Virtual OSCE only (NDEB fees page lists only 'Virtual OSCE $1750' plus a $450 application fee; the Virtual OSCE page: 'The Virtual OSCE consists of 200 questions. There are 50 standard single answer multiple choice questions and 150 case based questions' and 'An NDEB certificate is issued upon successful completion of the Virtual OSCE, and after proof of graduation is provided to the NDEB office'). It is delivered at Prometric test centres in Canada and the US (May/Nov sittings) and some sittings in Australia/Ireland. No manikin or patient component. Candidates may sit up to 4 months before graduation; max 3 attempts. Then RCDSO adds the online Jurisprudence & Ethics course/exam, language proficiency (CODA degree satisfies it), professional liability insurance, conduct/health declarations, and Canadian citizenship/PR/IRPA work authorization (s. 16(1) para 6). NDEB certification is non-exemptible (s. 16(2)).
- **Labour-mobility route (HPPC s. 22.18) for dentists already holding a general certificate in another Canadian province** (el becerisi: hayır): O. Reg. 205/94 s. 17: an applicant to whom s. 22.18 of the Health Professions Procedural Code applies 'is deemed to have met the requirements of paragraphs 1, 2 and 3 of subsection 16(1)'; must provide good-standing evidence from every jurisdiction (non-exemptible). Still requires J&E exam, PLI and the citizenship/PR/IRPA authorization requirement. Not available to a new graduate without a prior Canadian provincial licence.
- **Academic certificate of registration** (el becerisi: hayır): s. 20(1): dental degree, 'a full-time appointment of professorial rank to a faculty or school of dentistry at a university in Ontario', language, J&E exam, and Canadian citizenship/PR/IRPA authorization. No NDEB required, but practice is limited to the faculty/associated hospital, no fees may be charged, and the certificate is automatically revoked when the appointment ends. Not realistic for the candidate (needs a university appointment and a work permit).
- **Education / Graduate Student / Post-Specialty Training certificates (GPR, specialty or other postgraduate programmes)** (el becerisi: hayır): Programme-enrolment-based classes (O. Reg. 205/94 s. 21-23). They require enrolment in an Ontario programme (hence a study permit/visa) and are limited to the programme. The candidate has excluded residency/programme routes. Fee $350 + $215 reduced annual fee.
- **Short Duration, Instructional, Academic Visitor, Emergency certificates** (el becerisi: hayır): Restricted, temporary classes (e.g., Short Duration: courses of 14 days or less under direct supervision of an Ontario dentist; Academic Visitor: visiting academic appointments). None is a route to an unrestricted general licence.
- **US regional/ADEX/CRDTS clinical exams, DLOSCE, or PGY-1 in lieu of exam** (el becerisi: evet): NOT recognised in Ontario. The NDEB Certificate is the only accepted competency credential and is non-exemptible (s. 16(2)). No residency route exists in lieu of the NDEB examinations. (Hands-on flag is moot since these are not accepted.)

## Teori-only açıklaması
Examination-wise Ontario is fully theory/computer-based for a CODA/CDAC-accredited graduate. O. Reg. 205/94 (General, under the Dentistry Act, 1991), consolidated to 1 Jan 2026 (last amendment O. Reg. 270/25), s. 16(1): 'The additional requirements for the issuing of a general certificate of registration are the following: 1. The applicant has a degree in dentistry evidencing successful completion of a course in dental studies of at least four years duration at a university-based dental school. 2. The applicant, i. holds a Certificate of the National Dental Examining Board of Canada issued before January 1, 1994, or ii. has successfully completed the National Dental Examining Board of Canada examinations leading to a Certificate of the National Dental Examining Board of Canada at a time when those examinations were approved by the College ... 3. If the applicant satisfied the requirement of paragraph 2 more than three years before the date of the application, the applicant must have engaged in the practice of dentistry on a continuous and regular basis in Canada, the United States of America or another jurisdiction approved by the Registration Committee for a period of at least three years immediately before the date of the application. 4. The applicant is able to demonstrate the ability to speak and write either English or French with reasonable fluency. 5. The applicant has successfully completed an examination in ethics and jurisprudence set or approved by the College. 6. The applicant is a Canadian citizen or a permanent resident of Canada or has received the appropriate authorization under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (Canada) to permit the applicant to engage in the practice of dentistry in Canada as authorized by the certificate.' s. 16(2): 'the requirement of paragraph 2 of subsection (1) is non-exemptible.' The NDEB examination for accredited graduates is the Virtual OSCE ('200 questions ... 50 standard single answer multiple choice questions and 150 case based questions', computer-delivered at Prometric centres); the RCDSO Jurisprudence & Ethics examination is an online course with an online final exam (pass 60%). No manikin/patient exam and no residency exist anywhere in the Ontario rules. HOWEVER the route is closed to this candidate by para 6 (citizenship/PR/IRPA work authorization), not by any exam: a visitor visa is not 'authorization under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act ... to permit the applicant to engage in the practice of dentistry in Canada'. Note also para 3: the application must be made within 3 years of NDEB certification unless the applicant has 3 years of continuous practice in Canada/US/an approved jurisdiction (RCDSO FRP 2025 lists Ireland, Australia, New Zealand as approved; Turkey is not).

## DLOSCE notları
Not applicable. Ontario does not accept the DLOSCE, ADEX, CRDTS, or any US clinical examination, and no OSCE other than the NDEB's own is recognised. The NDEB Certificate (currently obtained by passing the computer-based NDEB Virtual OSCE, after the NDEB retired its separate Written Examination/OSCE pair; the NDEB FAQ still references earlier attempts at 'the Written Examination and OSCE' counting toward the 3-attempt limit) is the sole accepted competency credential and is non-exemptible under O. Reg. 205/94 s. 16(2). The closure of standalone DLOSCE administrations after Oct 2026 therefore has no effect on Ontario.

PGY-1 kabul: False

RCDSO 'What You Need to Apply': 'If you are not a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, you must submit: a copy of the authorization issued by Immigration Canada which permits you to work in Canada through the practice of dentistry.' RCDSO 'Before You Apply': 'Before you can practice dentistry in Ontario, you must first have appropriate work authorization issued to you by Immigration, Refugees, and Citizenship Canada (IRCC). Examples ... Canadian citizenship ... Permanent Residency ... Work Permit (including open work permit) that permits the individual to engage in the practice of dentistry or health profession in Canada. In some cases a Study Permit may be acceptable ... Applicants may apply prior to having received work authorization if they have already begun the IRCC process and expect to receive appropriate work authorization within 90 days or less.' FAQ: an expired PR card/Canadian passport is accepted; 'An expired Work Permit, however, is not acceptable.' A visitor visa/eTA is not work authorization, so a visitor cannot be issued any practising class of certificate. There is no non-practising/registration-only class in Ontario (classes listed in s. 10.1: General, Specialty, Academic, Education, Post-Specialty Training, Graduate Student, Academic Visitor, Instructional, Short Duration, Emergency) and the immigration requirement applies to general, specialty and academic classes. Once licensed, new s. 15(3) (O. Reg. 270/25, in force 1 Jan 2026) makes it a condition that 'The member shall not engage in the practice of dentistry unless the member is a Canadian citizen or permanent resident of Canada, or has appropriate authorization under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act' and 'The member shall immediately advise the Registrar in writing if the member ceases to be a Canadian citizen or a permanent resident ... or to have appropriate authorization' - i.e. loss of status after licensure bars practice in Ontario but does not itself revoke the certificate. Reinstatement after resignation again requires citizenship/PR/IRPA authorization (RCDSO reinstatement page). Technically s. 16(2) makes only para 2 (NDEB) non-exemptible, so para 6 could in theory be exempted by the Registration Committee under the Health Professions Procedural Code, but RCDSO treats work authorization as mandatory and no exemption practice is documented. Verification: applicant must upload copies of the citizenship/PR/IRCC documents (document-based lawful-status verification; no SAVE-type database check, no affidavit). Address: no Ontario address required; by-law 16.2.6: 'a member's business address shall be the address of each location at which the member practices in Ontario, or, where the member does not practice in Ontario, an address designated by the member'; by-law 16.4.1 requires notifying home address, e-mail and 'whether the member is practicing in Ontario'.

## Yüz yüze adımlar
RCDSO side: none. The application is made entirely in the online RCDSO applicant portal (form, scanned document uploads, online payment of the $350 fee; 'Documentation is no longer required by mail and only scans of documents should be submitted'). The Jurisprudence & Ethics requirement is an online course (13 modules, can be taken in several sittings) with an online final exam, pass mark 60%, results verified by staff. No fingerprinting, police certificate, Live Scan, interview or wall-certificate pickup is required (the application form asks conduct/health/criminal-charge questions by declaration; applications with professional/health/judicial history may be referred to the Registration Committee, which meets every 8-10 weeks and decides on paper). Notarisation is only needed if names differ between documents without a legal name change (sworn name affidavit before a lawyer/notary - can be done abroad). Certificates of Standing from any jurisdiction where the applicant was ever licensed (e.g. Turkey) must be e-mailed directly by that regulator to the RCDSO. Documents not in English/French need a certified translation. NDEB side: application documents are mailed to Ottawa (a notarized photocopy of a passport - a foreign passport is acceptable - and the signed Certification Process form; the university sends the transcript via MyCreds/Parchment etc.), which can be done from abroad. The Virtual OSCE itself must be sat in person at a Prometric test centre: sittings in Canada and the US (March/May/November) and some in Australia/Ireland; none in Turkey, so a short trip on a visitor visa (Canada or US) is required for the exam day. The one thing that cannot be done from abroad or on a visit is obtaining the IRCC work authorization that the licence requires.

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
(1) NDEB Certificate: apply to the NDEB Certification Process (application fee $450; eligibility 'Received a BDS/DDS/DMD from an accredited dental program within the last 5 years' or 'more than 5 years ago' or within 4 months of graduation), pass the Virtual OSCE ($1,750; results within 8 weeks; max 3 attempts), certificate issued after the university's official transcript showing the conferral date is received. (2) RCDSO online Jurisprudence & Ethics course/exam (pass 60%; results valid 3 years). (3) Language: 'reasonable fluency' in English or French; graduates of CODA-accredited programmes meet it by submitting the degree/transcript/dean's letter (RCDSO Language Proficiency Policy lists CODA-accredited programmes as a main pathway) - no IELTS needed. (4) Citizenship/PR/IRCC work authorization documents (see immigration). (5) Professional liability protection is now a requirement for issuance (s. 14(1.1), O. Reg. 270/25) - arranged through the College/Navacord (Jones DesLauriers) for 2026-2028; 2026 cost $1,500. (6) Continuous-practice rule: if NDEB was completed more than 3 years before applying, 3 years of continuous and regular practice in Canada/US/an approved jurisdiction (Ireland, Australia, NZ) is required - so a new graduate must apply within 3 years of NDEB certification. (7) Declarations of good character, conduct, health and criminal charges/convictions; Certificates of Standing from every jurisdiction ever licensed; name-change documents if applicable. (8) No CPR/BLS certificate, no criminal-record check and no background fingerprinting are listed. Timelines: application fee starts a regulated 15-day first-review clock; first review typically 1-2 weeks; whole process typically 2-8 weeks; decision within 30 days once the file is complete; applications expire after 90 days if not completed; licence issued only after final fee payment; public register updated within ~5 business days.

## İlk ücretler
NDEB (CAD): application fee $450 (one-time) + Virtual OSCE $1,750 = $2,200 (plus transcript/notary costs). RCDSO (CAD): non-refundable application fee $350 + prorated first-year annual fee: $2,170 if registered 1 Jan-31 May (total $2,520), $1,085 if 1 Jun-31 Aug (total $1,435), $660 if on/after 1 Sep (total $1,010); plus mandatory professional liability coverage (2026: $1,500 collected with registration; 2027 premium to be set by Navacord). No installment plans; no refunds once licensed. Certified translations and the J&E course are extra (course fee not stated on the pages read).

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Annual. All general/specialty/academic certificates expire 31 December; renewal (questionnaire + Health Professions Database questionnaire + insurance + fee) must be completed online by 15 December. Late renewal possible until 31 January with a $500 penalty; non-renewal = suspension for non-payment effective 1 February, and under s. 28.4 the Registrar 'shall revoke' once the suspension has continued 60 days.
- **renewal_fee:** $2,170 CAD annual (regulatory) fee (frozen for 2026 and proposed frozen for 2027; by-law 18.3.9 otherwise indexes it to CPI annually) + mandatory professional liability insurance paid to Navacord/Jones DesLauriers (2026 total $1,500; 2027 price not yet announced) + $500 late penalty if paid after 15 Dec. Reduced fee $215 only for dentists practising solely on a volunteer basis in Ontario (annual application, Registrar's discretion).
- **ce_hours:** 90 CE points per 3-year cycle (O. Reg. 27/10 s. 3(4)): minimum 15 points from Category 1 'core courses' approved by the QA Committee, minimum 45 points from Category 2 (clinical courses by approved sponsors, e-learning programs with assessment, teaching, publishing), remaining up to 30 points may be Category 3 (non-clinical courses, recorded webinars, reading peer-reviewed journals). First cycle starts on the registration date and ends three years later on 14 December; points cannot be carried forward. Plus the Practice Enhancement Tool (PET): an online multiple-choice self-assessment 5 years after registration and every 5 years thereafter (O. Reg. 27/10 s. 4). Annual QA compliance declaration in the renewal questionnaire. Members may also be selected for practice assessment/peer assessment under the QA regulation.
- **ce_online_ok:** Largely yes. RCDSO CE FAQ: 'Can I claim CE points for self-study or online (e-learning) courses? Yes.' Category 2 points are earned for 'e-Learning Program with assessment on clinical dental topics offered through approved sponsors'; Category 3 includes 'Recorded webinars' and 'Reading peer-reviewed dental journals'. No live or in-person attendance requirement is stated. Open point: the 15 Category 1 core points must come from QA-Committee-approved core courses offered through approved sponsors; many RCDSO core courses are delivered online, but availability of all 15 points online was not verified against the current Category 1 course list.
- **cpr_required:** No. Neither O. Reg. 27/10 nor the renewal pages require a CPR/BLS certificate for renewal. CPR courses are optional and can be claimed for Category 2 CE points ('You may claim CE points in Category 2 every year for completing a CPR course given by a CPR provider following the recommendations of ILCOR').
- **practice_hours_required:** None for renewal. RCDSO renewal FAQ: 'Members may choose to maintain their license even if they are not practicing in Ontario as we do not have a minimum practice hour requirement. If you choose to maintain your license while retired, you will still need to pay the full annual fee and be up to date with any Quality Assurance requirements.' A continuous-practice test (3 years in Canada/US/approved jurisdiction) applies only at initial registration (if NDEB >3 years old) and at reinstatement.
- **address_or_residency_required:** No Ontario address or residency required to hold the licence. By-law 16.2.6: where the member does not practise in Ontario, the register shows 'an address designated by the member'; by-law 16.4.1: member must notify home address, e-mail and whether practising in Ontario. (Residence/practice in Ontario matters only for Council voting/elections.)
- **insurance_required:** Yes, mandatory and cannot be opted out of (renewal page: 'Participation in the insurance program is mandatory as part of the renewal process, and members cannot opt out'). By-law 22.1.2: 'Every member holding a certificate of registration of any class issued by the College shall maintain professional liability coverage ... while providing Professional Dental Services in Ontario'; minimum $2,000,000 per occurrence / $6,000,000 aggregate (22.1.4). 2026-2028 coverage must be through Navacord/Jones DesLauriers (JDIMI); from 2029 any licensed Canadian insurer or employer policy. s. 15(2) O. Reg. 205/94 makes maintaining PLI a condition of every certificate; failure to provide evidence within 14 days of request leads to suspension (s. 28.3). 2026 cost $1,500.
- **inactive_status:** None. RCDSO renewal FAQ: 'We cannot put licenses "on hold" and we do not have a "retired class".' O. Reg. 27/10 'makes no provision for a retired status', so CE applies to every general/specialty/academic certificate holder. The only alternatives are (a) keep the full licence (full fee ~$2,170 + ~$1,500 PLI + CE + PET) or (b) resign in writing via the portal (s. 28; resignations filed 15 Oct-31 Dec take effect 31 Dec; no CE obligations after resignation) or (c) the $215 volunteer-only reduced fee (only for those volunteering in Ontario).
- **reactivation:** Reinstatement on application (O. Reg. 205/94 s. 30, as amended 2026): available to a former member who resigned or was revoked, only if the application is made 'within two years after the date of the revocation or resignation'; must pay the $350 reinstatement fee (by-law 18.7.3) plus the annual fee and any money owed, hold PLI, and 'satisf[y] the Registrar that they engaged in the practice of dentistry ... on a continuous and regular basis in Canada, the United States of America or another jurisdiction approved by the Registration Committee within three years before' meeting the other conditions. RCDSO reinstatement page adds: J&E course must be repeated if reinstating 3+ years after resigning; the applicant must again be 'a Canadian citizen, permanent resident or have authorization under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act'; ineligible if since leaving they were charged with any criminal offence, investigated, refused registration elsewhere, etc. If referred, the Registration Committee may require additional education/training or 'an assessment to ensure you meet the current standards'. After 2 years, a former member must apply as a NEW applicant (s. 31) and then faces s. 16(1) para 3 (3 years continuous practice in Canada/US/approved jurisdiction if NDEB is >3 years old). No clinical re-examination is prescribed, but practice in Turkey would not satisfy the continuous-practice test unless the Registration Committee approves that jurisdiction.
- **remote_maintainability:** moderate
- **details:** If the licence were ever obtained, it can be kept ACTIVE indefinitely from abroad: renewal is 100% online (portal questionnaire, HPDB questionnaire, Navacord insurance payment, credit-card fee), there are no practice-hour, Ontario-address or residency requirements, CE can be done by e-learning/recorded webinars/self-study (subject to the Category 1 core-course caveat), the PET is an online test every 5 years, and no CPR is required. The awkward parts are cost (~CAD 3,700/yr in 2026: $2,170 fee + $1,500 PLI) and the compulsory insurance. There is no cheap inactive/retired class; the only cheap option is resignation, and reinstatement requires recent practice in Canada/US/approved jurisdictions plus Canadian work authorization, so a lapse is hard to recover from after 2 years. Note s. 15(3): a member who loses IRPA status may not practise in Ontario and must notify the Registrar, but the certificate is not automatically revoked.(Annual-fee figures from RCDSO renewal page and by-law 18.3; CE from O. Reg. 27/10 and RCDSO QA pages; reinstatement from O. Reg. 205/94 s. 28-31 current consolidation.)

## Açık sorular
1. Exemption of the immigration requirement: O. Reg. 205/94 s. 16(2) makes only the NDEB requirement non-exemptible, so the Registration Committee could in theory exempt s. 16(1) para 6 under the Health Professions Procedural Code; no RCDSO policy or published decision granting such an exemption was found, and all RCDSO guidance treats IRCC work authorization as mandatory. Worth a direct written query to registration@rcdso.org, but expect 'no'. 2. The RCDSO 'Before You Apply' page says a study permit 'in some cases' may be acceptable and applicants may apply if authorization is expected within 90 days; which permit types (e.g., open work permits) RCDSO actually accepts, and whether any are obtainable by a Turkish citizen without an employer, is an immigration question outside this review. 3. Whether all 15 Category 1 core CE points per cycle can be earned fully online from abroad was not verified against the current approved Category 1 course list (many RCDSO core courses are online, but this should be confirmed with ce@rcdso.org). 4. 2027 and later professional liability premiums (Navacord) are not yet published; 2026 was $1,500. 5. Whether O. Reg. 27/10 practice/peer assessments could be imposed on a member living abroad who does not practise in Ontario (and how they would be conducted) is not addressed in the public pages. 6. NDEB treatment of Yeditepe as a CODA-accredited programme is being verified by another agent; the NDEB pages confirm 'CODA Accredited Dental Programs (US and international)' are accepted. 7. The NDEB now lists only the Virtual OSCE as the certification exam (the earlier separate Written Examination/OSCE appear to have been retired); confirm with the NDEB that no additional written component exists for 2027 sittings. 8. If the candidate is registered with any Turkish dental regulator, a Certificate of Standing must be sent directly by that body - feasibility with Turkish authorities not checked. 9. Fingerprinting/police checks: none appear in RCDSO's public requirements, but the portal document checklist (only visible after starting an application) was not accessible.
