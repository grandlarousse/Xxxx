# Nebraska (USA) — ajan bulgusu

Düzenleyici: Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), Division of Public Health, Licensure Unit, acting with the Nebraska Board of Dentistry — https://dhhs.ne.gov/licensure/pages/Dentist.aspx

**Teori-only rota:** no · **Göçmenlik engeli:** medium · **Uzaktan sürdürme:** moderate · **Güven:** medium

## Sınav yolları

- **Licensure by examination (INBDE + Board-approved manikin/simulation clinical exam + jurisprudence exam)** (el becerisi: evet): Neb. Rev. Stat. 38-1117(1): applicant must (a) graduate from an accredited dental school, (b) pass INBDE (or NBDE I+II), (c) 'demonstrate the applicant's psychomotor skill in clinical dentistry by passing a standardized national examination approved by the board', (d) pass a jurisprudence exam, (e) continuing clinical competency if required by the board. 38-1117(2)(a) requires that exam to be 'a simulation-based or manikin-based clinical competency psychomotor examination' with manikin periodontal, restorative, prosthodontic and endodontic components. 172 NAC 56-003.01(B): practical exam score 75+ on each part within the past 5 years. Board's current acceptable list (applications 1 Nov 2024-31 Oct 2025, still shown on the ADA page updated 10 Oct 2026): CRDTS, CDCA (with patient-based perio), CITA, WREB (+ prostho manikin/patient), SRTA (+ CRDTS perio), and ADEX (1 Jul 2025-30 Jun 2026) 'with a manikin or patient-based periodontal examination (Passing Score is 75)'. Scores valid 5 years. Criminal background check (fingerprints, $55) also required.
- **DLOSCE / OSCE-only route** (el becerisi: evet): NOT available. The DLOSCE is not on Nebraska's acceptable clinical examination list, and the statute (38-1117(2)(a)) expressly requires a 'simulation-based or manikin-based' psychomotor examination with four manikin components, which a computer-based OSCE cannot satisfy. ADEX is accepted only as the full exam with its manikin/patient periodontal component.
- **PGY-1 / residency in lieu of clinical exam** (el becerisi: evet): NOT available. Neither 38-1117 nor 172 NAC 56 provides for residency in lieu of the psychomotor exam. Residency only counts as 'active practice' toward the reciprocity route (38-1120) and for a 1-year temporary license for residents in Nebraska postgraduate programs (not a route to a full license).
- **Licensure by reciprocity / credential (license in another US state)** (el becerisi: hayır): Neb. Rev. Stat. 38-1120: applicant licensed in another US state/territory/DC who has 'been actively engaged in the practice of dentistry for at least one year' under that license (DHHS web page states 3 years active practice or residency, one within the last 3 years). 172 NAC 56-003.02: must meet 003.01 except (B) (i.e. INBDE + jurisprudence, but no new practical exam). 172 NAC 56-003.04 / Neb. Rev. Stat. 38-129.02: reciprocity licensees 'must submit documentation of establishing residency in Nebraska' within 90 days (lease, deed, utility bills etc.). Not usable by a new graduate (requires a prior US license and practice) and requires Nebraska residency.

## Teori-only açıklaması
Neb. Rev. Stat. 38-1117(1)(c) requires every applicant to 'demonstrate the applicant's psychomotor skill in clinical dentistry by passing a standardized national examination approved by the board', and 38-1117(2)(a) states: 'An examination approved by the board under subdivision (1)(c) of this section shall be a simulation-based or manikin-based clinical competency psychomotor examination that demonstrates evidence of psychometric soundness and shall include (i) a simulation-based or manikin-based periodontal component, (ii) a simulation-based or manikin-based restorative component, (iii) a simulation-based or manikin-based prosthodontic component, (iv) a simulation-based or manikin-based endodontic component, and (v) a remediation policy'. 172 NAC 56-003.01(B) requires 'Passage of the practical examination with the score of 75 or above on each part of the examination within the past 5 years'. The Board's acceptable-exam list contains only CRDTS, CDCA, CITA, WREB, SRTA and ADEX (all manikin/patient hands-on exams); the DLOSCE is not listed and no PGY-1/residency substitute exists. A hands-on clinical exam is therefore unavoidable for a new graduate.

## DLOSCE notları
Nebraska does not accept the DLOSCE (standalone or as a substitute) - it does not appear on the Board's 'Dentists - Acceptable Clinical Examinations (current)' list (DentAcceptableClinExams.pdf) and the statute requires a manikin/simulation-based psychomotor exam with four hands-on components. The ADEX Dental Examination (1 Jul 2025-30 Jun 2026 administrations, passing score 75) is accepted only 'with a manikin or patient-based periodontal examination'; because Nebraska needs the manikin ADEX components, the DLOSCE component that is now bundled into ADEX adds nothing by itself, and an ADEX-reported DLOSCE pass alone would not satisfy 38-1117. Earlier standalone DLOSCE scores are irrelevant in Nebraska. Note: the acceptable-exam list is refreshed each 1 November; the version on both DHHS and the ADA page (updated 10 Oct 2026) is still the Nov 2024-Oct 2025 list, so the Nov 2026 list should be checked, but the statute would still bar a DLOSCE-only route.

PGY-1 kabul: False

## Göçmenlik / kimlik

- **citizenship_or_pr_required:** False
- **work_authorization_required:** False
- **lawful_presence_verification:** True
- **ssn_or_sin_required:** False
- **itin_or_no_ssn_alternative:** The DHHS application form (Section A item 2, Dentist Reinstatement Application; initial application uses the same DHHS template) says 'Check the Appropriate Box(s): Social Security Number (SSN); Alien Registration Number (A#); or Form I-94 (Arrival-Departure Record) number ... If you have both a SSN and an A# or I-94 number, you must report both.' So an I-94 number (or A#) is accepted instead of an SSN. ITIN is not mentioned on the form.
- **in_jurisdiction_address_required:** False
- **barrier_level:** medium
- **details:** Neb. Rev. Stat. 38-129(2): 'A credential may only be issued to (a) a citizen of the United States, (b) an alien lawfully admitted into the United States who is eligible for a credential under the Uniform Credentialing Act, (c) a nonimmigrant lawfully present in the United States who is eligible for a credential under the Uniform Credentialing Act, or (d) a person who submits (i) an unexpired employment authorization document ... Form I-766, and (ii) documentation ... [DACA-type]'. Neb. Rev. Stat. 4-108(1): no state agency shall provide public benefits (which include professional licenses) 'to a person not lawfully present in the United States'; 4-111(1) requires an attestation of citizenship/lawful presence; 4-111(3)(d) requires SAVE verification for EAD-based applicants, and 4-111(2) limits an EAD-based license to the EAD validity period. Per E.M. v. Nebraska DHHS, 306 Neb. 1 (2020), 'lawfully present' means the classifications in 8 U.S.C. 1621(a)(1),(2),(3) - (a)(2) is 'a nonimmigrant under the INA', so a B-1/B-2 visitor is in principle 'lawfully present'. The DHHS dentist application form contains a 'Lawful Presence in the United States Attestation' (for 38-129) with three boxes - US citizen / alien lawfully admitted / 'I am a non-immigrant lawfully present in the United States who is eligible for a credential under the Uniform Credentialing Act' - and states: 'If you are a qualified alien ... OR a non-immigrant lawfully present in the United States, you must submit evidence of lawful presence which may include a copy of: 1. Green Card (I-551) ...; 2. unexpired foreign passport with Temporary I-551 stamp; 3. A document showing an Alien Registration Number (A#), an Employment Authorization Card/Document is NOT acceptable; or 4. A Form I-94 (Arrival-Departure Record). Your credential will NOT be issued until such proof is received by our office and your documents are verified by our office through the Department of Homeland Security. This process may take four to six weeks.' Separately, the identification block offers a box 'I am a non-immigrant whose visa for entry, or application for visa for entry, is related to such employment in the United States' (the federal SSN-exemption wording), which suggests the form was designed around work-visa holders. Net: no citizenship/PR/EAD is required on the face of the statute for a 'nonimmigrant lawfully present', and a visitor's I-94 is a listed proof document, but (i) DHHS verifies the document through DHS/SAVE, (ii) a B-2 I-94 is short-lived and the applicant must physically have entered the US to hold one, (iii) whether DHHS treats a B visitor as 'eligible for a credential' and whether license validity/renewal is tied to the I-94 is undocumented and discretionary. No in-state address or residency is required for the examination route (Nebraska residency documentation applies only to reciprocity licenses under 38-129.02 / 172 NAC 56-003.04).

## Yüz yüze adımlar
(1) Fingerprint-based criminal background check ($55): the DHHS dentist page directs applicants to 'Nebraska State Patrol fingerprinting locations' and other public LiveScan locations in Nebraska; whether ink fingerprint cards can be taken abroad and mailed was not verified (unclear). (2) Jurisprudence examination: required of all applicants; DHHS's resident-dentist application links to an online (ProProfs-hosted) Nebraska jurisprudence quiz, so it appears to be an online open-book test, but this was not verified for the full license application. (3) Lawful-presence proof: a copy of Form I-94 (or green card/A#) must be submitted and is verified by DHHS through DHS (4-6 weeks); an I-94 exists only after a physical entry to the US, so the candidate must have entered the US on their visa before/while applying. (4) No interview; forms are signed attestations (no notarisation seen on the DHHS forms). (5) The clinical exam itself (CRDTS/CDCA/ADEX etc.) is hands-on and must be sat in person at a US test site. Overall, a short US visit could cover fingerprinting, the clinical exam and obtaining an I-94; the rest can be done by mail/online.

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
Age 19+ and good character (38-129(1)); DDS/DMD from an accredited (CODA) program; INBDE (or NBDE I+II) score 75+ or equivalent (172 NAC 56-003.01(A)); Board-approved manikin/patient clinical exam passed within past 5 years, 75+ on each part; after two failures a Board-approved remedial course (min 15 h) is required (172 NAC 56-005); jurisprudence examination (score 75+) based on Nebraska statutes/rules; fingerprint criminal background check ($55); Lawful Presence attestation with supporting document verified via DHS; SSN or A#/I-94 number; disclosure of convictions/discipline; DEA registration only if prescribing controlled substances. No CPR/BLS, English test, or liability insurance requirement found for the initial dental license. Timeline: lawful-presence verification alone 4-6 weeks; acceptable-exam list refreshed each 1 November; regional exam scores valid 5 years.

## İlk ücretler
Criminal background check $55 (DHHS dentist page). Initial dentist license application fee not confirmed on the pages read; DHHS's dental faculty application shows $165 (including LAP fee) and the reinstatement form shows the biennial renewal fee as $165, so the initial fee is most likely in the $165 range (unverified). ## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Biennial: licenses expire/renew March 1 of odd-numbered years (DHHS dentist page; 172 NAC 56-007).
- **renewal_fee:** $165 renewal fee (stated on the DHHS Dentist Reinstatement Application: '$165.00 renewal fee and $35.00 reinstatement fee'); initial/renewal fee schedule page not separately read.
- **ce_hours:** 30 hours of acceptable continuing education in the preceding 24-month period, including a minimum of 2 hours infection control (172 NAC 56-007, 007.04(E)); extra opioid/PDMP CE only if prescribing controlled substances; 6 h sedation CE and 8 h hands-on ACLS only for sedation-permit holders.
- **ce_online_ok:** Largely yes. 172 NAC 56-007.04(A): 'A maximum of 10 hours each in a renewal period may be obtained through home study' (home study must have a post-test), BUT 007.01(F) states 'Interactive webinars which include the ability of the participant to interact with the presenter are not considered home study', so live interactive webinars are uncapped. Thus all 30 hours can be earned from abroad: at least 20 h via live interactive webinars plus up to 10 h home study/self-study. No in-person component is mandated. CE audits are random, documentation kept 4 years.
- **cpr_required:** No. CPR/BLS is not a renewal requirement for a general dental license; CPR certification/recertification is merely an acceptable CE topic (capped at 10 h initial / 4 h recert). Hands-on ACLS (8 h AHA course) is required only for moderate/general sedation permit holders.
- **practice_hours_required:** None. No minimum practice or currency hours are required to renew or to keep an active license (172 NAC 56-007 and the DHHS renewal page impose only CE and fee). Reactivation form asks whether you have practiced in Nebraska but imposes no practice-hour minimum.
- **address_or_residency_required:** None for the examination-route license; no Nebraska address or US residency is required to renew. (Nebraska residency documentation applies only to licenses issued by reciprocity, 172 NAC 56-003.04.)
- **insurance_required:** No mandatory professional liability insurance requirement found in 38-11xx or 172 NAC 56.
- **inactive_status:** Available: DHHS dentist page states 'License may be placed on Inactive Status at any time.' 172 NAC 10 (Uniform Credentialing Act regs, 10-007 per search summary, not read directly) provides that a credential that has expired or been placed on inactive status may be reinstated at any time. Inactive-status fee and CE obligations (Neb. Rev. Stat. 38-146/38-147, 172 NAC 10) were not verified; no maximum duration was found.
- **reactivation:** Reinstatement from inactive, expired or lapsed status uses the DHHS 'Application for Reinstatement of a License to Practice' (Revoked, Expired, Placed on Inactive Status, or Lapsed): fee $165 renewal + $35 reinstatement; 'You must have earned 30 hours of continuing competency within the previous two-year period immediately preceding the date of this application' (waivers for military, initial licensure within 24 months, or circumstances beyond control); new conviction/discipline disclosures; SSN/A#/I-94; new Lawful Presence attestation with proof document verified through DHS (4-6 weeks). The form does not require a new clinical exam or refresher course regardless of how long the license was inactive, though 38-1117(1)(e) lets the board demand 'continuing clinical competency as a condition of licensure if required by the board'.
- **remote_maintainability:** moderate
- **details:** Keeping the license active from abroad needs only the biennial $165 fee and 30 CE hours (achievable entirely online via live interactive webinars + up to 10 h home study), with no CPR, no practice hours, no in-state address, no insurance. Inactive status can be taken at any time and reinstated at any time with 30 CE hours and $200. The awkward element is immigration: reinstatement (and possibly renewal) triggers a new Lawful Presence attestation with a copy of an I-94/green card verified via DHS, which a visitor only holds after entering the US and which expires with each visit; whether DHHS re-verifies lawful presence at ordinary biennial online renewal was not confirmed. Rated moderate rather than easy because of that verification uncertainty.
