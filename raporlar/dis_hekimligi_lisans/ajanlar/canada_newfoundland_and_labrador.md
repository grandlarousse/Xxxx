# Newfoundland and Labrador (Canada) — ajan bulgusu

Düzenleyici: Newfoundland and Labrador Dental Board (NLDB) — https://www.nldb.ca/

**Teori-only rota:** yes · **Göçmenlik engeli:** high · **Uzaktan sürdürme:** hard · **Güven:** medium

## Sınav yolları

- **General licence via NDEB Certificate (graduate of accredited programme)** (el becerisi: hayır): Dental Regulations (CNLR 1103/96, as amended to 68/16) s.2(1)(i)(i): applicant must 'provide proof that is satisfactory to the board (i) that he or she holds a certificate issued by the National Dental Examining Board of Canada'. For a graduate of a CODA/CDAC-accredited programme the NDEB Certificate is obtained via the computer-based Virtual OSCE only (no hands-on). NLDB page 'Requirements for Licensure - Dentists' lists: notarized application form, copy of diploma, copy of NDEB certificate, proof of 500 practice hours in preceding 3 years OR otherwise satisfy the board of ability to practise (new graduates: see 'New Graduates will only pay the Registration Fee of $250.00 for initial licensure', implying new grads are accepted), in-person CPR Level C-AED, two character reference letters, proof of Canadian citizenship or PR, $3M malpractice insurance, NLDA membership. No provincial jurisprudence exam, no board clinical exam, no regional hands-on exam is listed. The only hands-on element is in NDEB's Equivalency Process for NON-accredited graduates, which does not apply here.
- **Provisional licence (foreign-degree, government salaried position)** (el becerisi: hayır): Dental Regulations s.4 / Dental Act s.19: for holders of a dental degree from outside Canada 'recognized and approved by the board'; requires 'Canadian citizenship or an employment visa under the Immigration Act (Canada)' AND 'a salaried position with the government of the province or an agency of the government'; max one year, non-renewable, restricted locality and scope. Not available to a visitor without a work visa or a government job; listed for completeness only.
- **Licensure by credentials / mutual recognition (licensed elsewhere in Canada)** (el becerisi: hayır): Applicants licensed in another Canadian or international jurisdiction must have a Certificate of Standing sent directly to the NLDB (valid 3 months). The NDEB certificate is still the stated qualification (s.2(1)(i)); legacy alternatives (general/provisional licence before 31 Dec 2002, MRA unrestricted licensure before 1 July 2001) are not available to a new graduate. Citizenship/PR requirement still applies.

## Teori-only açıklaması
Exam-wise, yes: the only examination credential NLDB requires is the NDEB Certificate. Dental Regulations s.2(1)(i): 'provide proof that is satisfactory to the board (i) that he or she holds a certificate issued by the National Dental Examining Board of Canada'. The NLDB requirements page lists only 'Copy of the National Dental Examining Board of Canada Certificate' with no provincial written/jurisprudence exam, no board clinical exam and no hands-on regional exam. For a graduate of a CODA/CDAC-accredited programme the NDEB Certificate is earned by the computer-based Virtual OSCE alone. HOWEVER the licence is unobtainable for this candidate on immigration grounds: s.2(1)(d) requires the applicant to 'provide proof of Canadian citizenship or permanent resident status in Canada', restated on the board page as 'Proof of Canadian Citizenship or Permanent Resident Status'. Also note s.2(1)(j)/(2): applicants without 500 practice hours in the last 3 years must 'otherwise satisfy the board of his or her ability to engage in the practice of dentistry' and 'the board may require the applicant to pass examinations and complete a refresher program' (discretionary; the fee note for new graduates suggests fresh graduates are accepted without extra exams, but this is not stated explicitly).

## DLOSCE notları
N/A for Canada. NLDB makes no reference to DLOSCE/ADEX; the sole accepted credential is the NDEB Certificate (for accredited graduates: NDEB Virtual OSCE, computer-based). No US regional exam is recognized or needed.

- **barrier_level:** high
- **details:** Dental Regulations (CNLR 1103/96) s.2(1): 'An applicant for a licence to be issued under section 18 of the Act shall ... (d) provide proof of Canadian citizenship or permanent resident status in Canada'. The NLDB 'Requirements for Licensure - Dentists' page repeats: 'Proof of Canadian Citizenship or Permanent Resident Status.' On the plain text, even a work-permit holder does not qualify for the general licence (only the government-salaried provisional licence under s.4(1)(f) accepts 'Canadian citizenship or an employment visa under the Immigration Act (Canada)'). A Turkish citizen with no Canadian status therefore cannot be registered in any category; the regulation's non-practising status (s.3.1) is only available to 'a person who holds a licence issued under section 18', i.e. someone who already met the citizenship/PR requirement. No in-province address or residency requirement was found; s.13 only requires notice of change of practice address within 10 days. The regulation dates from 1996 (last amended 2016) and was made under the former Dental Act, but the board's current web page restates the same citizenship/PR requirement, so it is current practice.

## Yüz yüze adımlar
(1) Application must be NOTARIZED 'by a Commissioner of Oaths, Justice of the Peace, Notary Public or a Lawyer' and 'MUST BE FORWARDED BY MAIL ONLY. WE DO NOT ACCEPT APPLICATIONS SENT BY EMAIL' - notarization can presumably be done abroad before a notary public/lawyer but acceptance of a foreign notary is not stated. (2) CPR Level C - AED (or higher), valid two years: 'Only in-person training is accepted. On-line training is not accepted' - hands-on, but could be completed in any country in principle (board acceptance of a non-Canadian provider not stated). (3) No fingerprinting, criminal record check, jurisprudence exam or interview is listed on the requirements page. (4) Two signed/dated character reference letters (current within 1 year). (5) Certificates of Standing from other regulators may be emailed. (6) Membership in the NL Dental Association required within a one-month grace period. (7) NDEB Virtual OSCE is sat at a Prometric centre (can be done on a visitor visa). Overall, apart from in-person CPR and notarization, no step requires physical presence in the province - but the citizenship/PR requirement makes the process unavailable to this candidate.

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
No jurisprudence/ethics exam listed. English: s.2(1)(c) 'demonstrate reasonable fluency in the English language' (no specific test named). CPR Level C-AED in person, valid 2 years. Professional malpractice insurance: s.2(1)(h); board currently requires $3 million. Two character references. Proof of no misconduct proceedings (s.2(1)(e)). Proof of 500 hours of practice in the preceding 3 years or otherwise satisfy the board (board may require exams/refresher, s.2(2)). Compliance with mandatory CE guidelines (s.2(1)(g)). Membership in the Newfoundland and Labrador Dental Association (one-month grace period). Timeline: applications are reviewed only when complete; no published processing time.

## İlk ücretler
Registration fee CAD $250 plus licensing fee CAD $1,300 (full year, prorated by quarter); 'New Graduates will only pay the Registration Fee of $250.00 for initial licensure.' Plus NDEB fees (separate), $3M malpractice insurance premium, NLDA membership dues (see www.nlda.net), notarization and CPR course costs.

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Annual. Dental Regulations s.10: 'Annual licence fees are due by November 30 in any year'; surcharge after that date, daily penalty after December 31, and s.11: licence cancelled once accumulated daily penalties reach $200. CE runs on a 3-year cycle (2025-2028 Mandatory Continuing Education Guidelines).
- **renewal_fee:** Full licensing fee currently CAD $1,300/year (prorated by quarter for new registrants); surcharge/penalties if late (amounts set by the board, not published on the pages read). NLDA membership dues additional.
- **ce_hours:** Dentists: 60 credit hours per three-year cycle, minimum 50 credits Category I, maximum 10 credits Category II (2025-2028 Mandatory Continuing Education Guidelines). Dentists offering facial esthetics need 6 extra hours in that field per cycle.
- **ce_online_ok:** Largely yes. Category I-A credits include 'live/synchronous webinars' at one credit per hour (no cap seen) and 'online asynchronous lectures ... with proof of completion of an examination - maximum 30 credits per cycle for dentists'; Category I-B 'online asynchronous lectures ... without examination' max 20 credits per cycle; Category II max 10. In principle all 60 credits could be earned remotely by combining live webinars (uncapped) with asynchronous courses; no mandatory in-person CE component was found. Hands-on courses earn 2 credits/hour but are optional.
- **cpr_required:** CPR Level C-AED (or higher) required for licensure, valid two years from issuance, 'Only in-person training is accepted. On-line training is not accepted.' Whether it must be kept current at every renewal is not stated on the pages read but is strongly implied by the 2-year validity.
- **practice_hours_required:** For initial licensure and (by the regulation's structure) for reinstatement from non-practising status: 'a minimum of 500 hours in the 3 years preceding his or her application or otherwise satisfy the board of his or her ability to engage in the practice of dentistry' (s.2(1)(j)); board may require exams and a refresher program (s.2(2)). No annual practice-hour minimum found for simple renewal of an active licence.
- **address_or_residency_required:** No in-province residency or address requirement found; s.13 requires notice of change of practice address within 10 days. But Canadian citizenship or PR is required for the licence itself.
- **insurance_required:** Yes - s.2(1)(h) professional malpractice insurance at or above the board minimum; currently $3 million. Non-practising status also 'is required to satisfy the requirements set by the board for continuing education and malpractice insurance' (s.3.1(2)).
- **inactive_status:** Non-practising status (s.3.1): a licence holder 'who does not wish to practise dentistry in the province for a period of time may apply to the registrar for non-practising status and may receive a refund of fees as approved by the board'; must still satisfy board requirements for CE and malpractice insurance; no maximum duration stated; fee not published. Consulting status (s.3.2) and education status (s.3.3) also exist. Only available to someone who already holds a s.18 licence (i.e. met citizenship/PR requirement).
- **reactivation:** s.3.1(3): a non-practising person 'may apply to the secretary to be reinstated to active practice status and on complying with rules for reinstatement set by the board, shall be reinstated'. Rules not published on the pages read; expect the 500-hours-in-3-years test or, failing that, board-required examinations and/or a refresher program (s.2(1)(j), s.2(2)). After cancellation for non-payment, 're-licensing shall be subject to the payment of all overdue fees and penalties' (s.11(2)).
- **remote_maintainability:** hard
- **details:** Even setting aside the citizenship/PR bar, keeping an NL licence active from abroad requires annual fees (~$1,300), NLDA membership, $3M malpractice coverage (Canadian insurers generally tie coverage to Canadian practice), in-person CPR every two years, and 60 CE credits per 3-year cycle (achievable online). Non-practising status keeps CE and insurance obligations and reinstatement may trigger the 500-hour currency test or board exams/refresher. The decisive factor is that the licence cannot be issued at all without Canadian citizenship or permanent residence.
