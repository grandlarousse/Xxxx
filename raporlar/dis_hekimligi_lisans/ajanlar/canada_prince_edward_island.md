# Prince Edward Island (Canada) — ajan bulgusu

Düzenleyici: Prince Edward Island Dental College (PEIDC; formerly the Dental Council of Prince Edward Island). 184 Belvedere Avenue, Charlottetown PEI C1A 2Z1; tel 902-628-8156. Governing law: Regulated Health Professions Act (2023), Dentists Regulations (PEI Reg EC109/23, college labels as 2024) and PEIDC Bylaws (v20260218). — https://www.dcpei.ca/ (also mirrored at https://www.peidc.ca/)

## Sınav yolları

- **General registration – first dental licence in Canada (NDEB certificate route)** (el becerisi: hayır): PEIDC Policy 001 'First Application for a Dental Licence in Canada—General Dentist' (established 25 Sep 2024, next review Sep 2026). Required: (a) certified copy of graduation certificate from a dental programme accredited by CDAC, CODA (no 'located in US/Canada' restriction stated), ADC, DCNZ or Irish Dental Council; (b) dean's letter certifying graduation; (c) copy of NDEB general dentistry certificate (for accredited-programme graduates = the computer-based Virtual OSCE only, no hands-on component); (d) if graduated >3 years ago, employer letter/affidavit of minimum 450 practice hours in the preceding 3 years; plus CPR/BLS from an in-person hands-on course, $5M professional liability insurance, criminal record check with vulnerable sector search (dated within 12 months), two character references, proof of identity/immigration status, legal statements, fees. No regional/ADEX exam, no DLOSCE, no provincial clinical exam and NO jurisprudence/ethics exam is mentioned anywhere in the policy, fee schedule or website. The only hands-on element is the CPR/BLS course, which is not a clinical competency exam. Application is entirely online through the HMS registration system; registrar 'if necessary' may schedule an appointment to discuss the application. Application profile is deleted if not completed within 3 months.
- **Labour mobility transfer (already registered in another Canadian jurisdiction)** (el becerisi: hayır): For dentists registered with a regulatory body in another Canadian province/territory (PEIDC Policy 002). Not available to a new graduate with no Canadian registration. Hands-on requirement depends on what the originating province required.
- **Special registration (limited purpose, 30 days)** (el becerisi: hayır): For dentists registered in another Canadian jurisdiction who want to practise in PEI for a special purpose and limited time (30 days). Application fee $80 + registration fee $300. Not available without an existing Canadian registration.
- **PGY-1 residency in lieu of exam** (el becerisi: hayır): Not a pathway in PEI; residency is neither required nor accepted as a substitute for the NDEB certificate.
- **ADEX / regional hands-on exams / DLOSCE** (el becerisi: evet): Not recognised or used in PEI. The only competency credential accepted is the NDEB general dentistry certificate.

## Teori-only açıklaması
PEIDC Policy 001 s.3.2.1 requires only: 'a. A certified copy of your graduation certificate from a dental program accredited by one of these general dentistry accreditation bodies: Commission on Dental Accreditation of Canada (CDAC); American Dental Association Commission on Dental Accreditation (CODA); Australian Dental Council (ADC)...; Dental Council of New Zealand (DCNZ)...; or Irish Dental Council...; b. A letter from the dean certifying your graduation from the dental program; c. A copy of your National Dental Examining Board of Canada (NDEB) general dentistry certificate; d. If you graduated more than three years ago, a letter from employer(s) or a signed affidavit verifying continual practice of minimum of 450 hours within the last three years preceding the date of application.' For graduates of accredited programmes the NDEB certificate is obtained via the computer-based Virtual OSCE alone (known fact). No regional/ADEX/DLOSCE exam, no residency and no jurisprudence exam appears in the policy, fee schedule or regulations as summarised. The only hands-on element is s.3.2.2: 'All general dentists practising in PEI must hold a CPR/BLS certification from an in-person hands-on course by a provider recognized by the Council' – a first-aid course, not a clinical exam. Caveat: the examination/competency route is theory-only, but the licence is in practice blocked for this candidate by the immigration requirement in s.3.1.2 (see immigration section). The statutory text of the Dentists Regulations (EC109/23) could not be read (government PDF returned HTTP 403), so the regulation wording itself is unverified.

## DLOSCE notları
Not applicable. PEI does not recognise the DLOSCE, ADEX or any US regional exam; the sole competency credential is the NDEB general dentistry certificate (Virtual OSCE for accredited-programme graduates). Whether DLOSCE was taken standalone or within ADEX is irrelevant to PEI registration.

## Yüz yüze adımlar
(1) No fingerprinting step is specified; a 'Vulnerable Sector Search and a Criminal Record Check' dated within 12 months is required (Policy 001 s.3.4.1) – in Canada a vulnerable-sector check is issued by the police service of the applicant's place of residence and often requires in-person attendance and sometimes fingerprints; how an applicant living abroad satisfies this (foreign police certificate?) is not stated – contact the registrar. (2) No jurisprudence/ethics exam exists in PEI; nothing to sit in person. (3) No interview is routine, but s.7.1: 'If necessary, the registrar will contact you to schedule an appointment and discuss your application' (format unspecified). (4) Notarised/certified copies of passport/citizenship/PR documents, graduation certificate, and IRCC authorization are required (see college 'Document certification process' page); certification can be done by a notary abroad per that process (not read in full). (5) CPR/BLS must come from an 'in-person hands-on course' (Policy 001 s.3.2.2) – can be taken anywhere from a provider recognised by the Council (college CPR page lists Canadian providers e.g. Life Start/CPR-AED HCP; acceptance of foreign in-person courses unclear). (6) Application itself is fully online via the HMS portal and documents are uploaded electronically; the NDEB Virtual OSCE is sat at Prometric centres. In principle everything except the hands-on CPR course and the VSS check could be done from abroad or during a short visit – but the IRCC work authorization requirement is the blocker.

## Diğer başlangıç şartları
No jurisprudence exam. No English-language test mentioned (translations of documents may be requested, s.3.1.2). CPR/BLS: in-person hands-on course by a Council-recognised provider, current certificate uploaded. Professional liability insurance: mandatory, $5 million per claim/occurrence and $5 million aggregate (s.3.3). Criminal Record Check with Vulnerable Sector Search dated within 12 months (s.3.4.1). Two signed, dated character references from non-family members who have known the applicant 4 years, written within 12 months (s.3.4.2). Signed legal statements in HMS. Currency: if graduated >3 years ago, proof of 450 practice hours in preceding 3 years; general currency rule – within 3 years of application must have completed dental education, actively practised to an extent acceptable to Council, or completed an approved refresher. Timeline: HMS profile deleted if application not completed within 3 months of starting; registration cycle 1 April–31 March, fee prorated after 30 Sep (policy) / for applications 1 Sep–31 Mar (fee schedule). Registrar reviews and may request more information, extending processing time.

## İlk ücretler
PEIDC: application fee $100 (non-refundable); registration fee $2,200 full year (applications 1 Apr–31 Aug) or $1,100 prorated (applications 1 Sep–31 Mar); verification/letter of good standing $125; special registration $80 application + $300 registration (30 days). Plus external costs: NDEB application/Virtual OSCE fees, CPR course, criminal record/VSS check, notarisation, $5M liability insurance premium. All CAD, from https://www.dcpei.ca/fee-schedule (2026).

## Lisansı sürdürme

- **renewal_period:** Annual. Registration cycle 1 April–31 March. Renewal opens 15 February; all mandatory information, documents and fee due 15 March; late fee 16–31 March; from 1 April renewal is no longer available and the reinstatement process applies.
- **renewal_fee:** $2,200 annual licence renewal fee (general dentists and specialists); late fee applies 16–31 March (amount not shown); reinstatement fee $600 plus the registration fee if lapsed.
- **ce_hours:** Continuing Competence Program (CCP): minimum 90 CCP credits per three-year cycle (current cycle 1 April 2025–31 March 2028), logged in the HMS profile; must include a course on Management of Medical Emergencies in the Dental Office at least once per cycle; credits fall into three categories each with minimum and maximum requirements (PEIDC CCP Guidelines, not read).
- **ce_online_ok:** Unclear. The college page does not state a live/in-person requirement for CCP credits, but the three-category min/max structure (guidelines PDF not read) may limit self-study credits; the Medical Emergencies course and the separately required CPR/BLS are typically hands-on. Treat 100%-online CE as unverified.
- **cpr_required:** Yes. A current CPR/BLS certificate must be in the HMS profile at every renewal; Policy 001 s.3.2.2 requires 'a CPR/BLS certification from an in-person hands-on course by a provider recognized by the Council'. Hands-on skills session therefore required (renewal interval per certificate validity, typically annual/biennial).
- **practice_hours_required:** Yes (currency rule). 'All dentists applying for or renewing their general registration with the PEI Dental College must demonstrate currency of professional knowledge and skills. Applicants must have completed one of the following within three (3) years preceding application or renewal: successfully completed the education requirements; actively practised general or specialty dentistry in PEI or another jurisdiction recognized by the Council to an extent that is acceptable to the Council; or completed a refresher program approved by the College Council.' Professional hours must be recorded in the HMS profile each cycle. For initial applicants graduating >3 years ago, 450 hours in the preceding 3 years is the benchmark.
- **address_or_residency_required:** No PEI address or residency requirement is stated for renewal; registrants must keep their HMS professional profile current. (Statutory text not verified.)
- **insurance_required:** Yes – valid proof of professional liability insurance ($5 million per claim and $5 million aggregate) must be in the profile at each renewal.
- **inactive_status:** None found. The fee schedule lists only general registration, special (30-day) registration and reinstatement; no non-practising, inactive or retired class appears on the website, fee schedule or Policy 001. Only options are to keep paying the full $2,200 active renewal (and meet CRC/VSS, CPR, insurance, CCP and currency requirements) or let the registration lapse.
- **reactivation:** Lapsed registration: 'you may apply for reinstatement for up to three years from the date of your registration lapse. You will need to pay the reinstatement fee [$600] and the registration fee [$2,200 / $1,100 prorated] ... and meet all the renewal requirements listed above' (CCP, currency, insurance, CRC/VSS, CPR, legal statements). After three years lapsed the website is silent – presumably a new application under Policy 001, which for anyone graduated >3 years ago requires proof of 450 practice hours in the preceding 3 years or a Council-approved refresher program; no re-sit of a clinical exam is mentioned. Anyone not practising for 3+ years would need the refresher program route (programme not specified).
- **remote_maintainability:** hard
- **details:** Keeping an active PEI licence from abroad is impractical: (i) the licence itself requires IRCC work authorization for non-citizens/non-PRs, (ii) annual renewal requires a fresh Canadian-style criminal record check with vulnerable sector screening, in-person hands-on CPR/BLS, $5M Canadian liability insurance and the $2,200 fee, (iii) the currency rule requires actual practice (or an approved refresher) within every rolling 3-year window, and (iv) there is no inactive/non-practising category and reinstatement is possible only within 3 years of lapse. Sources: https://www.dcpei.ca/renewal, https://www.dcpei.ca/continuing-education, https://www.dcpei.ca/currency-requirements, https://www.dcpei.ca/fee-schedule, Policy 001.
